# Do Synthetic Queries Preserve Insurance Eligibility Decisions?

**Author**: Shunchi Wang (Independent Researcher, `jakelihua@gmail.com`, ORCID: <https://orcid.org/0009-0009-8629-2078>)  
**Repository**: <https://github.com/jakelihua/chinese-insurance-rag-benchmark>

## Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) benchmarks often synthesize test queries from source passages using instruction-tuned language models. In rule-governed insurance workflows, however, a rewrite can rank the source clause at Top-1 while omitting or altering conditions that determine whether a claim is covered, excluded, or requires clarification. Across 26 official Chinese insurance policy wordings and 8 multi-threshold rule families (88 seeds across 25 clauses, N = 176 cross-model pairs) evaluated with Qwen and DeepSeek models without fine-tuning, we compile eligibility rules into a reachability verifier that separates slot completeness from decision determinacy and routes out-of-grammar phrasing via a text-only uncertainty gate. Across all 176 pairs, parser-guided slot repair reaches 98.86% (174/176) joint usability—including 97.5% (78/80) on confirmation seeds, 100.0% (48/48) on waiting-period holdouts, and 100.0% (48/48) on non-waiting-period insurance rules—outperforming all six LLM baselines under 25-clause-clustered permutation tests after Holm-Bonferroni correction (all p <= 0.00293). Auditing against a blinded three-flagship raw-clause panel (Fleiss' kappa = 0.7411, 87.19% verifier agreement; not human annotation) confirms 94.32% (166/176) panel-verified usability and 11/11 zero-shot schema extraction accuracy. Across six cloud LLM RAG pipelines, verified repair reduces evaluation MAE from 0.60–0.75 to 0.02–0.07 and prevents ranking reversal.

## 1. Introduction

Evaluating a vertical-domain retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system often begins before real user traffic is available. A standard cold-start practice in retrieval research (Dai et al., 2023; Jeronymo et al., 2023) and automated RAG evaluation (Saad-Falcon et al., 2024; Es et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Filice et al., 2025) is to prompt an instruction-tuned language model to turn domain passages into synthetic user questions, filter the outputs for surface fluency, and use the resulting benchmark to rank retrievers or score end-to-end answers.

Prior work has shown that synthetic queries can shift system rankings relative to real user queries (Rahmani et al., 2024; van Elburg et al., 2025; Kucia & Gawlik, 2026). In rule-governed settings such as insurance underwriting and claims review, a more direct problem arises at the level of individual test cases: retrieval metrics reward lexical and topical similarity, whereas coverage decisions depend on discrete eligibility conditions. Under Chinese commercial health insurance clauses, whether a claim is excluded by a waiting period depends on the exact product and benefit claimed, whether the policy is a first-time purchase or a continuous renewal, how many days have elapsed since the policy took effect, whether hospitalization resulted from illness or accidental injury, and whether the insurer approved a waiting-period waiver.

When a language model rewrites a structured eligibility scenario into a natural-sounding user question, two distinct failure modes occur even when the rewrite ranks the source clause at Top-1:

1. **Ambiguity Degradation (Clarification Required)**: To make a question sound conversational and brief, models frequently drop product titles, benefit types, enrollment status, or elapsed days. Omitting a *pivotal* slot turns a decidable test case into an underspecified inquiry under which both coverage and exclusion remain possible. Conversely, omitting a *non-pivotal* slot—for example, dropping whether a claim arose from illness or accident after the waiting period has already expired—leaves the coverage outcome unchanged. Simple slot-matching rules conflate pivotal and non-pivotal omissions, while retrieval scores ignore both.
2. **Condition Mutation and Decision Flip**: Smaller models and open-loop prompts often alter numeric durations (for instance, turning "Day 20" into "Day 2") or overwrite the policyholder's actual elapsed days (7, 45, or 120 days) with the statutory waiting-period threshold stated in the clause (14 or 90 days). Such edits increase lexical overlap with the target document while reversing the ground-truth eligibility decision.

This paper asks a focused diagnostic question: *when synthetic queries are generated from rule-bearing clauses, do they preserve the underlying eligibility decision, and can lightweight symbolic verification detect and repair decision drift?* Rather than proposing a new model architecture or claiming automated rule extraction from arbitrary legal prose, we formalize hand-compiled waiting-period rules over traceable policy clauses and separate surface slot completeness from reference-independent decision determinacy. Specifically, our verifier enumerates all admissible completions of any omitted fields over threshold-partitioned equivalence regions without consulting reference seed metadata, classifying each synthetic rewrite as preserving the unique reference decision, mutating a condition (or flipping the unique decision), or degrading into an ambiguous query certified by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses.

Our empirical analysis covers five connected settings, with supplementary community-QA retrieval diagnostics in Appendix A:

- **Development Policy Evaluation Across Model Scales (Section 3)**: On five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (20 seed scenarios, 80 controlled cases, and 120 paired rewrites across local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and cloud Qwen-Plus and Qwen-Max), we measure how unconstrained conversational rewriting versus explicit slot prompting affects slot retention and decision determinacy. Conversational rewrites preserve standalone decisions on only 30%, 0%, and 45% of seeds across the three model scales, whereas explicit condition constraints reach 95%–100%.
- **Extension to Additional Official Policies and Benefit-Specific Waiting Periods (Section 4)**: We extend the development-set parser and reachability verifier to four additional official insurance policy PDFs (16 seeds, 64 controlled cases) after adding colloquial product nicknames and benefit-level disambiguation (`benefit_id`) for contracts whose waiting periods differ by benefit (e.g., general inpatient allowance with a 30-day wait and accident exemption versus cancer inpatient allowance with a 90-day wait), stripping book-title spoilers (`《》`), and binding contract rules to PDF page and bounding-box rubrics (separately from synthetic seed scenario parameters). In this post-refinement evaluation, unconstrained conversational rewrites preserve 0/16 standalone decisions (5/16 when the policy identity is given), while single-pass constrained prompting already preserves 16/16 (so second-pass repair triggers 0 calls and provides no additional gain on single-clause insurance policies). Title-masked retrieval across all nine clauses further shows that standard retrieval scores rely heavily on product-title strings rather than eligibility conditions.
- **Equal-Budget Repair on Multi-Threshold Rules (Section 5)**: On a 16-seed (64-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark where all non-JSON rewrites reach 100% Top-1 retrieval, we compare symbolic parser-guided repair against an equal-budget unguided self-refinement baseline (26 LLM calls). Symbolic feedback raises standalone decision preservation from 8/16 (50.0%) to 12/16 (75.0%, exact two-sided McNemar $p = 0.125$) and reduces parser-detected condition mutations from 4/16 (25.0%) to 0/16 (0.0%); in a post-hoc sensitivity decomposition where the source clause identity is granted at evaluation time, conversational rewrites rise from 1/16 to 7/16 and parser-guided repair reaches 15/16 versus 8/16 for self-refinement (exact McNemar $p = 0.0156$).
- **Dual-Implementation Consistency Check, Cross-Model Confirmation, and Rule-Based Pipeline Selection (Section 6)**: We evaluate 11 zero-alias confirmation insurance policy PDFs (40 seeds, 160 controlled cases) and 6 post-hardening extension policy PDFs (24 seeds, 48 cross-model pairs) across `Qwen-Plus` (`response_model="qwen-plus"`) and `DeepSeek` (`deepseek-chat` API endpoint, `response_model="deepseek-flash"`). A text-only gate separates parser grammar boundaries (`PARSE_UNCERTAIN`) from question ambiguity (`CLARIFY_REQUIRED`). Across a 120-case diagnostic suite and a 400-query shared-rule-base consistency check, the verifier records a `0.0%` semantic false-accept rate, while 6 format-truncation false accepts occur in 160-token `structured_json_rewrite` (`0/80` on `slot_guard_repair`). On the confirmation set, `slot_guard_repair` reaches `97.5%` (`78/80`) joint usability, outperforming single-pass prompting and equal-budget self-refinement (both `87.5%`, `70/80`) and showing a positive trend over 320-token JSON rewriting (`91.25%`, `73/80`, $p = 0.0625$). In downstream evaluation using a reference-free inference interface (`predict(question, retrieved_clause, inference_config)`), unverified conversational rewrites invert pipeline rankings ($\tau \in [-0.8367, -0.7746]$), whereas verified filtering and repair recover the reference ordering ($\tau = 1.0000$).
- **Blinded Three-Flagship Raw-Clause Adjudication, Real LLM RAG Selection, and Non-Waiting-Period Rule Generalization (Section 7)**: We evaluate all 88 seeds ($N=1,408$ core records plus 160 deterministic template records) with a blinded three-flagship raw-clause adjudication panel (`Kimi-K3`, `DeepSeek-V4-Pro`, and `Qwen3.8-Max` reading only query text and verbatim policy clauses, Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7411$ on confirmation and $0.6741$ across all 88 seeds; 87.19% exact agreement with the symbolic verifier on confirmation), audit zero-shot schema compilation (11/11 exact policy match), compare against deterministic multi-template and 6-slot schema renderers, evaluate six end-to-end cloud LLM RAG pipelines (1,939 reader calls), and extend to three non-waiting-period insurance rule families (24 seeds, $N=48$ pairs). Pooled across all 88 seeds ($N=176$ pairs, 25 clauses), `slot_guard_repair` achieves 174/176 (98.86%) symbolic usability and 166/176 (94.32%) raw-clause panel usability, outperforming all six LLM baselines under 25-clause-clustered permutation tests after 6-baseline Holm-Bonferroni correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} \le 0.00293$).

## 2. Related Work and Dataset Provenance

**Synthetic Queries and Retriever Ranking Shifts.** LLM-generated queries are widely used to train dense retrievers (Dai et al., 2023; Jeronymo et al., 2023) and to construct automated RAG benchmarks (Saad-Falcon et al., 2024; Es et al., 2024). Several studies have examined whether synthetic benchmarks reproduce real retriever rankings. Rahmani et al. (2024) and van Elburg et al. (2025) analyze system-ranking correlations and generator biases across synthetic test collections. Kucia and Gawlik (2026) compare 1,851 LLM-generated synthetic questions against 322 student survey questions for a university faculty information system across dense, lexical (BM25), and hybrid (RRF) retrievers, showing that synthetic queries overestimate absolute performance and shift system rankings relative to human questions. Because ranking shifts between real and synthetic queries are already established in prior work, we treat our community-QA retrieval audit (Appendix A) as a complementary diagnostic that separates document-subsampling variance from within-document lexical copying.

**Schema-Driven RAG Synthesis and Question Evaluation.** To ground synthetic queries in domain structure, RAGEval (Zhu et al., 2025) extracts domain schemas from financial, medical, and legal documents to generate evaluation sets, while DataMorgana (Filice et al., 2025) parameterizes user personas and question categories for RAG benchmarking. For general question generation, QGEval (Fu et al., 2024) benchmarks multi-dimensional quality across seven criteria: fluency, clarity, conciseness, relevance, consistency, answerability, and answer consistency. However, open-loop schema prompting and surface answerability judges do not compile clause thresholds into a formal reachability check. As our multi-threshold experiments in Section 5 show, even when a prompt explicitly lists every required slot, a 1.5B generator still overwrites user durations with clause thresholds in 25.0%–31.2% of cases unless checked by a symbolic verifier.

**Rule-Grounded Clarification, Risk Control, and Insurance Benchmarks.** In conversational QA, ShARC (Saeidi et al., 2018) studies rule-grounded answering where an assistant must ask follow-up questions when decision variables are underspecified. Recent work such as CLAMBER (Zhang et al., 2024) and AskBench (Zhao et al., 2026) benchmarks how well LLMs detect ambiguous user requests and ask clarifying questions in open-domain and clinical dialogues. From a selective prediction perspective, RC-RAG (Chen et al., 2024) uses counterfactual prompting to estimate RAG confidence and control risk via selective abstention under misleading contexts. In the insurance domain, InsuranceQA (Feng et al., 2015) provides community QA pairs for answer selection, and InsQABench (Ding et al., 2025) evaluates LLM question answering over insurance product rules. Our focus is upstream of assistant answering: rather than testing whether a downstream chatbot asks clarifying questions on underspecified prompts, we verify whether *synthetic evaluation queries* retain the conditions needed to keep their reference labels determinate during benchmark creation.

**Statistical Calibration with Small Gold Samples.** Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) (Angelopoulos et al., 2023), ARES (Saad-Falcon et al., 2024), and PRECISE (Divekar, 2026) combine synthetic data and automated LLM judges with small human-labeled calibration sets to construct valid confidence intervals for RAG evaluation metrics. When synthetic queries are generated from a small subset of corpus documents without paired real queries across the full index, unpaired shrinkage and marginal query-propensity weighting fail to correct document-subsampling and query-to-source overlap biases; we report these negative calibration results alongside a heuristic bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule in Appendix A.

### 2.1 Data Provenance, Licensing Scope, and Inference-Only Setup

1. **Inference-Only Evaluation**: No generative LLM or embedding model weights are pre-trained, instruction-tuned, or fine-tuned in this work. All local runs (`Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` and `BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5`) use frozen weights under deterministic `float32/eager` inference, and cloud API runs (`Qwen-Plus` and `Qwen-Max`) use `temperature=0`. Few-shot prompts provide three fixed calibration examples in context. The only fitted statistical parameters in our code are corpus TF-IDF/BM25 term frequencies and a 2-fold logistic regression classifier used for propensity-weighting diagnostics in Appendix A.
2. **Official Insurance Clauses versus Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rules**: The nine insurance policies (5 development policies in Section 3 and 4 extension policies in Section 4) are official PDF wordings downloaded from public disclosure portals of Ping An, CPIC, and Taikang. Because public web availability does not confer a license to redistribute full commercial contracts, our repository releases SHA256 file hashes, source URLs, PDF page and bounding-box coordinates, and short verification excerpts rather than raw PDF binaries. In contrast, the four multi-threshold rules in Section 5 are researcher-authored synthetic clauses designed for controlled stress testing.
3. **Community QA Corpus (`baoxianzhidao_filter.csv`)**: Used in Appendix A, this file (8,362 raw rows; 3,746 unique answers and 3,744 valid questions after normalization) comes from `meowcao/InsuranceModel`, which mirrors `SophonPlus/ChineseNlpCorpus` (`datasets/baoxianzhidao/intro.ipynb`). Upstream documentation states that the pairs were collected from Baidu Zhidao insurance Q&A with original post URLs removed and sensitive fields filtered. Consequently, answers reflect historical community responses rather than actuarial ground truth, no verified open redistribution license chain is documented upstream, and Chinese foundation models may have encountered parts of this text during pretraining.
4. **Proxy Parser Scope, Boundary Risks, and Inspection Watchlist**: Formally, our reachability verifier is exact *relative to the extracted observation frame* $\hat{z} = g(q')$, where $g(\cdot)$ is a synonym-aware regular-expression slot parser that also resolves explicit contrastive corrections (`不是/并非/并不是 A，而是 B`), rejects negated numeric durations (`不到/未满 N 天`), and flags multi-value day conflicts (`第20天或第45天`). Because $g(\cdot)$ is a rule-based text extractor rather than a full semantic parser, it faces two boundary risks: **false-reject errors** (`DEGRADED_TO_CLARIFY`), when a valid rewrite uses uncataloged colloquial paraphrases or omits formal rule titles while retaining domain-specific keywords, and **false-accept errors** (`PRESERVED_DECISION`), if complex multi-clause discourse or long-range negation falls outside the regex scope rules. We release blinded two-rater HTML annotation packages (`reviewer_A.html` and `reviewer_B.html`), which remain uncompleted by external human raters and are not claimed as empirical validation results in this paper, alongside a deterministic 264-item verifier diagnostic log and manual-inspection watchlist (`data_v3/item_by_item_semantic_audit.jsonl`) that records per-slot parser traces and flags 29 surface-form edge cases for inspection without claiming external semantic validation or human agreement rates.

## 3. Eligibility Decision Verification on Development Insurance Policies

### 3.1 Traceable Clauses, Completion Witnesses, and Decision Determinacy

To test whether synthetic queries preserve eligibility decisions when contract rules are explicitly specified, we first compile waiting-period rules from five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (verified by SHA256 hashes and PDF bounding-box coordinates in `data_v3/sources.json`):

| Policy ID | Wait Period | Product Title and Waiting-Period Rule Summary |
|---|---|---|
| `pa_p0781` | 90 days | *Ping An Caring Companion Group Medical Insurance (Plan B)*: 90-day wait for first-time enrollment on specified/mild diseases; accidental injury waives wait. |
| `pa_h865` | 30 days | *Ping An Employee & Dependent Inpatient Medical Insurance*: 30-day wait for first-time sickness inpatient care; accidental injury or insurer-approved waiver exempts wait. |
| `cpic_86017` | 7–25 days | *CPIC Internet Outpatient Medical Insurance (Plan C)*: plan-specific wait ($\le 30$ days, tested at 7/15/25 days); non-continuous renewal without insurer waiver recalculates wait. |
| `cpic_82859` | 30 days | *CPIC Internet Personal 1-Year Inpatient Medical Insurance (Plan E)*: 30-day wait for first-time or unapproved renewal sickness inpatient care; accident or approved waiver exempts wait. |
| `tk_group_2018` | 30 days | *Taikang Joyful Global (Premier) High-End Medical Insurance*: 30-day wait for first-time non-psychiatric sickness inpatient care; accidental injury waives wait. |

Each development scenario frame specifies up to six fields: product identity (20/20 seeds), enrollment type (first-time purchase vs. renewal, 20/20), policy effective day (20/20), claim cause (illness vs. accidental injury, 20/20), selected plan waiting period (4/20 seeds on `cpic_86017`), and insurer-approved waiting-period waiver (3/20 renewal seeds). From 20 seed scenarios, we construct 80 controlled test cases across four variants: *complete* (all required slots stated), *surface* (synonymous phrasing), *missing-critical* (one pivotal condition removed, certified as requiring clarification via two completion witnesses that yield opposite decisions), and *counterfactual* (one condition altered to flip the rule outcome between waiting-period exclusion and coverage).

Because a seed scenario can contain slots that are non-pivotal for its specific state—for example, on `pa_h865` (30-day wait), a claim occurring on Day 45 satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting whether the cause was illness or accident still leads to coverage under every completion—we evaluate two distinct criteria:

1. **All-Slot Completeness**: Every required field of the reference seed is extracted without omission or value conflict.
2. **Reference-Independent Decision Determinacy**: Using only the slots extracted from the generated question and the public policy rules (without reading the reference seed's required-field list), the verifier enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unmentioned fields. Elapsed-day domains are partitioned into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t$, $= t$, $> t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ using representative days $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$), and all schema fields—including optional waiver overrides—are checked for value conflicts. A rewrite is classified as:
   - *Preserved Decision* (`PRESERVED_DECISION`) if no observed schema field conflicts with the seed and the set of reachable decisions across all admissible completions is the singleton matching the reference outcome;
   - *Condition Mutated / Decision Flip* (`CONDITION_MUTATED` / `COUNTERFACTUAL_FLIP`) if any observed schema field conflicts with the seed or if the reachable set is a singleton with the opposite coverage decision;
   - *Degraded to Clarification* (`DEGRADED_TO_CLARIFY`) if both coverage and exclusion are reachable across completions ($|\text{reachable}| > 1$).

### 3.3 Development Policy Findings

1. **High Retrieval Accuracy Coexists with Severe Decision Loss Under Conversational Rewriting**: Under `ordinary_style`, cloud models achieve 80%–90% Top-1 clause retrieval, yet standalone decision determinacy is only 30% (6/20) on `1.5B`, 0% (0/20) on `Qwen-Plus`, and 45% (9/20) on `Qwen-Max`. Even when the target policy identity is granted by context, 45% (`1.5B`), 25% (`Qwen-Plus`), and 20% (`Qwen-Max`) of conversational rewrites remain undecidable or mutated.
2. **Numeric Mutations on `1.5B` versus Conversational Compression on Cloud Models**: On `1.5B`, 3/20 conversational rewrites (`condition-013`, `015`, `016`) corrupt "保单生效第20天" (Day 20) into "保单生效后的第二天" (Day 2), and two seeds with opposite ground-truth outcomes (`condition-009` with a 7-day wait on Day 10 vs. `condition-010` with a 25-day wait on Day 10) collapse into the exact same question after dropping the plan waiting period. On `Qwen-Plus` and `Qwen-Max`, numeric value mutations are 0/20 in this sample (a finite-sample observation on $n=20$ seeds rather than proof of a zero population rate), but both cloud models omit administrative qualifiers to sound natural: `Qwen-Plus` drops the policy title in 20/20 seeds, and `Qwen-Max` drops the policy title in 10/20 seeds and replaces "首次投保" (first-time purchase) with "我刚买了…" ("I just bought...", which does not distinguish first purchase from renewal) in 6/20 seeds. When `Qwen-Max` keeps the product title (10/20 seeds), 5 of those seeds omit enrollment status on non-waivable clauses or post-waiting-period days where the coverage decision is still invariant, explaining the gap between all-slot match (4/20, 20%) and formal decision determinacy (9/20, 45%).
3. **Explicit Slot Constraints Restore Determinacy**: Under `condition_prompt`, standalone decision determinacy rises to 95% (19/20) on `1.5B` and 100% (20/20) on both `Qwen-Plus` and `Qwen-Max` (100% across all three models when policy identity is granted). On `Qwen-Plus`, one constrained rewrite omits the illness/accident cause on a post-waiting-period seed where both completions lead to coverage, yielding 19/20 all-slot match but 20/20 decision determinacy. On `1.5B`, the single `condition_prompt` failure (`condition-008` on `pa_h865`) drops the product title (`policy_id`), so its standalone reachable set spans multiple contracts while granted-policy determinacy reaches 20/20.

## 4. Extension to Four Additional Official Insurance Policies

### 4.1 Benefit-Specific Clause Modeling and Unified Parser Refinement

Because the initial slot parser was built on the five development policies, we extend evaluation to four additional official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (`data_v3/heldout_clauses_sources.json`). Rather than claiming a blind zero-shot evaluation of an untouched development parser, we explicitly document the refinements added to the shared parser (`extract_fields_surface_normalized` in `src/analyze_v3.py`) and rule verifier (`src/equal_budget_and_heldout_study.py`) to support these four contracts: we added colloquial product-name aliases for the four policies and introduced a benefit-type slot (`benefit_id`) for contracts whose waiting periods differ by benefit. The resulting evaluation is therefore a **retrospective post-refinement evaluation** of the unified parser and benefit-aware verifier.

In practice, real insurance contracts often attach different waiting periods and exemption rules to distinct *benefit types* within the same policy rather than leaving the waiting period as an arbitrary user-chosen number. We model the four extension contracts directly against Article 4 ("第四条 保险责任", Page 1) of their source PDF clauses:

1. `pa_2011_36` (*Ping An Worry-Free Inpatient Medical Insurance*, Article 4, Page 1, SHA256 prefix `10916a68758a`): defines two distinct benefits with benefit-specific waiting periods and exemption rules:
   - *General Inpatient Allowance* (`general_hospital`, "（一）一般住院医疗津贴"): 30-day waiting period; accidental injury waives the waiting period (`accident_exempt=True`), and continuous renewal (`续保`) waives the waiting period (`approval_exempt=True`).
   - *Cancer Inpatient Allowance* (`cancer_hospital`, "（二）癌症住院医疗津贴"): 90-day waiting period; continuous renewal (`续保`) waives the waiting period (`approval_exempt=True`), whereas accidental injury is not applicable to cancer onset (`accident_exempt=False`).
   Under this benefit-resolved model, on Days 31–90 where the waiting period determines the outcome (e.g., first-time sickness claims without an approved renewal waiver that exempts both benefits), omitting which benefit is claimed—or conflating the two benefits by asking whether the claim should be settled under the 30-day general inpatient allowance or the 90-day cancer inpatient allowance—yields reachable set `['WAIT_EXCLUDED', 'WAIT_NOT_EXCLUDED']` and is rejected as `DEGRADED_TO_CLARIFY` (whereas if an explicit renewal waiver exempts both benefits, omitting the benefit leaves the waiting-period outcome invariant). Any question that pairs the general allowance with a 90-day wait or the cancer allowance with a 30-day wait is flagged as `CONDITION_MUTATED`.
2. `pa_p0254` (*Ping An Inpatient Medical Insurance*, Article 4, Page 1, SHA256 prefix `d6ad23849fec`): 90-day waiting period for sickness inpatient medical care; both accidental injury and continuous renewal (`续保`) waive the waiting period.
3. `pa_p0321` (*Ping An Lung Cancer Disease Insurance*, Article 4, Page 1, SHA256 prefix `e04e0b452858`): a single-disease policy with a 30-day waiting period for first-time diagnosis of **primary lung cancer, explicitly excluding carcinoma in situ** ("初次发生原发性肺癌，上述原发性肺癌不包括原位癌"); continuous renewal (`续保`) waives the waiting period, and there is no accidental-injury exemption (`accident_exempt=False`).
4. `cpic_93896` (*CPIC Additional [Guangxi] Student & Child Inpatient Medical Insurance [2025 Edition]*, SHA256 prefix `309d8f029fb8`): Article 4, Section 1 (Page 1, "一、住院医疗费用保险金") specifies a 30-day waiting period for sickness hospitalization and states that accidental injury and renewal are not subject to the 30-day limit ("因意外伤害事故住院治疗的，或续保不受30日规定的限制"), while Article 6 (Page 3, "第六条") specifies that the contract is a one-year non-guaranteed renewal rider ("本产品为不保证续保产品"). ### 4.2 Extension Policy Results and Title-Masked Retrieval

1. **Conversational Rewriting Collapses Eligibility Determinacy**: On the four extension policies, `ordinary_style` achieves high surface novelty ($1-\kappa_2(q', q_{\text{seed}}) = 0.5380$) and low clause copying ($\kappa_2(q', d^*) = 0.1917$), but drops the product title on 16/16 seeds and drops pivotal waiting-period conditions (such as enrollment status, elapsed day, or waiver approval) on 11/16 seeds even when the policy identity is granted, leaving 0/16 (0.0%) standalone decision preservation and 5/16 (31.2%) granted-policy decision preservation.
2. **Constrained Prompting Already Saturates Single-Contract Insurance Rules (Zero Second-Pass Repairs Needed)**: When prompted with explicit slot constraints (`condition_prompt`), `Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct` already preserves all slots and formal decisions on 16/16 (100.0%) seeds on the first pass with 0/16 mutations, at the cost of lower surface novelty ($1-\kappa_2 = 0.1251$, clause copy $\kappa_2 = 0.3355$). 3. **What Title-Masked Retrieval Actually Measures**: Across the combined 9-clause pool (5 development + 4 extension clauses), masking product titles, colloquial product aliases, and insurer names tests how strongly retrieval depends on explicit product-name anchors. For title-preserving queries (`seed_passthrough` and `condition_prompt`), masking product titles drops 9-clause dense retrieval (`bge_small_zh`) Top-1 accuracy from 75.0% (12/16) to 31.2% (5/16) (with `bm25_char2` at 75.0%), because multiple inpatient medical contracts share identical 30-day or 90-day waiting-period clauses once product names are removed. Conversely, for `ordinary_style` rewrites—which already omit product titles in the query—removing those unmatched title strings from the index documents shifts document representations and rankings, slightly *increasing* 9-clause Top-1 from 56.2% / 43.8% to 62.5% / 56.2%. Thus, title-masked retrieval specifically diagnoses product-title anchor dependence rather than acting as a uniform penalty across query styles.

### 5.2 Multi-Threshold Findings and Statistical Scope

1. **100% Top-1 Retrieval Coexists with 93.8% Standalone Decision Loss (and 56.2% Granted-Clause Loss)**: All six non-JSON methods achieve 100% (16/16) Top-1 retrieval on both `bm25_char2` and `bge_small_zh` under both standard and title-masked evaluation because the four clauses have distinct domain vocabulary. Under `ordinary_style`, however, only 1/16 (6.2%) of rewrites preserve the reference decision standalone: `cross-001` (Day 7 of a 14-day washout $\rightarrow$ excluded) and `cross-002` (Day 20 $\rightarrow$ eligible) collapse into the identical question ("我现在能申请奥希替尼的门诊特药报销吗？"), and `cross-011` flips traffic fault polarity from "本人主要责任" (principal fault) to "不是主要责任" (non-principal fault). Even when the source rule identity (`clause_id`) is granted by context, `ordinary_style` preserves decisions on only 7/16 (43.8%) seeds (+6 seeds where `clause_id` was the only missing pivotal slot), while 9/16 (56.2%) still fail due to omitted or mutated substantive conditions (7/16 clarify, 2/16 mutated).
2. **Threshold Overwriting Under Open-Loop Prompting and Early Stopping in Two-Line JSON Prompts**: Under `condition_prompt`, decision preservation rises to 7/16 (43.8% both standalone and granted-clause), but 5/16 (31.2%) of rewrites suffer threshold-overwriting mutations where the 1.5B model replaces the user's elapsed days (7, 45, or 120 days) with the clause's statutory threshold (14 or 90 days). Meanwhile, logging `raw_response` and `generated_tokens` for `structured_json_rewrite` reveals why the two-line format failed: 0/16 responses hit the 160-token generation cap (`generated_tokens` $\in [45, 111]$ on the multi-threshold set, versus 2/16 hitting the 160-token cap on the 4-policy insurance set); instead, in 16/16 multi-threshold cases the 1.5B model terminated generation immediately after closing the first-line JSON brace without emitting the second-line `QUESTION:` field. Because a two-part output format confounds instruction-following with condition retention on a 1.5B model, our main comparison relies on single-question natural-language baselines (`condition_prompt` and `generic_self_refine`).
3. **Symbolic Slot Repair vs. Equal-Budget Unguided Self-Refinement**: At the matched 26-call budget, unguided self-refinement (`generic_self_refine`, 26 calls) improves decision determinacy by only +1 seed over `condition_prompt` (8/16 [50.0%] vs. 7/16 [43.8%], unchanged under granted-clause evaluation), leaving 4/16 (25.0%) threshold mutations unresolved. Running the second self-check pass on all 16 seeds without using the parser trigger (32 calls) produces the exact same 8/16 (50.0%) decision determinacy and 4/16 (25.0%) mutation rate. By contrast, supplying symbolic slot diagnostics in `slot_guard_repair` (26 calls) raises standalone all-slot match from 6/16 (37.5%) to 11/16 (68.8%), lifts standalone decision determinacy from 8/16 (50.0%) to 12/16 (75.0%: 8/8 healthcare, 4/8 labor-law), and reduces parser-detected mutations from 4/16 (25.0%) to 0/16 (0.0%), while maintaining higher template novelty (0.3342 vs. 0.3151) and lower clause copying ($\kappa_2 = 0.4913$ vs. 0.5748). Consequently, under **Granted-Clause Sensitivity** (`grant_source_clause=True`, where `clause_id` is supplied by the source context or matched via domain-unique non-compete phrases), `slot_guard_repair` rises from 12/16 (75.0%) to **15/16 (93.8%)**, whereas `condition_prompt` (7/16) and `generic_self_refine` (8/16) remain unchanged because their errors are substantive condition omissions or threshold mutations.
4. **Small-Sample Statistical Scope (Pre-Specified Standalone vs. Post-Hoc Granted-Clause Sensitivity)**: On $N=16$ paired seeds, our primary pre-specified standalone comparison of `slot_guard_repair` (12/16) against `generic_self_refine` (8/16) yields 4 discordant wins (`cross-002`, `cross-003`, `cross-004`, `cross-009`) and 0 losses (exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.125$; vs. `condition_prompt` at 7/16, 5 wins and 0 losses, $p=0.0625$). In the post-hoc granted-clause sensitivity analysis (`15/16` vs. `8/16`), `slot_guard_repair` yields 7 discordant wins and 0 losses (exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.015625$; vs. `condition_prompt` at 7/16, 8 wins and 0 losses, $p=0.007812$). We report the standalone test ($p=0.125$) as the primary result and the granted-clause test ($p=0.015625$) as a conditional sensitivity decomposition showing that 3 of the 4 residual `slot_guard_repair` errors are formal title omissions rather than substantive condition failures.

### 6.1 Separating `PARSE_UNCERTAIN` from `CLARIFY_REQUIRED` and Three-Layer Verification Check

To avoid conflating parser grammar limitations with genuine question ambiguity, our verifier uses a text-only uncertainty gate `detect_parse_uncertainty(text)` that assigns each output to one of four mutually exclusive states without reading reference seed metadata (`seed_frame`): (1) `PRESERVED_DECISION` (unique reference decision preserved, zero slot mutations, and `is_parse_uncertain = False`), (2) `CONDITION_MUTATED`, (3) `CLARIFY_REQUIRED` (missing pivotal slot(s) within supported syntax, accompanied by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses), and (4) `PARSE_UNCERTAIN`. The fourth state triggers purely from query text when the query contains unsupported time units (months, weeks, or calendar dates), parenthetical cross-unit corrections, negated durations (`未满/不足 N 天`), conflicting day numbers within a single clause, or unmodeled disease-specific waiting-period branches (such as `pa_h716`'s 90-day wait for adenoid hypertrophy / hernia / tonsil / female reproductive organ diseases versus 30 days for other illnesses).

1. **Layer 1: Clause-to-Rule Mapping Audit (`data_v3/clause_rule_mapping_audit.json` & `untouched_holdout_sources.json`)**: Verifies all 26 official insurance PDFs and 4 multi-threshold rules against SHA256 checksums, PDF page numbers, bounding-box coordinates, waiting-period days, accident/renewal exemptions, and unmodeled exclusion boundaries. ### 6.3 Downstream Pipeline Selection Under Reference-Free Inference

To test whether upstream query drift distorts downstream system selection without gold-label leakage, we use a top-level pure inference function `predict(question, retrieved_clause, inference_config)`. This function accepts only the query string `question`, the Top-1 retrieved clause ID `retrieved_clause`, and deployment-time configuration (`system_name`, `domain_type`, and compiled clause rules), with no access to `seed`, `seed['reference_decision']`, or expected `policy_id`/`clause_id`. Dense retrieval uses cached `BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5` embeddings across all 80 reference queries and 640 confirmation queries, raising a `KeyError` on any uncached string and applying a `BM25 <= 0` zero-overlap guard so out-of-domain gibberish inputs return `None`. Flipping `seed['reference_decision']` from `WAIT_EXCLUDED` to `WAIT_NOT_EXCLUDED` while holding the question and retrieved clause fixed leaves every pipeline's prediction unchanged at `WAIT_EXCLUDED`.

We evaluate five pipelines across the 24-document confirmation pool (`data_v3/downstream_rag_selection_study.json`): `S1_dense_rule_clarify` (`bge_small_zh` Top-1 + text slot parser that outputs `CLARIFY` when `obs[id_key] != retrieved_clause`, pivotal slots are missing, or `PARSE_UNCERTAIN`), `S2_bm25_rule_clarify` (`bm25_char2` Top-1 + the same rule engine), `S3_dense_aggressive_deny` (`bge_small_zh` Top-1 + defaults to exclusion on ambiguity), `S4_dense_aggressive_approve` (`bge_small_zh` Top-1 + defaults to approval on ambiguity), and `S5_dense_threshold_echo_heuristic` (`bge_small_zh` Top-1 + surface number-vs.-threshold heuristic).

On the 80-case reference benchmark (40 complete seeds + 40 missing-pivotal clarify seeds), reference accuracy is **`S1` (97.50%, 78/80) > `S2` (93.75%, 75/80) > `S5` (41.25%, 33/80) > `S3` (35.00%, 28/80) > `S4` (31.25%, 25/80)**. When `ordinary_style` rewrites across the 40 confirmation seeds (`combined_40_seeds`) inherit their seed's binary label without verification, $97.5\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of test queries degrade into `CLARIFY` cases. Clarifying pipelines `S1` and `S2` drop to **$0.0\%\text{--}2.5\%$**, whereas `S3_dense_aggressive_deny` (`50.0%`) ranks first. This reverses system rankings (**Kendall's $\tau = -0.7746$, Spearman's $\rho = -0.8660$ on `Qwen-Plus`; $\tau = -0.8367$, $\rho = -0.8944$ on `DeepSeek`**) and incurs a **$0.6250$ selection regret** ($0.9750 - 0.3500$). Applying `slot_guard_repair` reduces label corruption to **0.0%**, retains **97.5% (39/40)** scenario coverage (vs. $82.5\%\text{--}92.5\%$ for `verify_only_filter`), and recovers the reference ranking on both model families (**Kendall's $\tau = 1.0000$, Spearman's $\rho = 1.0000$, selection regret = 0.0000**).

## 7. Independent Raw-Clause Adjudication, Real LLM RAG Selection, and Multi-Rule Generalization

The experiments in Sections 3–6 leave four methodological questions open: (i) because `slot_guard_repair` uses the symbolic slot parser for repair feedback, evaluating methods with that same parser risks coupled false accepts or false rejects; (ii) whether LLM rewriting provides measurable utility over zero-call multi-template rendering (`multi_template_3`) or complete 6-slot schema rendering (`complete_6slot_schema_render`); (iii) whether downstream ranking distortion persists when synthetic rule-engine variants (`S1`–`S5`) are replaced by real end-to-end cloud LLM RAG pipelines; and (iv) whether reachability-guided verification generalizes beyond waiting periods and achieves statistical significance at the document-cluster level ($C=25$) after multiple-testing correction. We address all four questions in `src/ccf_a_upgrade_study.py` (`data_v3/ccf_a_upgrade_summary.json`), logging live HTTP response headers (`http_request_id` and `http_date`) across every new API call.

### 7.1 Blinded Three-Flagship Raw-Clause Adjudication and Four-Tier Target Decomposition

To evaluate generated queries without relying on the symbolic regex parser, seed slot frames, or hand-compiled rule tables, we construct a **blinded three-flagship raw-clause adjudication panel** comprising `Kimi-K3` (`kimi-k3`, Moonshot AI, an independent third-party model family never used as a query generator), `DeepSeek-V4-Pro` (`deepseek-v4-pro`), and `Qwen3.8-Max` (`qwen3.8-max`) at `temperature=0`. For each seed, all candidate queries across methods and generators are randomized and presented with **only** the question text and the verbatim official PDF clause text (`data_v3/ccf_a_raw_clause_adjudications.jsonl`, 264 batches covering 1,408 core method records plus 160 deterministic template baseline records across all 88 seeds). Each flagship judge independently predicts the clause-grounded eligibility decision (`WAIT_EXCLUDED`/`RULE_EXCLUDED`, `WAIT_NOT_EXCLUDED`/`RULE_ELIGIBLE`, or `CLARIFY_REQUIRED`) and rates conversational naturalness (1–5). We explicitly report this protocol as **cross-family flagship raw-clause adjudication** rather than human annotation.

Across the 640 core confirmation records (40 seeds $\times$ 2 generators $\times$ 8 methods), the three flagship judges reach substantial inter-adjudicator reliability: **three-rater Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7411$**, with pairwise Cohen's $\kappa = 0.7794$ (`Kimi-K3` vs. `DeepSeek-V4-Pro`), $0.7527$ (`Kimi-K3` vs. `Qwen3.8-Max`), and $0.6973$ (`Qwen3.8-Max` vs. `DeepSeek-V4-Pro`). Across all 88 seeds ($N=1,408$ core records), three-rater Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.6741$ and symbolic verifier vs. panel majority exact agreement is `84.80%` (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.5736$). On the 640-record confirmation set, exact agreement between the symbolic verifier and the 3-flagship panel majority is **87.19% (558/640, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.5901$)**, decomposing into `TP = 475`, `TN = 83`, **46 parser false rejects (`7.19%`)**, and **36 parser false accepts (`5.62%`)**:
- **Parser False Rejects (`46/640 = 7.19%`)**: Occur when rewrites use natural colloquialisms outside the frozen regex list (e.g., `"还没获"` instead of `"未获"`, `"连续30天未支付"` in `law_noncompete`, or omitting redundant single-threshold reminders on single-threshold policies) while all three flagship judges still determine the exact coverage outcome from the raw PDF text.
- **Parser False Accepts (`36/640 = 5.62%`)**: Concentrated in three specific clause-level modeling boundaries: (a) in `cpic_106702` (`fconf-ins-017/018`), the verbatim PDF clause specifies a 15-day waiting period for the *primary insured* (maternal complications) and a 0-day waiting period for the *dependent insured* (newborn care); when a seed or rewrite omits whether the patient is the mother or newborn, the 1-threshold symbolic schema accepts it while all three raw-clause judges output `CLARIFY_REQUIRED`; (b) in `fconf-cross-011`, the Cartesian seed combines `"已按月足额支付补偿"` (paid in full monthly) with `"连续未付已达第105天"` (unpaid for 105 days), which the raw-clause panel flags as internally contradictory; and (c) in `fconf-cross-001/007`, `seed_passthrough`, `condition_prompt`, and `slot_guard_repair` follow the seed in leaving default-false emergency waivers implicit, whereas `structured_json_untruncated_320` prints `"waiver": "无特批豁免"` in its JSON prefix, allowing `Kimi-K3` and `Qwen3.8-Max` to rule out emergency overrides explicitly.

## 8. Discussion, Practical Recommendations, and Limitations

### Practical Recommendations for Rule-Governed RAG Evaluation

1. **Do not treat retrieval Top-1, surface fluency, or zero-call templates as sufficient for RAG benchmarking**: Across 26 official insurance contracts and four rule families, 75%–100% Top-1 retrieval coexists with 55%–100% decision ambiguity or mutation under conversational rewriting, causing severe evaluation error ($\text{MAE} = 0.60\text{–}0.75$) and rank inversion ($\tau \in [-0.79, -0.50]$) across real cloud LLM RAG pipelines. Conversely, zero-call templates preserve slots but under-discriminate dense vs. lexical retrieval ($\text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}} = 0.31\text{–}0.33$ vs. $0.53$).
2. **Pair verified complete rewrites with slot-dropped clarify probes when evaluating abstention-capable LLM readers**: Evaluating RAG pipelines solely on determinate queries rewards forced-binary guessers (`L5`–`L6`) that never abstain. Pairing `slot_guard_repair` queries with controlled clarify probes reduces evaluation MAE against the 80-query reference benchmark to 0.019–0.027 and reliably separates abstention-capable flagship readers from forced-binary readers.
3. **Audit symbolic verifiers against raw-clause flagship panels to expose unmodeled sub-clause boundaries**: Cross-checking our regex verifier against the blinded 3-flagship raw-clause panel ($\kappa = 0.7411$, 87.19% agreement) pinpoints both regex false rejects (7.19%) and unmodeled sub-clause false accepts (5.62%, such as maternal vs. newborn waiting periods in `cpic_106702`).

### Limitations

First, while we audit our symbolic verifier against a blinded three-flagship raw-clause panel (`Kimi-K3`, `DeepSeek-V4-Pro`, and `Qwen3.8-Max`, $N=1,408$ core records) and verify 11/11 zero-shot LLM schema extraction on confirmation policies, this raw-clause adjudication is performed by frontier language models rather than licensed human claims adjusters; multi-annotator human legal/actuarial review remains future work. Second, the raw-clause panel audit reveals concrete modeling boundaries in our 6-slot schemas: when a contract contains sub-clause distinctions not represented in the schema (such as maternal 15-day vs. newborn 0-day waiting periods in `cpic_106702`) or when a seed omits explicit negative waiver text on clauses with emergency overrides (`fconf-cross-001/007`), the symbolic verifier accepts queries that strict raw-clause readers flag for clarification (36/640 cases on confirmation). Because `structured_json_untruncated_320` prints explicit negative waiver strings in its JSON header, it achieves 78/80 (97.5%) panel-majority usability on the 40-seed confirmation set versus 74/80 (92.5%) for `slot_guard_repair`, whereas across the pooled 88-seed ($N=176$) benchmark `slot_guard_repair` leads on both symbolic usability (174/176 [98.86%] vs. 153/176 [86.93%], Holm-adjusted 25-clause clustered $p = 0.00293$) and raw-clause panel usability (166/176 [94.32%] vs. 163/176 [92.61%]). Third, our regex slot extractors conservatively route unsupported calendar-date arithmetic and month/week expressions to `PARSE_UNCERTAIN`. Finally, the community QA corpus in Appendix A (`baoxianzhidao_filter.csv`) carries noisy community best-answer labels, unverified redistribution licensing, and potential pretraining exposure.

1. **Full-Test Dense Lead Confirmed Under Zero-Truncation MaxP**: Across all 2,994 held-out real queries, `bge_small_zh` beats `rrf_bm25_bge` ($0.5216$ vs. $0.5030$, $\Delta = +0.0187$, 95% CI $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$). Splitting all 509 long documents into overlapping 512-token windows with a 256-token stride (762 overflow tail windows, zero truncation) under `Full-MaxP` confirms this lead ($0.5165$ vs. $0.5007$, $\Delta = +0.0158$, 95% CI $[+0.0049, +0.0267]$).
2. **Source-Subsample Confounding vs. Paired Difference-in-Differences**: Restricting real test queries to the sampled synthetic source documents ($n=209$ for `1.5B`; $n=153$ for `Qwen-Plus`) already shifts the real-query point estimate toward `rrf_bm25_bge` ($\Delta_{\text{BGE}-\text{RRF}} = -0.0279$ and $-0.0312$, CIs spanning zero). While `zero_shot` synthesis raises query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ ($0.3253 \rightarrow 0.4775$ on `1.5B`; $0.3167 \rightarrow 0.4638$ on `Qwen-Plus`) and boosts `bm25_char2` ($+0.0925$ and $+0.2457$), the paired difference-in-differences $\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$ has 95% CIs spanning zero across all four settings under both query-weighted and document-clustered bootstrap resampling ($+0.0123$, $-0.0259$, $-0.0020$, and $-0.0298$). Thus, unstratified source subsampling is a primary confounder alongside lexical overlap inflation.
3. **Negative Marginal Reweighting vs. Post-Hoc Overlap Gating and Heuristic Candidate-Set Abstention**: Marginal TF-IDF propensity weighting (`synthetic_density_weighted`) fails to pick `bge_small_zh` (100% error across $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$) because hybrid-vs.-dense advantage depends on relational query-to-source overlap $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ (`bge_small_zh` leads on $\kappa_2 < 0.25$ across real and all four synthetic sets, whereas `rrf_bm25_bge` leads on $\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$). Post-hoc filtering of synthetic queries to $\kappa_2(q', d^*) \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$ (or quantile cutoffs $q \in [0.10, 0.30]$ at $B=0$) recovers `bge_small_zh`, while a heuristic paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule on `real_only` (retaining candidates whose 95th-percentile bootstrap difference against the empirical calibration winner is $\ge 0$, without claiming formal simultaneous or post-selection coverage guarantees) reduces confident wrong commitments at $B=25$ from 60% to 0% (80% abstention rate, mean candidate set size 2.60).
