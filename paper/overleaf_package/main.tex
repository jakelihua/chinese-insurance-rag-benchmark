\begin{abstract}
Synthetic queries are widely used to evaluate retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems before real user logs exist. In rule-governed insurance domains, however, a synthetic query can retrieve the right clause while dropping or altering a condition that determines coverage. We test whether synthetic rewrites preserve waiting-period decisions across nine official Chinese insurance policies (five development, four held-out) and a 16-seed synthetic multi-threshold testbed, using off-the-shelf models without fine-tuning. We distinguish full slot retention from decision determinacy: whether a rewrite keeps the original coverage outcome, flips it through condition mutation, or becomes ambiguous enough to require follow-up clarification. On the development policies, unconstrained conversational rewriting preserves standalone decisions on only $30\%$, $0\%$, and $45\%$ of seeds across three Qwen model scales, whereas explicit condition constraints reach $95\%\text{--}100\%$. On four held-out policies with benefit-specific waiting periods, conversational rewrites preserve $0/16$ standalone decisions ($5/16$ when the product name is given), while constrained prompting preserves $16/16$. On the 16-seed multi-threshold testbed, parser-guided repair preserves $12/16$ decisions versus $8/16$ for unguided self-refinement (exact McNemar $p=0.125$). A complementary retrieval audit on $3{,}744$ community QA pairs shows that document subsampling and lexical copying confound synthetic retriever rankings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating a vertical-domain retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system often begins before real user traffic is available. A standard cold-start practice in retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas,zhu2025rageval,filice2025datamorgana} is to prompt an instruction-tuned language model to turn domain passages into synthetic user questions, filter the outputs for surface fluency, and use the resulting benchmark to rank retrievers or score end-to-end answers.

Prior work has shown that synthetic queries can shift system rankings relative to real user queries \cite{rahmani2024syndl,vanelburg2025synthetic,kucia2026beyond}. In rule-governed settings such as insurance underwriting and claims review, a more direct problem arises at the level of individual test cases: retrieval metrics reward lexical and topical similarity, whereas coverage decisions depend on discrete eligibility conditions. Under Chinese commercial health insurance clauses, whether a claim is excluded by a waiting period depends on the exact product and benefit claimed, whether the policy is a first-time purchase or a continuous renewal, how many days have elapsed since the policy took effect, whether hospitalization resulted from illness or accidental injury, and whether the insurer approved a waiting-period waiver.

When a language model rewrites a structured eligibility scenario into a natural-sounding user question, two distinct failure modes occur even when the rewrite ranks the source clause at Top-1:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Ambiguity Degradation (Clarification Required)}: To make a question sound conversational and brief, models frequently drop product titles, benefit types, enrollment status, or elapsed days. Omitting a \emph{pivotal} slot turns a decidable test case into an underspecified inquiry under which both coverage and exclusion remain possible. Conversely, omitting a \emph{non-pivotal} slot---for example, dropping whether a claim arose from illness or accident after the waiting period has already expired---leaves the coverage outcome unchanged. Simple slot-matching rules conflate pivotal and non-pivotal omissions, while retrieval scores ignore both.
  \item \textbf{Condition Mutation and Decision Flip}: Smaller models and open-loop prompts often alter numeric durations (for instance, turning ``Day~20'' into ``Day~2'') or overwrite the policyholder's actual elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the statutory waiting-period threshold stated in the clause ($14$ or $90$ days). Such edits increase lexical overlap with the target document while reversing the ground-truth eligibility decision.
\end{enumerate}

This paper asks a focused diagnostic question: \emph{when synthetic queries are generated from rule-bearing clauses, do they preserve the underlying eligibility decision, and can lightweight symbolic verification detect and repair decision drift?} Rather than proposing a new model architecture or claiming automated rule extraction from arbitrary legal prose, we formalize hand-compiled waiting-period rules over traceable policy clauses and separate surface slot completeness from reference-independent decision determinacy. Specifically, our verifier enumerates all admissible completions of any omitted fields over threshold-partitioned equivalence regions without consulting reference seed metadata, classifying each synthetic rewrite as preserving the unique reference decision, mutating a condition (or flipping the unique decision), or degrading into an ambiguous query certified by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses.

Our empirical analysis covers three connected settings, with supplementary community-QA retrieval diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Development Policy Evaluation Across Model Scales (Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies})}: On five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs ($20$ seed scenarios, $80$ controlled cases, and $120$ paired rewrites across local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and cloud Qwen-Plus and Qwen-Max), we measure how unconstrained conversational rewriting versus explicit slot prompting affects slot retention and decision determinacy. Conversational rewrites preserve standalone decisions on only $30\%$, $0\%$, and $45\%$ of seeds across the three model scales, whereas explicit condition constraints reach $95\%\text{--}100\%$.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Policy Evaluation and Benefit-Specific Waiting Periods (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies})}: We test the frozen development-set parser and reachability verifier on four unseen official insurance policy PDFs ($16$ seeds, $64$ controlled cases), explicitly modeling benefit-specific waiting periods within a single contract (e.g., general inpatient allowance with a 30-day wait and accident exemption versus cancer inpatient allowance with a 90-day wait). Unconstrained conversational rewrites preserve $0/16$ standalone decisions ($5/16$ when the policy identity is given), while constrained prompting preserves $16/16$. Title-masked retrieval across all nine clauses further shows that standard retrieval scores rely heavily on product-title strings rather than eligibility conditions.
  \item \textbf{Equal-Budget Repair on Multi-Threshold Rules (Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold})}: On a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark where all non-JSON rewrites reach $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval, we compare symbolic parser-guided repair against an equal-budget unguided self-refinement baseline ($26$ LLM calls). Symbolic feedback raises decision preservation from $8/16$ ($50.0\%$) to $12/16$ ($75.0\%$, exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.125$) and reduces parser-detected condition mutations from $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) to $0/16$ ($0.0\%$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Dataset Provenance}

\paragraph{Synthetic Queries and Retriever Ranking Shifts.}
LLM-generated queries are widely used to train dense retrievers \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and to construct automated RAG benchmarks \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas}. Several studies have examined whether synthetic benchmarks reproduce real retriever rankings. Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and van Elburg et al.\ \cite{vanelburg2025synthetic} analyze system-ranking correlations and generator biases across synthetic test collections. Kucia and Gawlik \cite{kucia2026beyond} compare $1{,}851$ LLM-generated synthetic questions against $322$ student survey questions for a university faculty information system across dense, lexical (BM25), and hybrid (RRF) retrievers, showing that synthetic queries overestimate absolute performance and shift system rankings relative to human questions. Because ranking shifts between real and synthetic queries are already established in prior work, we treat our community-QA retrieval audit (Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}) as a complementary diagnostic that separates document-subsampling variance from within-document lexical copying.

\paragraph{Schema-Driven RAG Synthesis and Question Evaluation.}
To ground synthetic queries in domain structure, RAGEval \cite{zhu2025rageval} extracts domain schemas from financial, medical, and legal documents to generate evaluation sets, while DataMorgana \cite{filice2025datamorgana} parameterizes user personas and question categories for RAG benchmarking. For general question generation, QGEval \cite{fu2024qgeval} benchmarks multi-dimensional quality across seven criteria: fluency, clarity, conciseness, relevance, consistency, answerability, and answer consistency. However, open-loop schema prompting and surface answerability judges do not compile clause thresholds into a formal reachability check. As our multi-threshold experiments in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} show, even when a prompt explicitly lists every required slot, a 1.5B generator still overwrites user durations with clause thresholds in $25.0\%\text{--}31.2\%$ of cases unless checked by a symbolic verifier.

\paragraph{Rule-Grounded Clarification, Risk Control, and Insurance Benchmarks.}
In conversational QA, ShARC \cite{saeidi2018sharc} studies rule-grounded answering where an assistant must ask follow-up questions when decision variables are underspecified. Recent work such as CLAMBER \cite{zhang2024clamber} and AskBench \cite{zhao2026askbench} benchmarks how well LLMs detect ambiguous user requests and ask clarifying questions in open-domain and clinical dialogues. From a selective prediction perspective, RC-RAG \cite{chen2024rcrag} uses counterfactual prompting to estimate RAG confidence and control risk via selective abstention under misleading contexts. In the insurance domain, InsuranceQA \cite{feng2020insuranceqa} provides community QA pairs for answer selection, and InsQABench \cite{ding2025insqabench} evaluates LLM question answering over insurance product rules. Our focus is upstream of assistant answering: rather than testing whether a downstream chatbot asks clarifying questions on underspecified prompts, we verify whether \emph{synthetic evaluation queries} retain the conditions needed to keep their reference labels determinate during benchmark creation.

\paragraph{Statistical Calibration with Small Gold Samples.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction}, ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}, and PRECISE \cite{divekar2026precise} combine synthetic data and automated LLM judges with small human-labeled calibration sets to construct valid confidence intervals for RAG evaluation metrics. When synthetic queries are generated from a small subset of corpus documents without paired real queries across the full index, unpaired shrinkage and marginal query-propensity weighting fail to correct document-subsampling and query-to-source overlap biases; we report these negative calibration results alongside a bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.

\subsection{Data Provenance, Licensing Scope, and Inference-Only Setup}
Table~\ref{tab:data_audit} documents the origin, scope, and usage constraints of all study materials:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-Only Evaluation}: No generative LLM or embedding model weights are pre-trained, instruction-tuned, or fine-tuned in this work. All local runs (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} and \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5}) use frozen weights under deterministic \texttt{float32/eager} inference, and cloud API runs (\texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}) use \texttt{temperature}=0. Few-shot prompts provide three fixed calibration examples in context. The only fitted statistical parameters in our code are corpus TF-IDF/BM25 term frequencies and a 2-fold logistic regression classifier used for propensity-weighting diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.
  \item \textbf{Official Insurance Clauses versus Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rules}: The nine insurance policies ($5$ development policies in Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies} and $4$ held-out policies in Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies}) are official PDF wordings downloaded from public disclosure portals of Ping An, CPIC, and Taikang. Because public web availability does not confer a license to redistribute full commercial contracts, our repository releases SHA256 file hashes, source URLs, PDF page and bounding-box coordinates, and short verification excerpts rather than raw PDF binaries. In contrast, the four multi-threshold rules in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} are researcher-authored synthetic clauses designed for controlled stress testing.
  \item \textbf{Community QA Corpus (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv})}: Used in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}, this file ($8{,}362$ raw rows; $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid questions after normalization) comes from \texttt{meowcao/InsuranceModel}, which mirrors \texttt{SophonPlus/ChineseNlpCorpus} (\texttt{datasets/baoxianzhidao/intro.ipynb}). Upstream documentation states that the pairs were collected from Baidu Zhidao insurance Q\&A with original post URLs removed and sensitive fields filtered. Consequently, answers reflect historical community responses rather than actuarial ground truth, no verified open redistribution license chain is documented upstream, and Chinese foundation models may have encountered parts of this text during pretraining.
  \item \textbf{Proxy Parser Evaluation and Exploratory Semantic Audit}: Our formal reachability verifier relies on a synonym-aware regular-expression slot extractor. To inspect potential surface-phrasing mismatches between regex extraction and natural Chinese phrasing under limited human annotation resources, we combine two resources without overclaiming independent human validation: (i)~blinded two-rater HTML annotation packages (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, which remain uncompleted by external raters and are not claimed as human results in this paper), and (ii)~an item-by-item author-inspected and Qwen-assisted exploratory semantic audit across all $264$ primary generated questions (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}), written in natural claims-review language and explicitly marked as an exploratory single-team/LLM-assisted audit rather than independent multi-rater ground truth.
\end{enumerate}

Each development scenario frame specifies up to six fields: product identity ($20/20$ seeds), enrollment type (first-time purchase vs.\ renewal, $20/20$), policy effective day ($20/20$), claim cause (illness vs.\ accidental injury, $20/20$), selected plan waiting period ($4/20$ seeds on \texttt{cpic\_86017}), and insurer-approved waiting-period waiver ($3/20$ renewal seeds). From $20$ seed scenarios, we construct $80$ controlled test cases across four variants: \emph{complete} (all required slots stated), \emph{surface} (synonymous phrasing), \emph{missing-critical} (one pivotal condition removed, certified as requiring clarification via two completion witnesses that yield opposite decisions), and \emph{counterfactual} (one condition altered to flip the rule outcome between waiting-period exclusion and coverage).

Because a seed scenario can contain slots that are non-pivotal for its specific state---for example, on \texttt{pa\_h865} (30-day wait), a claim occurring on Day~45 satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting whether the cause was illness or accident still leads to coverage under every completion---we evaluate two distinct criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{All-Slot Completeness}: Every required field of the reference seed is extracted without omission or value conflict.
  \item \textbf{Reference-Independent Decision Determinacy}: Using only the slots extracted from the generated question and the public policy rules (without reading the reference seed's required-field list), the verifier enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unmentioned fields. Elapsed-day domains are partitioned into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t$, $= t$, $> t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ using representative days $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$), and all schema fields---including optional waiver overrides---are checked for value conflicts. A rewrite is classified as:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{Preserved Decision} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}) if no observed schema field conflicts with the seed and the set of reachable decisions across all admissible completions is the singleton matching the reference outcome;
    \item \emph{Condition Mutated / Decision Flip} (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} / \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP}) if any observed schema field conflicts with the seed or if the reachable set is a singleton with the opposite coverage decision;
    \item \emph{Degraded to Clarification} (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}) if both coverage and exclusion are reachable across completions ($|\text{reachable}| > 1$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Paired Rewriting Across Three Qwen Tiers}
We pass the $20$ seed questions and their source clause excerpts to three off-the-shelf generators---local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} (\texttt{temperature}=0, parameter counts undisclosed by the provider)---under two prompting conditions ($120$ total rewrites):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conversational Style (\texttt{ordinary\_style})}: Instructs the model to rewrite the seed question into a short, natural everyday user inquiry while keeping the original intent.
  \item \textbf{Condition-Constrained Prompt (\texttt{condition\_prompt})}: Explicitly instructs the model to retain the full product title, first-time vs.\ renewal status, effective day, illness vs.\ accident cause, coverage scope, plan waiting period, and waiver approval status without adding or omitting conditions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Development Policy Findings}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} reveal three key patterns:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{High Retrieval Accuracy Coexists with Severe Decision Loss Under Conversational Rewriting}: Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, cloud models achieve $80\%\text{--}90\%$ Top-1 clause retrieval, yet standalone decision determinacy is only $30\%$ ($6/20$) on \texttt{1.5B}, $0\%$ ($0/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and $45\%$ ($9/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}. Even when the target policy identity is granted by context, $45\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $25\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $20\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) of conversational rewrites remain undecidable or mutated.
  \item \textbf{Numeric Mutations on \texttt{1.5B} versus Conversational Compression on Cloud Models}: On \texttt{1.5B}, $3/20$ conversational rewrites (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016}) corrupt ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2), and two seeds with opposite ground-truth outcomes (\texttt{condition-009} with a 7-day wait on Day~10 vs.\ \texttt{condition-010} with a 25-day wait on Day~10) collapse into the exact same question after dropping the plan waiting period. On \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, numeric value mutations are $0/20$ in this sample (a finite-sample observation on $n=20$ seeds rather than proof of a zero population rate), but both cloud models omit administrative qualifiers to sound natural: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the policy title in $20/20$ seeds, and \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops the policy title in $10/20$ seeds and replaces ``首次投保'' (first-time purchase) with ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which does not distinguish first purchase from renewal) in $6/20$ seeds. When \texttt{Qwen-Max} keeps the product title ($10/20$ seeds), $5$ of those seeds omit enrollment status on non-waivable clauses or post-waiting-period days where the coverage decision is still invariant, explaining the gap between all-slot match ($4/20$, $20\%$) and formal decision determinacy ($9/20$, $45\%$).
  \item \textbf{Explicit Slot Constraints Restore Determinacy}: Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, standalone decision determinacy rises to $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on both \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} ($100\%$ across all three models when policy identity is granted). On \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, one constrained rewrite omits the illness/accident cause on a post-waiting-period seed where both completions lead to coverage, yielding $19/20$ all-slot match but $20/20$ decision determinacy. In our item-by-item exploratory semantic audit (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}), the single \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{condition\_prompt} failure (\texttt{condition-012}) is confirmed to have dropped the product title ``太康欣享全球'', matching the parser diagnosis ($39/40$ agreement across the 40 local development rewrites; on \texttt{condition-018} \texttt{ordinary\_style}, the model wrote ``第一天投保'', which is colloquial for first-time purchase and was conservatively left unparsed by the regex).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Out-of-Sample Evaluation on Unseen Official Insurance Policies}
\label{sec:heldout_policies}

\subsection{Benefit-Specific Clause Modeling and Unified Parser Verification}
Because the synonym-aware slot parser was designed on the five development policies in Table~\ref{tab:clauses}, we evaluate out-of-sample generalization on four additional official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_clauses\_sources.json}) using the exact same parser function (\texttt{extract\_fields\_surface\_normalized} in \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}) without adding any held-out-specific regular expressions.

Crucially, real insurance contracts often attach different waiting periods and exemption rules to distinct \emph{benefit types} within the same policy rather than leaving the waiting period as an arbitrary user-chosen number. We model the four held-out contracts faithfully against their source PDF clauses:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{pa\_2011\_36} (\emph{Ping An Worry-Free Inpatient Medical Insurance}, Clause~4, Page~1): defines two distinct benefits with benefit-specific waiting periods and exemption rules:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{General Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{general\_hospital}, ``一般住院医疗津贴''): 30-day waiting period; accidental injury waives the waiting period (\texttt{accident\_exempt=True}), and insurer-approved renewal waives the waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}).
    \item \emph{Cancer Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{cancer\_hospital}, ``癌症住院医疗津贴''): 90-day waiting period; insurer-approved renewal waives the waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}), whereas accidental injury is not applicable to cancer onset (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \end{itemize}
  Under this benefit-resolved model, any synthetic question on Days~$31\text{--}90$ that omits which benefit is claimed---or that conflates the two benefits by asking whether the claim should be settled under the 30-day general inpatient allowance or the 90-day cancer inpatient allowance---has reachable set $[\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}, \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}]$ and is rejected as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}. Any question that pairs the general allowance with a 90-day wait or the cancer allowance with a 30-day wait is flagged as \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0254} (\emph{Ping An Inpatient Medical Insurance}, Clause~2.1, Page~1): 90-day waiting period for inpatient medical expense reimbursement; both accidental injury and approved renewal waive the waiting period.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0321} (\emph{Ping An Lung Cancer Disease Insurance}, Clause~1.3, Page~1): a single-disease policy with a 30-day waiting period for lung cancer / carcinoma in situ where approved renewal waives the waiting period, but accidental injury does not waive the disease waiting period (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \item \texttt{cpic\_93896} (\emph{CPIC Additional [Guangxi] Student \& Child Inpatient Medical Insurance [2025 Edition]}): Clause~2.3 (Page~1) specifies a 30-day waiting period for illness hospitalization and states that accidental injury and renewal are not subject to the 30-day limit (``因意外伤害事故住院治疗的，或续保不受30日规定的限制''), while Clause~6.1 (Page~3) specifies that the contract is a one-year non-guaranteed renewal rider (``本产品为不保证续保产品''). To avoid conflating guaranteed and non-guaranteed renewal semantics, all four evaluation seeds on \texttt{cpic\_93896} test first-time enrollment (\texttt{enrollment=first}), where the 30-day illness waiting period and accidental-injury exemption apply directly.
\end{enumerate}

We construct $16$ natural policyholder consultation seeds ($4$ per held-out policy) and $64$ controlled test cases (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_controlled\_cases.jsonl}) using a three-part anti-spoiler design: (i)~stripping textbook book-title brackets (\texttt{《》}) and explicit clause-number headers from queries, (ii)~supporting colloquial product nicknames (e.g., ``平安住院安心医疗险'', ``太平洋广西学生幼儿住院医疗险'') in the shared surface-normalized parser, and (iii)~binding every test case to atomic scoring rubrics anchored by physical PDF filenames, page numbers, and bounding-box coordinates (\texttt{bbox\_pdf\_points}), alongside two opposite-outcome completion witnesses on every \texttt{missing\_critical} case. Running the development-set parser and reachability verifier certifies $64/64$ ($100\%$) of the controlled cases ($16/16$ \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} on \texttt{complete} and \texttt{surface}, $16/16$ \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} on \texttt{missing\_critical}, and $16/16$ \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} on \texttt{counterfactual}), with zero parser discrepancies against \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}.

\subsection{Held-Out Policy Results and Title-Masked Retrieval}
Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance} reports the held-out evaluation (\texttt{data\_v3/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_summary.json}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conversational Rewriting Collapses Eligibility Determinacy}: On the four unseen policies, \texttt{ordinary\_style} achieves high surface novelty ($1-\kappa_2(q', q_{\text{seed}}) = 0.5380$) and low clause copying ($\kappa_2(q', d^*) = 0.1917$), but drops the product title on $16/16$ seeds and drops pivotal waiting-period conditions (such as enrollment status, elapsed day, or waiver approval) on $11/16$ seeds even when the policy identity is granted, leaving $0/16$ ($0.0\%$) standalone decision preservation and $5/16$ ($31.2\%$) granted-policy decision preservation.

Consequently, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} triggers zero second-pass repairs ($16$ total calls, exact two-sided McNemar $p=1.0$ vs.\ \texttt{condition\_prompt}, and $p=0.03125$ vs.\ \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} on standalone decision preservation).   \item \textbf{What Title-Masked Retrieval Actually Measures}: Across the combined 9-clause pool ($5$ development + $4$ held-out clauses), masking product titles, colloquial product aliases, and insurer names tests how strongly retrieval depends on explicit product-name anchors. For title-preserving queries (\texttt{seed\_passthrough} and \texttt{condition\_prompt}), masking product titles drops 9-clause dense retrieval (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) Top-1 accuracy from $75.0\%$ ($12/16$) to $31.2\%$ ($5/16$) (with \texttt{bm25\_char2} at $75.0\%$), because multiple inpatient medical contracts share identical 30-day or 90-day waiting-period clauses once product names are removed. Thus, title-masked retrieval specifically diagnoses product-title anchor dependence rather than acting as a uniform penalty across query styles.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Equal-Call-Budget Comparison on Multi-Threshold Rules}
\label{sec:multithreshold}

\subsection{Synthetic Multi-Threshold Testbed and Evaluation Protocol}
While single-clause insurance rewrites already achieve $100\%$ decision preservation under \texttt{condition\_prompt} (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance}), multi-threshold rules with competing numeric windows place heavier demands on small local generators. To test whether closed-loop symbolic verification provides value beyond open-loop prompting and unguided LLM self-refinement under a matched call budget, we evaluate a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark (\texttt{src/cross\_domain\_study.py} and \texttt{src/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_study.py}). Unlike the nine official insurance policy PDFs in Sections~\ref{sec:dev_policies}--\ref{sec:heldout_policies}, these four clauses are researcher-authored synthetic rules inspired by clinical pathways and PRC labor statutes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (combining T790M status, a 14-day washout threshold after prior therapy, and an emergency specialist waiver); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (contrasting a 10-day knee replacement window against a 35-day hip replacement window, with active bleeding contraindication and hematology consult override).
  \item \textbf{Labor-Law-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (inspired by PRC Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14(6) on non-principal commuting fault and Art.~17 contrasting the 30-day employer filing deadline against the 365-day employee/kin deadline and administrative extension); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (inspired by PRC Labor Contract Law Art.~24 and SPC Judicial Interpretation~I Art.~38 on 90-day default of post-termination non-compete compensation, augmented with a synthetic supplemental grace-period agreement).
\end{itemize}

For each seed, unobserved durations are expanded over a complete threshold partition ($\{7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 30, 32, 35, 45, 90, 120, 365, 400\}$ covering $< t, = t, > t$ for all thresholds $\{10, 14, 30, 35, 90, 365\}$), and all six schema fields are checked for mutations and reachability without consulting reference seed metadata.

We compare seven methods on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=160):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{seed\_passthrough} ($0$ calls): deterministic template question prior to LLM rewriting (information-preservation upper bound, zero template novelty).
  \item \texttt{ordinary\_style} ($16$ calls): 1-call unconstrained conversational rewrite.
  \item \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($16$ calls): 1-call open-loop slot-constrained rewrite.
  \item \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($16$ calls): 1-call prompt instructing the model to output a single-line JSON object of all 6 slot values on Line~1 and \texttt{QUESTION: <question>} on Line~2.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (budget-matched $26$ calls): takes the 16 \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts and runs a second unguided LLM self-check pass (asking the model to verify all conditions against the source clause and seed question \emph{without symbolic parser diagnostics}) on the exact same $10$ seeds triggered by \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}. Sharing the trigger schedule isolates the effect of symbolic slot feedback in the repair prompt while holding both the total call budget ($26$ calls) and the refined seed subset fixed.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (all seeds, $32$ calls): runs the unguided second-pass LLM self-check across all $16$ seeds without consulting the symbolic parser trigger.
  \item \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($26$ calls): runs the symbolic slot parser on the $16$ \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts, triggers a second pass on the $10$ seeds where any slot is missing or mutated, and supplies the exact missing/mutated slot names and target values in the repair prompt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Threshold Findings and Statistical Scope}
Table~\ref{tab:crossdomain} summarizes four findings:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{100\% Top-1 Retrieval Coexists with 93.8\% Decision Loss}: All six non-JSON methods achieve $100\%$ ($16/16$) Top-1 retrieval on both \texttt{bm25\_char2} and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} under both standard and title-masked evaluation because the four clauses have distinct domain vocabulary. Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, however, only $1/16$ ($6.2\%$) of rewrites preserve the reference decision: \texttt{cross-001} (Day~7 of a 14-day washout $\rightarrow$ excluded) and \texttt{cross-002} (Day~20 $\rightarrow$ eligible) collapse into the identical question (``我现在能申请奥希替尼的门诊特药报销吗？''), and \texttt{cross-011} flips traffic fault polarity from ``本人主要责任'' (principal fault) to ``不是主要责任'' (non-principal fault).
  \item \textbf{Threshold Overwriting Under Open-Loop Prompting and Early Stopping in Two-Line JSON Prompts}: Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, decision preservation rises to $7/16$ ($43.8\%$), but $5/16$ ($31.2\%$) of rewrites suffer threshold-overwriting mutations where the 1.5B model replaces the user's elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the clause's statutory threshold ($14$ or $90$ days). Meanwhile, logging \texttt{raw\_response} and \texttt{generated\_tokens} for \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} reveals why the two-line format failed: $0/16$ responses hit the 160-token generation cap (\texttt{generated\_tokens} $\in [47, 73]$); instead, in $16/16$ cases the 1.5B model terminated generation immediately after closing the first-line JSON brace without emitting the second-line \texttt{QUESTION:} field. Because a two-part output format confounds instruction-following with condition retention on a 1.5B model, our main comparison relies on single-question natural-language baselines (\texttt{condition\_prompt} and \texttt{generic\_self\_refine}).
  \item \textbf{Symbolic Slot Repair vs.\ Equal-Budget Unguided Self-Refinement}: At the matched 26-call budget, unguided self-refinement (\texttt{generic\_self\_refine}, $26$ calls) improves decision determinacy by only $+1$ seed over \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($8/16$ [$50.0\%$] vs.\ $7/16$ [$43.8\%$]), leaving $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) threshold mutations unresolved. Running the second self-check pass on all $16$ seeds without using the parser trigger ($32$ calls) produces the exact same $8/16$ ($50.0\%$) decision determinacy and $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) mutation rate. By contrast, supplying symbolic slot diagnostics in \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($26$ calls) raises all-slot match from $6/16$ ($37.5\%$) to $11/16$ ($68.8\%$), lifts decision determinacy from $8/16$ ($50.0\%$) to $12/16$ ($75.0\%$: $8/8$ healthcare, $4/8$ labor-law), and reduces parser-detected mutations from $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) to $0/16$ ($0.0\%$), while maintaining higher template novelty ($0.3342$ vs.\ $0.3151$) and lower clause copying ($\kappa_2 = 0.4913$ vs.\ $0.5748$). In our item-by-item exploratory audit (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}), $108/112$ multi-threshold records match the formal verifier (the $4$ discrepancies are surface wording edge cases on \texttt{law\_work\_injury} where expressions like ``下班顺路买菜途中被车撞伤（非本人主要责任）'' or ``超过法定30天但未满1年'' were conservatively left unparsed by the literal regex).
  \item \textbf{Small-Sample Statistical Scope}: On $N=16$ paired seeds, the $+25.0$ percentage-point decision-preservation gain of \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($12/16$) over \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} ($8/16$) corresponds to $4$ discordant wins (\texttt{cross-002}, \texttt{cross-003}, \texttt{cross-004}, \texttt{cross-009}) and $0$ losses (exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.125$; vs.\ \texttt{condition\_prompt} at $7/16$, $5$ wins and $0$ losses, $p=0.0625$). Because an exact two-sided binomial test at $\alpha=0.05$ requires at least $6$ discordant wins with $0$ losses, $N=16$ is underpowered for standalone hypothesis testing; we report this comparison as a controlled mechanism diagnostic showing how symbolic slot localization corrects threshold-overwriting errors that unguided self-refinement misses.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion, Practical Recommendations, and Limitations}
\paragraph{Practical Recommendations for Rule-Governed RAG Evaluation.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Do not treat retrieval Top-1 or surface fluency as evidence of label validity}: Across both official insurance contracts and multi-threshold rules, $75\%\text{--}100\%$ Top-1 retrieval routinely coexists with $55\%\text{--}100\%$ rates of decision ambiguity or condition mutation under unconstrained conversational rewriting.
  \item \textbf{Separate slot completeness from formal decision determinacy}: Compiling target rules into a threshold-partitioned reachability verifier distinguishes harmless omissions of non-pivotal background slots from pivotal omissions that require clarification, while catching numeric threshold overwrites on small generators.
  \item \textbf{Audit source subsampling and query-to-source overlap in corpus retrieval benchmarks}: As detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}, when evaluating retrievers on community QA corpora, pair synthetic queries with source-matched real queries and check sensitivity to query-to-source bigram overlap $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, our formal verifiers cover hand-compiled waiting-period rules over nine official Chinese insurance policies ($5$ development, $4$ held-out, $36$ seeds / $144$ controlled cases in total) and four researcher-authored multi-threshold clauses ($16$ seeds / $64$ controlled cases). While this demonstrates that explicit rule verification catches condition drift that retrieval metrics miss, we do not claim automated rule compilation from arbitrary unstructured contracts, nor is $N=16$ per testbed sufficient for small-effect statistical power (exact McNemar $p=0.125$ and $p=0.0625$). Second, all generators belong to the Qwen family (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, \texttt{Qwen-Max}) evaluated off-the-shelf without weight fine-tuning. Third, automated regex slot extraction is a conservative proxy that can miss colloquial paraphrases; while we release blinded two-rater HTML forms (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html}, \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}) and a complete 264-item author-inspected and Qwen-assisted exploratory semantic audit (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}, showing $97.3\%$ [$257/264$] agreement with the formal verifier), independent multi-rater human annotation remains future work. Finally, the community QA corpus in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval} (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}) carries noisy community best-answer labels, unverified redistribution licensing, and potential pretraining exposure.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Full-Test Dense Lead Confirmed Under Zero-Truncation MaxP}: Across all $2{,}994$ held-out real queries, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} beats \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$, $\Delta = +0.0187$, 95\% CI $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$).   \item \textbf{Source-Subsample Confounding vs.\ Paired Difference-in-Differences}: Restricting real test queries to the sampled synthetic source documents ($n=209$ for \texttt{1.5B}; $n=153$ for \texttt{Qwen-Plus}) already shifts the real-query point estimate toward \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($\Delta_{\text{BGE}-\text{RRF}} = -0.0279$ and $-0.0312$, CIs spanning zero). While \texttt{zero\_shot} synthesis raises query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ ($0.3253 \rightarrow 0.4775$ on \texttt{1.5B}; $0.3167 \rightarrow 0.4638$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}) and boosts \texttt{bm25\_char2} ($+0.0925$ and $+0.2457$), the paired difference-in-differences $\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}} = (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}}-\text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{syn}} - (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}}-\text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{real}}$ has 95\% CIs spanning zero across all four settings under both query-weighted and document-clustered bootstrap resampling ($+0.0123$, $-0.0259$, $-0.0020$, and $-0.0298$). Thus, unstratified source subsampling is a primary confounder alongside lexical overlap inflation.
  \item \textbf{Negative Marginal Reweighting vs.\ Post-Hoc Overlap Gating and Selection Abstention}: Table~\ref{tab:abstention} shows that marginal TF-IDF propensity weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_density\_weighted}) fails to pick \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} ($100\%$ error across $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$) because hybrid-vs.-dense advantage depends on relational query-to-source overlap $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} leads on $\kappa_2 < 0.25$ across real and all four synthetic sets, whereas \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} leads on $\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$). Post-hoc filtering of synthetic queries to $\kappa_2(q', d^*) \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$ (or quantile cutoffs $q \in [0.10, 0.30]$ at $B=0$) recovers \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}, while paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention on \texttt{real\_only} reduces confident wrong commitments at $B=25$ from $60\%$ to $0\%$ ($80\%$ abstention rate, mean candidate set size $2.60$).
\end{enumerate}
