\begin{abstract}
When cold-starting a domain-specific retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system before user query logs exist, practitioners often synthesize queries from knowledge-base passages to rank candidate retrievers. This paper examines two practical questions in Chinese insurance retrieval: (1)~when does ranking retrievers on synthetic queries reverse the winner observed on real user questions, and (2)~to what extent do format-valid synthetic rewrites drop or alter underwriting and claims eligibility conditions across model scales?

We study these questions in three rounds. In \textbf{Round~1}, we deduplicate $3{,}746$ Chinese insurance answers and partition QA pairs into $3{,}725$ connected components via Union-Find so that no shared answer spans calibration ($750$ queries) and test ($2{,}994$ queries) splits. Evaluating six lexical retrievers, we show that a source-prefix diagnostic proxy selects character $3\text{--}5$-gram TF-IDF ($\text{MRR@10}=0.9330$) over character-bigram BM25 ($0.9311$), whereas real test queries favor bigram BM25 ($0.3817$ vs.\ $0.2727$, selection regret $0.1090$). In \textbf{Round~2}, we add dense (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) and hybrid (\texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}) retrievers and generate zero-shot and 3-shot synthetic queries from held-out answers using both a local 1.5B model (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, $N=249$ common valid sources) and a cloud production model (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}, $N=179$). On the full held-out real test set ($N=2{,}994$), pure dense retrieval ranks first ($\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$ for hybrid RRF; paired difference $+0.0187$, 10,000-resample 95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$; a full-document sliding-window MaxP check across all $509$ long answers with $762$ overflow tail chunks and zero truncated documents confirms $+0.0158$, 95\% CI $[+0.0049, +0.0267]$). On the uncalibrated $249$- and $179$-source synthetic benchmarks, the top-1 ranking reverses to \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} across both models and prompting modes (statistically significant on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} at $+0.0445$, 95\% CI $[+0.0093, +0.0806]$, and point-estimate reversals with CIs spanning zero on the remaining three sets). Decomposing this reversal via source-matched real test subsets ($n=209$ and $n=153$) shows that on the matched sources, real queries already slightly favor RRF in point estimate ($\Delta_{\text{BGE}-\text{RRF}} = -0.0279$ and $-0.0312$, CIs spanning zero), and while \texttt{zero\_shot} synthesis significantly raises query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2$ from $0.32\text{--}0.33$ to $0.47\text{--}0.48$ and boosts \texttt{bm25\_char2} by $+0.0925$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0227, +0.1633]$) and $+0.2457$ ($[+0.1695, +0.3216]$) MRR@10 on those paired queries, the paired difference-in-differences in the RRF-over-BGE gap ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}} = +0.0123$, CI $[-0.0375, +0.0649]$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}; $-0.0020$, CI $[-0.0479, +0.0438]$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}) spans zero across all four settings, meaning within-source phrasing effects on the relative RRF$-$BGE gap cannot be statistically separated from source-subsampling variance at current sample sizes. As an exploratory post-hoc diagnostic, \textbf{Relational Source-Overlap Gating} (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}, retaining low-$\kappa_2$ synthetic queries) recovers \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} for quantile cutoffs $q \in [0.10, 0.30]$ and across calibrated budgets $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$, while paired-bootstrap \textbf{Candidate-Set Abstention} on small real calibration sets reduces confident wrong commitments at $B=25$ to $0\%$ ($80\%$ abstention, $100\%$ optimal coverage, mean set size $2.60$ of $8$; $80\%$ coverage at $B=50$ and $100\%$ at $B \ge 100$). In \textbf{Round~3}, we build $80$ controlled clause-grounded cases with double-witness counterexamples from five official insurance policy wordings and evaluate $120$ paired rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, explicitly separating \emph{all-slot match rate} from reference-independent \emph{formal decision determinacy} across complete threshold-partitioned equivalence regions of omitted slots. Under unconstrained conversational rewriting (\texttt{ordinary\_style}), all-slot match is only $30\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $0\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $20\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) (standalone decision determinacy $30\%, 0\%, 45\%$), whereas explicit slot constraints (\texttt{condition\_prompt}) raise all-slot match to $95\%\text{--}100\%$ and decision determinacy to $95\%\text{--}100\%$. Finally, in a $64$-case \textbf{synthetic multi-threshold rule-compiler stress test} inspired by healthcare and labor-law structures ($48$ method records from $42$ local 1.5B generation calls), \texttt{ordinary\_style} preserves all required slots in only $6.2\%$ ($1/16$) of seeds, while pairing \texttt{condition\_prompt} with a 1-step parser-guided \textbf{slot-guard repair} (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) lifts automatic all-slot match to $68.8\%$ ($11/16$, decision determinacy $75.0\%$ [$12/16$]) and reduces parser-detected threshold mutations from $31.2\%$ to $0.0\%$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Selecting a retriever for a vertical-domain RAG system---for instance, choosing among character $n$-gram BM25, dense bi-encoders, or Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)---often happens before real user traffic is available. A common workflow in both retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation frameworks \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas,zhu2025rageval} is to generate synthetic questions from corpus passages using an instruction-tuned LLM, score candidate retrievers on the synthetic set, and deploy the top-ranked system.

While prior work has studied macro-level rank correlations between synthetic and real test collections \cite{rahmani2024syndl,vanelburg2025synthetic}, two specific failure modes matter when making concrete deployment decisions in insurance retrieval:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Dense vs.\ Hybrid Selection Reversal}: Evaluating on a small synthetic query set generated from sampled passages can reverse the winner relative to a full deployment test distribution through two factors: (a)~\emph{source-sampling variance} when only a subset of corpus documents is sampled for generation, and (b)~\emph{passage-conditioned lexical copy inflation}, where the LLM reuses domain terms from the source passage, significantly inflating BM25 scores on the sampled sources (while paired difference-in-differences tests show that at $n=209/153$, the change in the relative RRF$-$BGE gap has 95\% CIs spanning zero and cannot be statistically separated from source-sampling variance).
  \item \textbf{Loss of Underwriting and Claims Conditions}: In insurance clauses, coverage outcomes (\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} vs.\ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) depend on specific variables: product identity, first-time vs.\ renewal enrollment, policy effective day, sickness vs.\ accidental injury cause, plan-specific waiting period (e.g., 7 vs.\ 25 days), and insurer waiver approval. A rewrite that satisfies surface format filters can still drop or alter one of these variables, either omitting a non-pivotal slot (leaving the formal decision determined) or omitting/mutating a pivotal slot (rendering the question genuinely ambiguous or flipping its coverage outcome).
\end{enumerate}

This paper reports a three-round empirical and methodological study on Chinese insurance retrieval, pairing diagnostic failure analysis with falsifiable mitigations and explicit statistical/provenance boundaries. To avoid terminology ambiguity, we distinguish two levels of abstention studied in this work: \textbf{retriever-selection candidate-set abstention} (Round~2, where an evaluation pipeline returns a confidence set of candidate retrievers rather than committing to a single noisy winner under small calibration budgets) and \textbf{query-level clarification abstention (\texttt{CLARIFY})} (Round~3, where a question missing a pivotal underwriting slot cannot be decided without user clarification, certified via two counterexample completions). We also strictly separate \textbf{automatic required-slot match} from \textbf{reference-independent formal decision determinacy} (computed by enumerating complete threshold-partitioned equivalence regions of any omitted slots without reading reference seed metadata). Specifically:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Round~1 (Group-Disjoint Lexical Baseline)}: On $3{,}746$ deduplicated answers from a public Chinese insurance QA corpus partitioned into $3{,}725$ connected groups ($750$ calibration, $2{,}994$ held-out test queries), we compare six lexical retrievers against extractive keyword and prefix-copy proxies, quantifying how long-$n$-gram copy bias selects \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} over the true lexical winner \texttt{bm25\_char2} (regret $0.1090$ MRR@10).
  \item \textbf{Round~2 (Source-Matched Confounding Decomposition, Source-Overlap Gating, and Candidate-Set Abstention)}: Expanding to eight systems (six lexical, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}, and \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}), we evaluate zero-shot and 3-shot queries generated by local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} ($N=249$ common valid sources) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($N=179$). Relative to the full real test set ($N=2{,}994$, where \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} beats \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} at $0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$, 95\% CI $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$, and $0.5165$ vs.\ $0.5007$ under zero-truncation \texttt{Full-MaxP}), all four uncalibrated synthetic benchmarks rank \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} first in point estimate. By aligning synthetic queries with real test queries targeting the exact same documents ($n=209$ for \texttt{1.5B}, $n=153$ for \texttt{Qwen-Plus}), we show that while \texttt{zero\_shot} significantly increases $\kappa_2$ ($0.32\text{--}0.33 \rightarrow 0.47\text{--}0.48$) and \texttt{bm25\_char2} MRR@10 ($+0.0925$ and $+0.2457$), the paired change in RRF-over-BGE advantage ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$) has 95\% CIs spanning zero across all four settings ($+0.0123, -0.0259, -0.0020, -0.0298$), showing that source-subsample selection variance is a major confounder alongside lexical overlap. We then evaluate \textbf{relational source-overlap gating} (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}) across a full quantile sweep ($Q_{0.10}\text{--}Q_{1.00}$) and matched-size random/length baselines, alongside paired-bootstrap \textbf{candidate-set abstention} across budgets $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$.
  \item \textbf{Round~3 (Double-Witness Counterexamples, Slot Match vs.\ Decision Determinacy, and Synthetic Cross-Domain Stress Test)}: Using five official Chinese insurance policy clauses (verified by local PDF SHA256 hashes), we formalize a waiting-period rule compiler and construct $20$ seed scenarios ($80$ controlled variants where every \texttt{missing\_critical} \texttt{CLARIFY} case carries two opposite-outcome completion witnesses) alongside $120$ paired LLM rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}. We separate numeric value mutations ($15\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} vs.\ $0/20$ observed on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}/\texttt{Qwen-Max} in this 20-seed sample) from pragmatic slot omission under ordinary conversational prompting, and report both automatic slot match and reference-independent decision determinacy. Finally, we construct a $16$-seed ($64$-case) \textbf{synthetic multi-threshold rule-compiler stress test} inspired by healthcare and labor-law structures ($48$ method records from $42$ local 1.5B calls), evaluating how a 1-step parser-guided \textbf{slot-guard repair} loop (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) improves slot retention and repairs threshold-overwriting errors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Synthetic Queries in IR Evaluation.}
Promptagator \cite{dai2023promptagator} and InPars-v2 \cite{jeronymo2023inparsv2} use LLM-generated queries to train retrievers when in-domain labels are scarce. Evaluation frameworks such as ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}, RAGAs \cite{es2024ragas}, and RAGEval \cite{zhu2025rageval} also use synthetic queries to score RAG pipelines. Recent analyses by Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and van Elburg et al.\ \cite{vanelburg2025synthetic} note that LLM-generated evaluation sets carry generator-specific biases. We focus on decomposing source-sampling variance from passage-conditioned lexical overlap inflation when ranking dense vs.\ hybrid retrievers, and test both 1.5B and commercial cloud generators.

\paragraph{Calibration and Abstention under Small Budgets.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} combine synthetic predictions with small gold samples to construct confidence intervals on paired data. In cold-start retriever selection, however, synthetic queries are generated from a subset of documents without 1-to-1 paired real user questions across the full test corpus. We evaluate unpaired shrinkage and surface-density weighting as negative baselines, and formulate a bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule that withholds point commitment when calibration uncertainty spans multiple systems.

\paragraph{Insurance QA Benchmarks.}
InsuranceQA \cite{feng2020insuranceqa} and its Chinese adaptations provide community question-answering pairs for answer selection. Because community answers contain duplicates and lack formal underwriting parameters, we augment our Round~1--2 FAQ experiments with a Round~3 benchmark built directly from five official Chinese insurance policy wordings with deterministic waiting-period rules.

\section{Round 1: Group-Disjoint Split and Lexical Proxy Diagnostics}

\subsection{Leakage-Free Group Split}
Public Chinese insurance FAQ corpora (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}, derived from insuranceQA-zh) contain repeated questions and shared boilerplate answers. Splitting QA pairs at random places identical answer texts in both calibration and test sets.

After normalizing whitespace and deduplicating exact answer strings, we obtain $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid QA pairs. We build an undirected graph linking any pairs that share normalized questions, normalized answers, or near-duplicate question prefixes, yielding $3{,}725$ connected components via Union-Find. Assigning whole components to splits (seed \texttt{20261025}) produces:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Real Calibration Pool}: $750$ queries across $745$ components (used for few-shot demonstrations and small-budget calibration sampling).
  \item \textbf{Real Test Set}: $2{,}994$ queries across $2{,}980$ components (zero component overlap with calibration).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lexical Systems and Deterministic Proxies}
In Round~1, we index all $3{,}746$ answers under six character $n$-gram lexical retrievers: sublinear TF-IDF cosine similarity over character unigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1}), bigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char2}), $1\text{--}3$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1\_3}), and $3\text{--}5$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5}), alongside Okapi BM25 ($k_1=1.2, b=0.75$) over character unigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char1}) and bigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char2}). Each system is scored by $\text{MRR@10}$ and $\text{Hit@5}$ (recall at rank~5).

We compare held-out real test queries against two deterministic non-LLM proxies constructed from the target answers: (1)~\texttt{synthetic\_keywords}, which concatenates top corpus-level TF-IDF terms from the answer into a question template, and (2)~\texttt{source\_prefix\_diagnostic}, a stress-test proxy that turns the opening clause of the answer into a question.

\subsection{Round 1 Results}
Table~\ref{tab:round1} and Figure~\ref{fig:round1} show that on held-out real queries, \texttt{bm25\_char2} ranks first ($\text{MRR@10}=0.3817, \text{Hit@5}=0.4826$), whereas \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} ranks fourth ($\text{MRR@10}=0.2727, \text{Hit@5}=0.3557$) because real user questions rarely share contiguous $3\text{--}5$-character spans with answer texts. On \texttt{source\_prefix\_diagnostic}, contiguous substring copying pushes \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} to first place ($0.9330$ vs.\ $0.9311$ for \texttt{bm25\_char2}), causing a true-test selection regret of $0.3817 - 0.2727 = 0.1090$.

In budget sensitivity trials ($5$ random draws per budget $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$ from the calibration pool), selecting the winner directly on real calibration samples (\texttt{real\_only}) picks \texttt{bm25\_char2} in $80\%$ ($4/5$) of draws at $B=25$ (mean regret $0.0126$, Kendall's $\tau_b = 0.4133$) and $100\%$ ($5/5$) of draws at $B \ge 50$ (zero regret, with $\tau_b$ increasing from $0.6267$ at $B=50$ to $0.8933$ at $B=100$ and $0.9200$ at $B=200$).

\section{Round 2: LLM Synthetic Queries, Source-Sampling Confounding, and Abstention}

\subsection{Setup and Shared Valid Source Sets}
To test how LLM query generators behave when dense and hybrid retrievers are included, we expand the candidate pool to eight systems by adding:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}: Dense retrieval with \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} (512-dim normalized embeddings; 512-token limit, which truncates $509$ of $3{,}746$ long answers, while $2$ of $256$ source answers exceed $512$ tokens during LLM prompt construction).
  \item \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}: Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k=60$, depth $100$) combining \texttt{bm25\_char2} (requiring positive lexical score) and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}.
\end{itemize}

We sample $256$ target answer passages and generate synthetic questions under two prompting modes (\texttt{zero\_shot} and \texttt{few\_shot\_3}, where the 3 style demonstrations are fixed real questions drawn from the calibration pool; test questions are never exposed). We run two generators:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (local MPS, \texttt{float32/eager}, greedy decoding, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=64): out of $256$ sources, $255$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $250$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) pass format validation (single Chinese question ending in a question mark, length $6\text{--}160$ chars, no PII), leaving \textbf{$N=249$ common valid sources} for paired evaluation.\footnote{An initial \texttt{float16+sdpa} run on MPS produced numerical corruption and $0$ valid outputs; we logged that failed run in \texttt{failed\_float16\_run.json} and switched to \texttt{float32+eager}.}
  \item \texttt{Qwen-Plus} (cloud API, \texttt{temperature}=0, \texttt{max\_tokens}=64): $243/256$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $184/256$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) pass the same single-question filter, yielding \textbf{$N=179$ common valid sources}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decomposing Selection Reversal: Source-Sampling Confounding vs.\ Within-Source Lexical Inflation}
Table~\ref{tab:round2} and Figure~\ref{fig:round2} reveal that comparing an uncalibrated synthetic benchmark ($N=249$ or $179$ sampled sources) against the full real test distribution ($N=2{,}994$) conflates two distinct effects, which we explicitly separate:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Full-Test Deployment Winner and Full-Document MaxP Check}: Across all $2{,}994$ held-out real questions, pure dense retrieval (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6453$), beating \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.5030$, $\text{Hit@5}=0.6075$) by $+0.0187$ (10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$). To verify that this dense-over-hybrid ranking is not an artifact of 512-token truncation on the $509$ long documents, \texttt{src/maxp\_ablation.py} runs a full-document sliding-window ablation (\texttt{Full-MaxP}), splitting all $509$ long answers into $512$-token windows with a $256$-token stride ($762$ additional non-redundant overflow tail chunks, $1{,}271$ total windows across the $509$ long answers, $0$ truncated documents; retokenizing each decoded tail string confirms $\le 510$ content tokens and $536/762$ exact token-ID matches) and scoring each document by maximum passage cosine similarity $\max_{c \in d} \cos(\mathbf{e}_q, \mathbf{e}_c)$. Under \texttt{Full-MaxP}, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5165$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6389$) vs.\ $0.5007$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6055$) for \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($\Delta = +0.0158$, 95\% CI $[+0.0049, +0.0267]$), confirming that pure dense retrieval remains the full-test winner with zero document truncation.
  \item \textbf{Source-Subsample Selection Confounding}: Because the $256$ generation sources were sampled from the document pool rather than paired with all $2{,}994$ test queries, restricting \texttt{real\_test} to queries whose gold answers fall in the valid synthetic source subsets ($n=209$ real queries across $207$ unique docs for \texttt{1.5B}; $n=153$ real queries across $151$ unique docs for \texttt{Qwen-Plus}) shifts the real-query point estimate to favor \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.4975$ vs.\ $0.4696$, $\Delta_{\text{BGE}-\text{RRF}} = -0.0279$, 95\% CI $[-0.0645, +0.0096]$ on \texttt{1.5B}-matched; $0.4980$ vs.\ $0.4668$, $\Delta = -0.0312$, 95\% CI $[-0.0687, +0.0073]$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}-matched). Although both matched-subset CIs span zero, this demonstrates that small unstratified document subsamples introduce substantial source-selection variance before any query synthesis occurs.
  \item \textbf{Within-Source Lexical Copy Inflation and Paired Difference-in-Differences ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$)}: Controlling for source composition by pairing each real query with its corresponding synthetic query \emph{on the exact same target document} ($n=209$ and $n=153$) isolates the within-source query-style effect. On the $209$ \texttt{1.5B}-matched pairs, \texttt{zero\_shot} generation increases query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ from $0.3253$ (real) to $0.4775$ ($0.4789$ across the $207$ unique docs), boosting \texttt{bm25\_char2} from $0.4073$ to $0.4998$ ($0.5036$ on the $207$ unique docs; paired gain $+0.0925$, 95\% CI $[+0.0227, +0.1633]$), while on the $153$ \texttt{Qwen-Plus}-matched pairs, \texttt{zero\_shot} raises $\kappa_2$ from $0.3167$ to $0.4638$ ($0.4651$ on $151$ unique docs) and jumps \texttt{bm25\_char2} from $0.3909$ to $0.6366$ ($0.6450$ on $151$ unique docs; paired gain $+0.2457$, 95\% CI $[+0.1695, +0.3216]$). However, when we test the paired difference-in-differences in RRF's advantage over BGE,
  \[
    \Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}} = (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}} - \text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{syn}} - (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}} - \text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{real}},
  \]
  all four 10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95\% CIs span zero under both query-equal-weighted resampling and source-document-clustered resampling (which averages per unique target document before resampling documents with equal weight): query-weighted $+0.0123$ (CI $[-0.0375, +0.0649]$; document-equal-weighted mean $+0.0185$, clustered CI $[-0.0314, +0.0698]$) on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, query-weighted $-0.0259$ (CI $[-0.0736, +0.0217]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0225$, clustered CI $[-0.0710, +0.0258]$) on \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3}, query-weighted $-0.0020$ (CI $[-0.0479, +0.0438]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0036$, clustered CI $[-0.0474, +0.0421]$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}, and query-weighted $-0.0298$ (CI $[-0.0791, +0.0204]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0320$, clustered CI $[-0.0813, +0.0187]$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3}. Thus, while \texttt{zero\_shot} synthesis demonstrably inflates $\kappa_2$ and \texttt{bm25\_char2}, the current paired sample sizes ($n=209/153$) do not show a statistically significant within-source amplification of the RRF$-$BGE gap beyond source-sampling shift. Providing 3 calibration examples (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) reduces $\kappa_2$ to $0.3180$ (\texttt{1.5B}) and $0.4108$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), though on \texttt{1.5B} a 20-source audit (\texttt{semantic\_audit.json}) shows that mimicking unrelated examples reduces source-supported questions from $12/20$ to $9/20$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Marginal Reweighting Fails vs.\ Exploratory Source-Overlap Gating and Candidate-Set Abstention}
In \texttt{validate\_v2.py}, a 2-fold cross-fit logistic domain classifier trained on character $1\text{--}3$-gram TF-IDF features separates real calibration queries from synthetic queries with $\text{ROC-AUC}=0.8120$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $0.7733$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) at $B=25$ (rising to $0.8765$ and $0.9148$ at $B=200$). Yet across $5$ repeated draws at each budget $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$, propensity density weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_density\_weighted}) selects the full-test winner \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in \textbf{$0\%$ of trials}, and length weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_length\_weighted}) reaches $0\%$ on \texttt{zero\_shot} ($0\%\text{--}20\%$ on \texttt{few\_shot\_3}).

Why does marginal query-feature reweighting fail? Because lexical advantage between \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} and \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} is governed by the \emph{relational} overlap $\kappa_2(q, d^*) = |\text{Bi}(q) \cap \text{Bi}(d^*)| / |\text{Bi}(q)|$ between a query and its target document $d^*$ rather than marginal query text $P(q)$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Low-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 < 0.25$, $40.0\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=1{,}199$)}: \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} outperforms \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} on \texttt{real\_test} ($0.3022$ vs.\ $0.2220$, $\Delta = +0.0801$) \emph{and on all four LLM synthetic sets}: \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} ($+0.0839$, $n=29$), \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3} ($+0.0326$, $n=115$), \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot} ($+0.0821$, $n=13$), and \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3} ($+0.1600$, $n=14$).
  \item \textbf{High-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$, $20.6\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=617$)}: \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} outperforms \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} on \texttt{real\_test} ($\Delta = -0.0457$) and on all four synthetic sets ($\Delta \in [-0.0811, -0.0447]$). Unweighted \texttt{zero\_shot} evaluation over-weights this stratum ($48.6\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} and $41.9\%$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, vs.\ $20.6\%$ on \texttt{real\_test}).
\end{itemize}

We therefore evaluate two diagnostic and decision-theoretic procedures (noting explicitly that \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} was formulated post-hoc after inspecting the Round~2 overlap strata and should be validated on independent corpora before deployment as a standalone cold-start rule):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Exploratory Relational Source-Overlap Gating (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}) and Quantile Sensitivity}: Filtering synthetic queries to retain those with $\kappa_2(q', d^*) \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$ (where $\hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)} = \text{median}_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}} \kappa_2(q, d^*) \approx 0.292$, falling back to $Q_{0.20}(\kappa_2)$ if fewer than $5$ synthetic queries satisfy $\kappa_2 \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$) selects \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in $5/5$ calibration draws across $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$ on both \texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus}.\footnote{On deterministic \texttt{keywords\_matched}, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} still fails ($100\%$ wrong selection) because keyword concatenation has zero support in $\kappa_2 < 0.25$.} To test sensitivity to the quantile cutoff in the uncalibrated cold-start setting ($B=0$, a single deterministic evaluation per synthetic set), \texttt{src/maxp\_ablation.py} sweeps $q \in [0.10, 1.00]$: all four synthetic sets select \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} for $q \in \{0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30\}$ (and up to $q=0.40$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, $q=0.60$ on \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3}, and $q=0.80$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3}), whereas relaxing the cutoff to $q \ge 0.40$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot} ($\Delta = -0.0033$) or $q \ge 0.50$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} ($\Delta = -0.0099$) reverts to \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}. At the same subset size $k=Q_{0.20}$ ($k=70$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, $k=36$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}), 1,000 random subsamples select \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in only $5.9\%$ and $18.2\%$ of draws, confirming that gating depends on $\kappa_2$ rather than sample-size reduction.
  \item \textbf{Retriever-Selection Candidate-Set Abstention}: When evaluating directly on a small real calibration sample (\texttt{real\_only}) of size $B$ with empirical leader $\hat{s}$, we run $200$ paired bootstrap resamples over the $B$ queries and retain all systems $s$ whose 95\% upper bound on $(\text{MRR}_s - \text{MRR}_{\hat{s}})$ is $\ge 0$. When $|\mathcal{C}_B| > 1$, the selector abstains from point commitment and returns $\mathcal{C}_B$.
\end{enumerate}

Each structured scenario frame specifies up to six required fields: \texttt{policy\_id} (required in $20/20$ seeds), \texttt{enrollment} (\texttt{first} vs.\ \texttt{new}, $20/20$), \texttt{policy\_day} ($20/20$), \texttt{cause} (\texttt{illness} vs.\ \texttt{accident}, $20/20$), \texttt{plan\_wait\_days} (required in $4/20$ seeds on \texttt{cpic\_86017}), and \texttt{approved\_waiver} (required in $3/20$ renewal seeds). From $20$ seed frames, we generate $80$ controlled test cases across four variants: \texttt{complete} (all required fields stated), \texttt{surface} (synonymous wording), \texttt{missing\_critical} (one pivotal condition removed, certified as \texttt{CLARIFY} via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses), and \texttt{counterfactual} (one field modified to flip the rule outcome between \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} and \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}).

Crucially, because a seed scenario can include non-pivotal slots (for example, on \texttt{pa\_h865} with a 30-day waiting period, an illness occurring on Day~45 already satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting \texttt{cause} still yields \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED} under both \texttt{illness} and \texttt{accident}), \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py} separates two distinct evaluation metrics:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{All Required Slots Matched (\texttt{surface\_v2\_preserved})}: Requires every field in \texttt{required\_fields} to be extracted without omission or mutation.
  \item \textbf{Reference-Independent Formal Decision Determinacy (\texttt{enumerate\_admissible\_decisions})}: Enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unobserved slots \emph{using only the extracted question observations and public policy rules} (never reading ground-truth \texttt{seed\_frame} metadata), partitioning day domains into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t, = t, > t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ via representative values $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$) and checking reachability and slot mutations across all six schema fields (\texttt{ALL\_INSURANCE\_FIELDS}, including optional waiver slots not in a seed's minimal \texttt{required\_fields}): a rewrite is scored as \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} iff no observed schema field is mutated and the reachable decision set is the singleton $[\text{decision}(f^*)]$, as \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP} / \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} if any schema field is mutated or the unique reachable decision flips, and as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} when $|\text{reachable}| \neq 1$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key Findings on Condition Drift}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} highlight four empirical observations:

\paragraph{1. Surface Format Checks Miss Both Multi-Question Outputs and Slot Omissions.}
All $120/120$ generated questions pass the lenient single-line \texttt{format\_valid} check (though $7/40$ local \texttt{1.5B} outputs contain multiple question marks in a single line, compared to $0/40$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}). Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, $70\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $100\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $80\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) fail automatic all-slot matching, and $70\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $100\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $55\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) fail standalone decision determinacy even after enumerating all admissible completions of non-pivotal slots.

\paragraph{2. Value Mutation on \texttt{1.5B} vs.\ Persistent Conversational Omission Across Tested Scales.}
Comparing the three tested model tiers separates two failure types:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Value Mutation and Label Collision on \texttt{1.5B}}: In $3/20$ seeds (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016} on \texttt{cpic\_82859}), \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{ordinary\_style} mutates ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2). Furthermore, on \texttt{condition-009} (7-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) and \texttt{condition-010} (25-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}), \texttt{1.5B} drops both the product name and the plan waiting period, generating the exact same text for two opposite ground-truth labels:
  \begin{quote}
  \small
  ``我是在网上看病花了钱，是第一次买这个保险，保单生效第10天了，因为不是意外病，想问一下这属于等待期吗？''
  \end{quote}
  \item \textbf{Conversational Omission on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}}: Moving to \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} reduces observed numeric value mutations on this 20-seed sample to $0\%$ ($0/20$; as a finite sample observation on $n=20$ seeds, this does not imply a zero population error rate). However, under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, both cloud models shorten administrative details to sound conversational: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the product title in $20/20$ ($100\%$) of seeds, while \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops product titles in $10/20$ ($50\%$) and rewrites ``首次投保'' (first-time enrollment) into ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which is ambiguous between first enrollment and renewal) in $6/20$ ($30\%$) of seeds. When \texttt{Qwen-Max} retains \texttt{policy\_id} ($10/20$ seeds), $5$ of those seeds omit \texttt{enrollment} on non-waivable policies or post-waiting-period days where the decision remains uniquely determined (\texttt{NON\_PIVOTAL\_SLOT\_OMITTED\_DECISION\_PRESERVED}), yielding $4/20$ ($20\%$) all-slot match vs.\ $9/20$ ($45\%$) standalone decision determinacy.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Slot-Constrained Prompting Across Tested Scales.}
Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, automatic all-slot match (\texttt{v2\_synonym\_aware}) reaches $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}. Moreover, on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} \texttt{condition\_prompt}, the single seed with an omitted \texttt{cause} slot occurs on a non-pivotal scenario where both illness and accident completions yield the same waiting-period outcome, so completion-enumerated standalone decision determinacy reaches $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on both \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} ($100\%$ across all three models when source \texttt{policy\_id} is granted).

\paragraph{4. Strict Regex False Negatives and Blinded Review Pack.}
On \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{condition\_prompt}, \texttt{v1\_strict\_regex} flags $10/20$ questions even though $9$ of those $10$ retain all conditions under synonymous phrasing (e.g., ``生效是第20天'', ``同意免去等待期''). Because automated slot parsers remain proxy evaluators rather than ground-truth semantic judges, we package all $80$ controlled cases and $40$ local generations into randomized, blinded two-rater HTML forms (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, scored by \texttt{score\_annotations.py}); human annotation results are not claimed in this manuscript prior to independent completion.

\section{Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rule-Compiler Stress Test (Healthcare and Labor-Law Inspired)}
To test whether our double-witness verification compiler and closed-loop slot repair mechanics operate on multi-threshold rule structures beyond insurance waiting periods, we construct a \textbf{synthetic rule-compiler stress test} in \texttt{src/cross\_domain\_study.py} (\texttt{data\_v3/cross\_domain\_controlled\_cases.jsonl} and \texttt{cross\_domain\_summary.json}). We emphasize provenance boundaries explicitly: unlike the five SHA256-verified official insurance policy PDFs in Round~3, these four rules are \textbf{researcher-authored synthetic composite clauses} inspired by clinical pathway and PRC labor-law structures (designed for controlled formal rule testing rather than clinical or legal compliance benchmarking):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare-Inspired Synthetic Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (a composite EGFR-TKI prior-authorization rule combining T790M mutation status, a 14-day second-line washout interval, and specialist waiver override); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (an orthopedic post-op VTE prophylaxis pathway contrasting a 10-day knee replacement window vs.\ a 35-day hip replacement window, with active bleeding contraindication and hematology consult waiver).
  \item \textbf{Labor-Law-Inspired Synthetic Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (inspired by PRC Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14(6) on non-principal commuting traffic fault and Art.~17 contrasting the 30-day employer filing window vs.\ the 1-year/365-day employee/kin window and administrative extension); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (inspired by PRC Labor Contract Law Art.~24 on confidential personnel and SPC Labor Dispute Judicial Interpretation~I Art.~38 on 3-month/90-day compensation default, augmented with a synthetic grace-period waiver clause).
\end{itemize}

Every seed scenario is compiled into four controlled variants (\texttt{complete}, \texttt{surface}, \texttt{missing\_critical} certified as \texttt{CLARIFY} via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses, and \texttt{counterfactual} flipping \texttt{RULE\_ELIGIBLE} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{RULE\_EXCLUDED}). In \texttt{enumerate\_cross\_decisions}, unobserved duration slots are expanded across a complete threshold-equivalence partition ($\{7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 30, 32, 35, 45, 90, 120, 365, 400\}$, covering $< t, = t, > t$ across all rule thresholds $\{10, 14, 30, 35, 90, 365\}$), and reachability is evaluated strictly from the extracted question slots and public clause schema without reading ground-truth \texttt{seed\_frame} metadata (with negated waivers such as ``尚未获…特批'' or ``未批准延长申请时限'' parsed as \texttt{False}, explicit waiver uncertainty parsed as \texttt{unknown} $\rightarrow \{\texttt{False}, \texttt{True}\}$, and unmentioned extraordinary waivers defaulting to $\{\texttt{False}\}$). In \texttt{evaluate\_cross\_outcome}, mutations are checked across all six schema fields (\texttt{ALL\_CROSS\_FIELDS}, including optional waiver exceptions not in a seed's minimal \texttt{required\_fields}) and reachability is always checked first: the scorer returns \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} iff no schema field is mutated and $\text{reachable} = [\text{cross\_decision}(f^*)]$, returns \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} whenever any schema field is mutated or $|\text{reachable}| = 1$ with $\text{reachable}[0] \neq \text{cross\_decision}(f^*)$ (e.g., if an affirmative waiver is deleted and defaults to \texttt{False}, or if a positive waiver is spuriously added), and returns \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} when $|\text{reachable}| \neq 1$. We run \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32/eager}, greedy decoding) across all $16$ seeds under three protocols ($48$ total method records reconstructed from $42$ actual LLM generation calls via the \texttt{repaired} flags in \texttt{cross\_domain\_generations.jsonl}): (1)~\texttt{ordinary\_style} ($16$ calls), (2)~open-loop \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($16$ calls), and (3)~\textbf{1-step parser-guided \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}} ($10$ repair generation calls triggered by the unified predicate \texttt{should\_trigger\_slot\_guard\_repair(missing, mutated) = bool(missing or mutated)} across both fresh generation and \texttt{--score-only} replay on the $10$ seeds where \texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2} detected a missing required slot or any mutated schema slot in \texttt{condition\_prompt}, including \texttt{cross-001} where non-pivotal \texttt{threshold\_days} was omitted, plus $6$ unrepaired \texttt{condition\_prompt} outputs reused directly). Because \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} is triggered and scored by the same synonym-aware slot parser (\texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2}), we report automatic slot-match and completion-enumerated decision-determinacy metrics as parser-verified proxy rates rather than independent human semantic evaluations.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Practical Recommendations and Limitations}
\paragraph{Practical Recommendations.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Audit both source-sampling coverage and query-to-source overlap ($\kappa_2(q', d^*)$)}: Always pair synthetic benchmarks with source-matched real diagnostics where available, and monitor $\kappa_2(q', d^*)$ via \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} ($q \in [0.10, 0.30]$) rather than relying on unweighted synthetic rankings or marginal query propensity weights.
  \item \textbf{Use small group-disjoint real calibration sets with candidate-set abstention}: When evaluating directly on $25\text{--}50$ real queries, paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention avoids premature point commitment while retaining the true winner in $\mathcal{C}_B$ ($100\%$ coverage at $B=25, 100, 200$ and $80\%$ at $B=50$).
  \item \textbf{Separate required-slot completeness from formal decision determinacy}: Use formal rule compilers and completion enumeration (\texttt{enumerate\_admissible\_decisions}) alongside \texttt{condition\_prompt} and parser-guided \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} to distinguish benign omission of non-pivotal slots from outcome-flipping mutations or ambiguous \texttt{CLARIFY} degradations.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, our Round~1--2 FAQ corpus comes from a single community insurance dataset whose historical answers provide weak relevance labels rather than expert-verified legal advice, and our $249$/$179$ synthetic sets exhibit source-subsampling variance relative to the full $2{,}994$-query test set; furthermore, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} was designed post-hoc on this benchmark and requires prospective validation on unseen corpora. Second, our experiments evaluate models within the Qwen family (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}); testing additional LLM families is needed to assess broader generator diversity. Third, while the five Round~3 insurance clauses are anchored to SHA256-verified official policy PDFs, the four cross-domain clauses in Section~6 are researcher-constructed synthetic stress-test rules, and automatic slot-parser metrics (\texttt{v2\_synonym\_aware} and \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) remain proxy evaluations pending independent two-rater human annotation of the blinded HTML review packs.
