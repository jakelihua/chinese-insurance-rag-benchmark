\begin{abstract}
When cold-starting or benchmarking a domain-specific retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system, practitioners commonly synthesize evaluation queries from source documents using instruction-tuned LLMs. Although prior work shows that synthetic queries can shift macro-level retriever rankings, a more direct failure mode arises in rule-governed vertical domains: \emph{a synthetic query can achieve $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval while omitting or mutating pivotal eligibility conditions, either flipping the ground-truth business decision or turning a determinate scenario into an ambiguous query that requires user clarification}. We study this problem in Chinese insurance through a clause-grounded, decision-preserving verification framework that separates \emph{all-slot completeness} from reference-independent \emph{formal decision determinacy} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}, \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}/\texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP}, and double-witness-certified \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}), alongside retrieval-ranking and source-sampling diagnostics. All language models and dense encoders are evaluated off-the-shelf with frozen weights; no LLM or embedding parameters are pre-trained or fine-tuned.

In our corpus-level retrieval diagnostics, we deduplicate $3{,}746$ answers from a public historical community QA corpus (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}) into $3{,}725$ leakage-free connected components ($750$ calibration, $2{,}994$ held-out test queries). Source-prefix copying reverses lexical rankings ($\text{regret}=0.1090$), and across eight lexical, dense (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}), and hybrid (\texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}) retrievers evaluated on local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} ($N=249$) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($N=179$) synthetic sets, all four uncalibrated synthetic benchmarks rank \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} first in point estimate. On the full real test set ($N=2{,}994$), however, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} outperforms \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$, paired $\Delta=+0.0187$, 95\% CI $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$; confirmed under zero-truncation sliding-window \texttt{Full-MaxP} at $+0.0158$, CI $[+0.0049, +0.0267]$). Pairing real and synthetic queries on the same source documents ($n=209$ and $n=153$) shows that while \texttt{zero\_shot} synthesis raises query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2$ ($0.32\text{--}0.33 \rightarrow 0.47\text{--}0.48$) and boosts \texttt{bm25\_char2} ($+0.0925$ and $+0.2457$), the paired difference-in-differences in the RRF-over-BGE gap ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$) has 95\% CIs spanning zero across all four settings, indicating that source-subsample selection variance is a primary confounder alongside lexical overlap. We also evaluate post-hoc relational source-overlap gating ($q \in [0.10, 0.30]$) and paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention across calibration budgets $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$.

For clause-grounded decision verification, we compile formal waiting-period decision rules over nine official Chinese insurance policy PDFs: five development clauses ($20$ seeds, $80$ controlled cases with double-completion counterexample witnesses, $120$ paired rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}) and four held-out official clauses ($16$ seeds, $64$ controlled cases, evaluated with the frozen development-set parser and verifier without regex changes). On the five development clauses, unconstrained conversational rewriting (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) yields $30\%, 0\%,$ and $45\%$ standalone decision determinacy across \texttt{1.5B}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, whereas explicit slot constraints (\texttt{condition\_prompt}) reach $95\%\text{--}100\%$. On the four unseen held-out clauses, the frozen verifier certifies $100\%$ ($64/64$) of controlled cases and shows that \texttt{ordinary\_style} achieves high template novelty ($1-\kappa_2 = 0.6410$) but $0\%$ ($0/16$) standalone decision determinacy ($31.2\%$ [$5/16$] when granted the source policy title), whereas \texttt{condition\_prompt} and \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} reach $93.8\%$ ($15/16$ automatic frozen-parser match; $16/16$ under manual semantic check) with $0\%$ condition mutations. Masking policy titles and insurer names drops 9-clause Top-1 retrieval from $75.0\%$ to $31.2\%\text{--}56.2\%$ (\texttt{bm25\_char2}) and from $62.5\%\text{--}81.2\%$ to $25.0\%\text{--}56.2\%$ (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}), showing that standard retrieval scores rely heavily on title anchors while remaining insensitive to eligibility condition drift. Finally, in an equal-call-budget comparison on a $16$-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold rule-compiler benchmark, all non-JSON rewrites achieve $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval on both \texttt{bm25\_char2} and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}, yet single-pass JSON rewriting fails format validation ($0/16$ valid, $31.2\%$ decision preservation), and an equal-budget LLM self-check without parser feedback (\texttt{generic\_self\_refine}, $26$ calls) reaches $37.5\%$ ($6/16$) all-slot match and $50.0\%$ ($8/16$) decision determinacy with $25.0\%$ ($4/16$) threshold mutations (unchanged at $32$ calls). At the same $26$-call budget, symbolic parser-guided \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} lifts all-slot match to $68.8\%$ ($11/16$), lifts decision determinacy to $75.0\%$ ($12/16$), and reduces parser-detected threshold mutations to $0.0\%$ ($0/16$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Selecting a retriever or building a synthetic evaluation benchmark for a vertical-domain RAG system, such as insurance underwriting and claims advisory, often takes place before real user logs are available. A common workflow in retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas,zhu2025rageval,filice2025datamorgana} is to prompt an instruction-tuned LLM to generate user questions from corpus passages, filter outputs by surface format checks, and use the resulting synthetic queries to rank retrievers or score RAG pipelines.

While recent studies show that synthetic queries can alter macro-level retriever rankings relative to real user traffic \cite{rahmani2024syndl,vanelburg2025synthetic,kucia2026beyond}, a structural mismatch arises in rule-governed domains such as insurance: retrievers score topical and lexical similarity, whereas domain validity depends on discrete eligibility conditions. In Chinese insurance clauses, whether a claim is excluded by a waiting period (\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} vs.\ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) is a deterministic function of product identity, first-time vs.\ renewal enrollment, policy effective day, sickness vs.\ accidental injury cause, plan-specific waiting period (e.g., $7/15/25$ or $30/90$ days), and insurer waiver approval. When an LLM rewrites a seed scenario into a conversational question, two failure modes appear even when the rewrite achieves $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval against the source clause:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Condition Omission and Ambiguity Degradation (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY})}: Conversational rewrites often drop pivotal underwriting slots to sound brief and natural, collapsing a uniquely decidable test case into an underspecified question where opposite coverage outcomes are reachable. Conversely, omitting a \emph{non-pivotal} slot (such as sickness vs.\ accident cause after the waiting period has already expired) leaves the formal coverage decision unchanged. Simple slot-matching scripts conflate these two cases, whereas retrieval metrics ignore both.
  \item \textbf{Condition Mutation and Threshold Overwriting (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} / \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP})}: Small local models and open-loop prompts frequently alter numeric durations (e.g., rewriting ``Day~20'' into ``Day~2'') or replace a user's actual elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the statutory threshold stated in the source clause ($14$ or $90$ days), flipping the ground-truth coverage decision while increasing lexical overlap with the target document.
\end{enumerate}

At the corpus retrieval level, comparing candidate retrievers (e.g., character-bigram BM25, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}, and hybrid \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}) on uncalibrated synthetic query sets also conflates \emph{source-subsampling variance} (when synthetic queries are generated from a small subset of documents) with \emph{within-source lexical copy inflation} ($\kappa_2(q, d^*)$).

We investigate clause-grounded, decision-preserving verification for synthetic RAG evaluation alongside corpus-level retrieval-ranking diagnostics. To keep terminology distinct, we separate two forms of abstention: \emph{retriever-selection candidate-set abstention} (Section~4, withholding single-winner commitment when small-sample calibration uncertainty spans multiple retrievers) and \emph{query-level clarification abstention (\texttt{CLARIFY})} (Section~5, where a question missing a pivotal underwriting slot cannot be decided without user clarification, certified via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses). We also distinguish \emph{automatic required-slot match} from \emph{reference-independent formal decision determinacy} (computed by enumerating complete threshold-partitioned equivalence regions of any omitted slots without reading reference seed metadata). Our work makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Source-Matched Retrieval Confounding Decomposition, Overlap Gating, and Candidate-Set Abstention}: On $3{,}746$ deduplicated answers from \texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv} partitioned into $3{,}725$ connected groups ($750$ calibration, $2{,}994$ held-out test queries), we evaluate eight lexical, dense (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}), and hybrid (\texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}) systems under deterministic proxies, local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} ($N=249$), and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($N=179$) synthetic queries. Zero-truncation sliding-window \texttt{Full-MaxP} ($509$ long docs, $762$ tail windows) confirms that \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} leads on the full test set ($+0.0187$ standard, $+0.0158$ \texttt{Full-MaxP}). Source-matched real test pairs ($n=209$ and $n=153$) show via paired difference-in-differences ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$) that source-subsampling shift is a major confounder alongside $\kappa_2$ inflation. We then evaluate post-hoc \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} ($q \in [0.10, 0.30]$) and paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention across $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$.
  \item \textbf{Clause-Grounded Decision Verification Across Model Scales}: Over five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (SHA256-verified with PDF bounding-box anchors), we compile formal waiting-period decision rules to build $20$ seed frames ($80$ controlled cases where every \texttt{missing\_critical} \texttt{CLARIFY} case carries two opposite-outcome completion witnesses) and evaluate $120$ paired rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}. We separate numeric mutations ($15\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} vs.\ $0/20$ observed on cloud models in this sample) from conversational slot omission, and measure the gap between strict slot completeness and reference-independent decision determinacy.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Evaluation on Four Unseen Official Insurance Policies and Title-Masked Retrieval}: To evaluate whether the rule compiler and slot verifier generalize beyond the five development policies, we collect four held-out official insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{pa\_2011\_36}, \texttt{pa\_p0254}, \texttt{pa\_p0321}, \texttt{cpic\_93896}; $16$ seeds, $64$ controlled cases) and run the frozen development-set parser and reachability verifier without regex modifications. The frozen verifier certifies $64/64$ controlled cases, shows $0/16$ standalone ($5/16$ granted-policy) decision preservation under \texttt{ordinary\_style} vs.\ $15/16$ automatic ($16/16$ manually verified) preservation and $0/16$ mutations under \texttt{condition\_prompt}/\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}, and shows via title-masked retrieval over the 9-clause pool that lexical and dense retrievers depend heavily on product-title strings while failing to detect decision loss.
  \item \textbf{Equal-Call-Budget Baseline Comparison on a Multi-Threshold Rule-Compiler Benchmark}: On a $16$-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark inspired by healthcare and labor-law rules, we compare \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} against five baselines including an identity upper bound (\texttt{seed\_passthrough}, $0$ calls), single-pass JSON schema rewriting (\texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite}, $16$ calls), and an equal-call-budget LLM self-refinement baseline without symbolic parser feedback (\texttt{generic\_self\_refine}, evaluated at both the matched $26$-call budget and full $32$-call budget), measuring both decision preservation and template novelty ($1 - \kappa_2(q', q_{\text{seed}})$). At the same $26$-call budget, symbolic parser-guided \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} exceeds \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} by $+31.3$ percentage points in all-slot match ($68.8\%$ vs.\ $37.5\%$) and $+25.0$ percentage points in formal decision determinacy ($75.0\%$ vs.\ $50.0\%$), while reducing parser-detected condition mutations from $25.0\%$ ($4/16$) to $0.0\%$ ($0/16$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Dataset Provenance}
\paragraph{Synthetic Queries and Retriever Ranking Sensitivity.}
Promptagator \cite{dai2023promptagator} and InPars-v2 \cite{jeronymo2023inparsv2} use LLM-generated queries to train retrievers when in-domain labels are scarce, while evaluation frameworks such as ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} and RAGAs \cite{es2024ragas} use synthetic queries to score RAG pipelines. Recent work has examined whether synthetic queries preserve system rankings: Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and van Elburg et al.\ \cite{vanelburg2025synthetic} analyze macro-level rank correlations and generator biases on synthetic test collections, while Kucia and Gawlik \cite{kucia2026beyond} show on FiQA that minor query perturbations shift rankings across BM25, dense bi-encoders, and hybrid RRF. Because synthetic-vs.-real ranking shifts are known in prior literature, we use our corpus-level retrieval experiments in Sections~3--4 as a diagnostic baseline in Chinese insurance that (i)~decomposes \emph{source-subsampling variance} from \emph{within-source lexical overlap inflation} ($\kappa_2(q, d^*)$) via paired difference-in-differences ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$), and (ii)~connects retrieval behavior to downstream eligibility-condition preservation.

\paragraph{Schema-Driven RAG Synthesis and Question Generation Evaluation.}
To improve synthetic benchmark diversity and domain grounding, RAGEval \cite{zhu2025rageval} extracts domain schemas from documents in finance, medicine, and law to synthesize RAG evaluation sets, and DataMorgana \cite{filice2025datamorgana} provides configurable user and question categorizations for synthetic benchmark generation. In parallel, QGEval \cite{fu2024qgeval} benchmarks multi-dimensional question generation quality (fluency, clarity, answerability). However, schema-guided generation (RAGEval, DataMorgana) and surface answerability scoring (QGEval) operate open-loop during question rewriting: they do not compile source clause thresholds into a reachability verifier that checks whether a generated rewrite preserves the business decision (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}), flips or mutates a condition (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}/\texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP}), or degrades into an underspecified question (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}). Our equal-budget experiments in Section~6 show that open-loop schema/JSON prompting and unguided LLM self-refinement leave $25.0\%\text{--}31.2\%$ of multi-threshold rewrites with mutated conditions unless paired with symbolic slot verification.

\paragraph{Rule-Following, Clarification Abstention (\texttt{CLARIFY}), and Insurance QA.}
In conversational QA, ShARC \cite{saeidi2018sharc} formulates rule-grounded question answering where an assistant asks follow-up questions when required variables are missing. More recently, CLAMBER \cite{zhang2024clamber}, AskBench \cite{zhao2026askbench}, and PRECISE \cite{divekar2026precise} evaluate and train LLMs to identify ambiguous user requests and ask clarifying questions in medical and open-domain dialogues, while RC-RAG \cite{chen2024rcrag} studies rule-contradiction detection in RAG answers. In the insurance domain, InsuranceQA \cite{feng2020insuranceqa} provides community QA pairs for answer selection, and InsQABench \cite{ding2025insqabench} benchmarks LLM accuracy on insurance product rules. Our work studies an upstream problem: rather than evaluating whether a \emph{downstream answering assistant} asks follow-up questions on given prompts, we verify and repair \emph{synthetic evaluation queries} during benchmark construction, certifying ambiguous cases via double-completion counterexample witnesses and separating non-pivotal slot omissions from decision ambiguity.

\paragraph{Calibration under Small Budgets.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} combine synthetic predictions with small gold samples on paired data. In cold-start retriever selection, where synthetic queries are generated from a document subset without 1-to-1 paired real queries across the full corpus, we evaluate unpaired shrinkage and propensity weighting as negative baselines and formulate a bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule.

\subsection{Corpus Provenance, Licensing Scope, and Inference-Only Setup}
Table~\ref{tab:data_audit} summarizes the provenance, licensing scope, and modeling role of each dataset and model:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-Only Model Evaluation}: We do not pre-train, instruction-tune, or fine-tune (SFT/LoRA) any generative LLM or dense embedding model weights. All LLM runs (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, \texttt{Qwen-Max}) and dense encoding runs (\texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5}) use frozen weights in inference mode (\texttt{float32/eager} greedy decoding locally; \texttt{temperature}=0 via API). Few-shot prompts (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) provide 3 calibration examples in context. The only fitted statistical parameters in the codebase are corpus TF-IDF/BM25 term statistics and the 2-fold cross-fit logistic regression domain classifier in \texttt{src/experiment.py} used to estimate real-vs.-synthetic propensity weights.
  \item \textbf{Provenance of \texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}}: The community QA file ($8{,}362$ raw records; $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid questions after normalization) comes from the public GitHub repository \texttt{meowcao/InsuranceModel}, which references \texttt{SophonPlus/ChineseNlpCorpus} (\texttt{datasets/baoxianzhidao/intro.ipynb}). Upstream documentation notes that the data was collected from Baidu Zhidao insurance Q\&A with original URLs stripped and sensitive fields filtered. Thus, individual post URLs cannot be recovered from the CSV, answers represent historical community-selected responses rather than actuarial rulings, neither repository documents a verified open redistribution license chain, and Chinese foundation models may have seen portions of this corpus during pretraining.
  \item \textbf{Official Insurance Clauses vs.\ Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rules}: The nine insurance clauses ($5$ development clauses in Table~\ref{tab:clauses} and $4$ held-out clauses in Section~5.5) are extracted from official PDF policy wordings on insurer disclosure portals. Because public web access does not grant a license to redistribute full commercial PDFs, our repository distributes SHA256 file hashes, download URLs, PDF page and bounding-box coordinates, and short verification excerpts rather than raw PDF binaries. By contrast, the four multi-threshold rules in Section~6 are researcher-authored synthetic specifications.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Group-Disjoint Corpus Split and Lexical Proxy Baseline}

\subsection{Leakage-Free Group Split}
Public Chinese insurance FAQ corpora (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}, derived from insuranceQA-zh) contain repeated questions and shared boilerplate answers. Splitting QA pairs at random places identical answer texts in both calibration and test sets.

After normalizing whitespace and deduplicating exact answer strings, we obtain $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid QA pairs. We build an undirected graph linking any pairs that share normalized questions, normalized answers, or near-duplicate question prefixes, yielding $3{,}725$ connected components via Union-Find. Assigning whole components to splits (seed \texttt{20261025}) produces:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Real Calibration Pool}: $750$ queries across $745$ components (used for few-shot demonstrations and small-budget calibration sampling).
  \item \textbf{Real Test Set}: $2{,}994$ queries across $2{,}980$ components (zero component overlap with calibration).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lexical Systems and Deterministic Proxies}
We first index all $3{,}746$ answers under six character $n$-gram lexical retrievers: sublinear TF-IDF cosine similarity over character unigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1}), bigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char2}), $1\text{--}3$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1\_3}), and $3\text{--}5$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5}), alongside Okapi BM25 ($k_1=1.2, b=0.75$) over character unigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char1}) and bigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char2}). Each system is scored by $\text{MRR@10}$ and $\text{Hit@5}$ (recall at rank~5).

We compare held-out real test queries against two deterministic non-LLM proxies constructed from the target answers: (1)~\texttt{synthetic\_keywords}, which concatenates top corpus-level TF-IDF terms from the answer into a question template, and (2)~\texttt{source\_prefix\_diagnostic}, a proxy that turns the opening clause of the answer into a question.

\subsection{Lexical Baseline Results}
Table~\ref{tab:round1} and Figure~\ref{fig:round1} show that on held-out real queries, \texttt{bm25\_char2} ranks first ($\text{MRR@10}=0.3817, \text{Hit@5}=0.4826$), whereas \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} ranks fourth ($\text{MRR@10}=0.2727, \text{Hit@5}=0.3557$) because real user questions rarely share contiguous $3\text{--}5$-character spans with answer texts. \section{Retriever Ranking Shifts, Source-Sampling Confounding, and Selection Abstention}

\subsection{Setup and Shared Valid Source Sets}
To evaluate how LLM query generators behave when dense and hybrid retrievers are included, we expand the candidate pool to eight systems by adding:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}: Dense retrieval with \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} (512-dim normalized embeddings; 512-token limit, which truncates $509$ of $3{,}746$ long answers, while $2$ of $256$ source answers exceed $512$ tokens during LLM prompt construction).
  \item \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}: Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k=60$, depth $100$) combining \texttt{bm25\_char2} (requiring positive lexical score) and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}.
\end{itemize}

We sample $256$ target answer passages and generate synthetic questions under two prompting modes (\texttt{zero\_shot} and \texttt{few\_shot\_3}, where the 3 style demonstrations are fixed real questions drawn from the calibration pool; test questions are never exposed). We run two generators:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]

\subsection{Decomposing Selection Reversal: Source-Sampling Confounding vs.\ Within-Source Lexical Inflation}
Table~\ref{tab:round2} and Figure~\ref{fig:round2} reveal that comparing an uncalibrated synthetic benchmark ($N=249$ or $179$ sampled sources) against the full real test distribution ($N=2{,}994$) conflates two distinct effects, which we explicitly separate:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Full-Test Deployment Winner and Full-Document MaxP Check}: Across all $2{,}994$ held-out real questions, pure dense retrieval (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6453$), beating \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.5030$, $\text{Hit@5}=0.6075$) by $+0.0187$ (10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+0.0077, +0.0296]$). To verify that this dense-over-hybrid ranking is not an artifact of 512-token truncation on the $509$ long documents, \texttt{src/maxp\_ablation.py} runs a full-document sliding-window ablation (\texttt{Full-MaxP}), splitting all $509$ long answers into $512$-token windows with a $256$-token stride ($762$ additional non-redundant overflow tail chunks, $1{,}271$ total windows across the $509$ long answers, $0$ truncated documents; retokenizing each decoded tail string confirms $\le 510$ content tokens and $536/762$ exact token-ID matches) and scoring each document by maximum passage cosine similarity $\max_{c \in d} \cos(\mathbf{e}_q, \mathbf{e}_c)$. Under \texttt{Full-MaxP}, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5165$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6389$) vs.\ $0.5007$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6055$) for \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($\Delta = +0.0158$, 95\% CI $[+0.0049, +0.0267]$), confirming that pure dense retrieval remains the full-test winner with zero document truncation.
  \item \textbf{Source-Subsample Selection Confounding}: Because the $256$ generation sources were sampled from the document pool rather than paired with all $2{,}994$ test queries, restricting \texttt{real\_test} to queries whose gold answers fall in the valid synthetic source subsets ($n=209$ real queries across $207$ unique docs for \texttt{1.5B}; $n=153$ real queries across $151$ unique docs for \texttt{Qwen-Plus}) shifts the real-query point estimate to favor \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.4975$ vs.\ $0.4696$, $\Delta_{\text{BGE}-\text{RRF}} = -0.0279$, 95\% CI $[-0.0645, +0.0096]$ on \texttt{1.5B}-matched; $0.4980$ vs.\ $0.4668$, $\Delta = -0.0312$, 95\% CI $[-0.0687, +0.0073]$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}-matched). Although both matched-subset CIs span zero, this demonstrates that small unstratified document subsamples introduce substantial source-selection variance before any query synthesis occurs.
  \item \textbf{Within-Source Lexical Copy Inflation and Paired Difference-in-Differences ($\Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}}$)}: Controlling for source composition by pairing each real query with its corresponding synthetic query \emph{on the exact same target document} ($n=209$ and $n=153$) isolates the within-source query-style effect. On the $209$ \texttt{1.5B}-matched pairs, \texttt{zero\_shot} generation increases query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ from $0.3253$ (real) to $0.4775$ ($0.4789$ across the $207$ unique docs), boosting \texttt{bm25\_char2} from $0.4073$ to $0.4998$ ($0.5036$ on the $207$ unique docs; paired gain $+0.0925$, 95\% CI $[+0.0227, +0.1633]$), while on the $153$ \texttt{Qwen-Plus}-matched pairs, \texttt{zero\_shot} raises $\kappa_2$ from $0.3167$ to $0.4638$ ($0.4651$ on $151$ unique docs) and jumps \texttt{bm25\_char2} from $0.3909$ to $0.6366$ ($0.6450$ on $151$ unique docs; paired gain $+0.2457$, 95\% CI $[+0.1695, +0.3216]$). However, when we test the paired difference-in-differences in RRF's advantage over BGE,
  \[
    \Delta\Delta_{\text{RRF}-\text{BGE}} = (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}} - \text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{syn}} - (\text{MRR}_{\text{RRF}} - \text{MRR}_{\text{BGE}})_{\text{real}},
  \]
  all four 10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95\% CIs span zero under both query-equal-weighted resampling and source-document-clustered resampling (which averages per unique target document before resampling documents with equal weight): query-weighted $+0.0123$ (CI $[-0.0375, +0.0649]$; document-equal-weighted mean $+0.0185$, clustered CI $[-0.0314, +0.0698]$) on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, query-weighted $-0.0259$ (CI $[-0.0736, +0.0217]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0225$, clustered CI $[-0.0710, +0.0258]$) on \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3}, query-weighted $-0.0020$ (CI $[-0.0479, +0.0438]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0036$, clustered CI $[-0.0474, +0.0421]$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}, and query-weighted $-0.0298$ (CI $[-0.0791, +0.0204]$; doc-weighted mean $-0.0320$, clustered CI $[-0.0813, +0.0187]$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3}. Thus, while \texttt{zero\_shot} synthesis demonstrably inflates $\kappa_2$ and \texttt{bm25\_char2}, the current paired sample sizes ($n=209/153$) do not show a statistically significant within-source amplification of the RRF$-$BGE gap beyond source-sampling shift. Providing 3 calibration examples (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) reduces $\kappa_2$ to $0.3180$ (\texttt{1.5B}) and $0.4108$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), though on \texttt{1.5B} a 20-source audit (\texttt{semantic\_audit.json}) shows that mimicking unrelated examples reduces source-supported questions from $12/20$ to $9/20$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Marginal Reweighting Fails vs.\ Exploratory Source-Overlap Gating and Candidate-Set Abstention}
In \texttt{validate\_v2.py}, a 2-fold cross-fit logistic domain classifier trained on character $1\text{--}3$-gram TF-IDF features separates real calibration queries from synthetic queries with $\text{ROC-AUC}=0.8120$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $0.7733$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) at $B=25$ (rising to $0.8765$ and $0.9148$ at $B=200$). Yet across $5$ repeated draws at each budget $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$, propensity density weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_density\_weighted}) selects the full-test winner \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in \textbf{$0\%$ of trials}, and length weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_length\_weighted}) reaches $0\%$ on \texttt{zero\_shot} ($0\%\text{--}20\%$ on \texttt{few\_shot\_3}).

Why does marginal query-feature reweighting fail? Because lexical advantage between \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} and \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} is governed by the \emph{relational} overlap $\kappa_2(q, d^*) = |\text{Bi}(q) \cap \text{Bi}(d^*)| / |\text{Bi}(q)|$ between a query and its target document $d^*$ rather than marginal query text $P(q)$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Low-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 < 0.25$, $40.0\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=1{,}199$)}: \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} outperforms \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} on \texttt{real\_test} ($0.3022$ vs.\ $0.2220$, $\Delta = +0.0801$) \emph{and on all four LLM synthetic sets}: \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} ($+0.0839$, $n=29$), \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3} ($+0.0326$, $n=115$), \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot} ($+0.0821$, $n=13$), and \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3} ($+0.1600$, $n=14$).
  \item \textbf{High-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$, $20.6\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=617$)}: \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} outperforms \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} on \texttt{real\_test} ($\Delta = -0.0457$) and on all four synthetic sets ($\Delta \in [-0.0811, -0.0447]$). Unweighted \texttt{zero\_shot} evaluation over-weights this stratum ($48.6\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} and $41.9\%$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, vs.\ $20.6\%$ on \texttt{real\_test}).
\end{itemize}

We therefore evaluate two diagnostic and decision-theoretic procedures (noting that \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} was formulated post-hoc after inspecting the overlap strata and should be validated on independent corpora before deployment as a standalone cold-start rule):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Exploratory Relational Source-Overlap Gating (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}) and Quantile Sensitivity}: Filtering synthetic queries to retain those with $\kappa_2(q', d^*) \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$ (where $\hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)} = \text{median}_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}} \kappa_2(q, d^*) \approx 0.292$, falling back to $Q_{0.20}(\kappa_2)$ if fewer than $5$ synthetic queries satisfy $\kappa_2 \le \hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}$) selects \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in $5/5$ calibration draws across $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$ on both \texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus}.\footnote{On deterministic \texttt{keywords\_matched}, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} still fails ($100\%$ wrong selection) because keyword concatenation has zero support in $\kappa_2 < 0.25$.} To test sensitivity to the quantile cutoff in the uncalibrated cold-start setting ($B=0$, a single deterministic evaluation per synthetic set), \texttt{src/maxp\_ablation.py} sweeps $q \in [0.10, 1.00]$: all four synthetic sets select \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} for $q \in \{0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30\}$ (and up to $q=0.40$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, $q=0.60$ on \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3}, and $q=0.80$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3}), whereas relaxing the cutoff to $q \ge 0.40$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot} ($\Delta = -0.0033$) or $q \ge 0.50$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} ($\Delta = -0.0099$) reverts to \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}. At the same subset size $k=Q_{0.20}$ ($k=70$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot}, $k=36$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}), 1,000 random subsamples select \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in only $5.9\%$ and $18.2\%$ of draws, confirming that gating depends on $\kappa_2$ rather than sample-size reduction.
  \item \textbf{Retriever-Selection Candidate-Set Abstention}: When evaluating directly on a small real calibration sample (\texttt{real\_only}) of size $B$ with empirical leader $\hat{s}$, we run $200$ paired bootstrap resamples over the $B$ queries and retain all systems $s$ whose 95\% upper bound on $(\text{MRR}_s - \text{MRR}_{\hat{s}})$ is $\ge 0$. When $|\mathcal{C}_B| > 1$, the selector abstains from point commitment and returns $\mathcal{C}_B$.
\end{enumerate}

Each structured scenario frame specifies up to six required fields: \texttt{policy\_id} (required in $20/20$ seeds), \texttt{enrollment} (\texttt{first} vs.\ \texttt{new}, $20/20$), \texttt{policy\_day} ($20/20$), \texttt{cause} (\texttt{illness} vs.\ \texttt{accident}, $20/20$), \texttt{plan\_wait\_days} (required in $4/20$ seeds on \texttt{cpic\_86017}), and \texttt{approved\_waiver} (required in $3/20$ renewal seeds). From $20$ seed frames, we generate $80$ controlled test cases across four variants: \texttt{complete} (all required fields stated), \texttt{surface} (synonymous wording), \texttt{missing\_critical} (one pivotal condition removed, certified as \texttt{CLARIFY} via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses), and \texttt{counterfactual} (one field modified to flip the rule outcome between \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} and \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}).

Because a seed scenario can include non-pivotal slots (for example, on \texttt{pa\_h865} with a 30-day waiting period, an illness occurring on Day~45 already satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting \texttt{cause} still yields \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED} under both \texttt{illness} and \texttt{accident}), \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py} separates two evaluation metrics:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{All Required Slots Matched (\texttt{surface\_v2\_preserved})}: Requires every field in \texttt{required\_fields} to be extracted without omission or mutation.
  \item \textbf{Reference-Independent Formal Decision Determinacy (\texttt{enumerate\_admissible\_decisions})}: Enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unobserved slots \emph{using only the extracted question observations and public policy rules} (without reading ground-truth \texttt{seed\_frame} metadata), partitioning day domains into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t, = t, > t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ via representative values $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$) and checking reachability and slot mutations across all six schema fields (\texttt{ALL\_INSURANCE\_FIELDS}, including optional waiver slots not in a seed's minimal \texttt{required\_fields}): a rewrite is scored as \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} iff no observed schema field is mutated and the reachable decision set is the singleton $[\text{decision}(f^*)]$, as \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP} / \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} if any schema field is mutated or the unique reachable decision flips, and as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} when $|\text{reachable}| \neq 1$.
\end{enumerate}

  \item \texttt{ordinary\_style}: ``把原问题改写成用户口语化提问，简短自然，保持原本意图。'' (Rewrite into a short, natural colloquial question keeping original intent.)

\subsection{Findings on Condition Drift}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} show four main patterns:

\paragraph{1. Surface Format Checks Miss Multi-Question Outputs and Slot Omissions.}
All $120/120$ generated questions pass the single-line \texttt{format\_valid} check (though $7/40$ local \texttt{1.5B} outputs contain multiple question marks in a single line, compared to $0/40$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}). Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, $70\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $100\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $80\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) fail automatic all-slot matching, and $70\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $100\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $55\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) fail standalone decision determinacy even after enumerating all admissible completions of non-pivotal slots.

\paragraph{2. Value Mutation on \texttt{1.5B} vs.\ Conversational Omission Across Tested Scales.}
Comparing the three tested model tiers separates two failure types:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Value Mutation and Label Collision on \texttt{1.5B}}: In $3/20$ seeds (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016} on \texttt{cpic\_82859}), \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{ordinary\_style} mutates ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2). On \texttt{condition-009} (7-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) and \texttt{condition-010} (25-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}), \texttt{1.5B} drops both the product name and the plan waiting period, generating the exact same text for two opposite ground-truth labels:
  \begin{quote}
  \small
  ``我是在网上看病花了钱，是第一次买这个保险，保单生效第10天了，因为不是意外病，想问一下这属于等待期吗？''
  \end{quote}
  \item \textbf{Conversational Omission on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}}: On \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, observed numeric value mutations on this 20-seed sample drop to $0\%$ ($0/20$; as a finite sample observation on $n=20$ seeds, this does not imply a zero population error rate). Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, however, both cloud models shorten administrative details to sound conversational: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the product title in $20/20$ ($100\%$) of seeds, while \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops product titles in $10/20$ ($50\%$) and rewrites ``首次投保'' (first-time enrollment) into ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which is ambiguous between first enrollment and renewal) in $6/20$ ($30\%$) of seeds. When \texttt{Qwen-Max} retains \texttt{policy\_id} ($10/20$ seeds), $5$ of those seeds omit \texttt{enrollment} on non-waivable policies or post-waiting-period days where the decision remains uniquely determined (\texttt{NON\_PIVOTAL\_SLOT\_OMITTED\_DECISION\_PRESERVED}), yielding $4/20$ ($20\%$) all-slot match vs.\ $9/20$ ($45\%$) standalone decision determinacy.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Slot-Constrained Prompting Across Tested Scales.}
Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, automatic all-slot match (\texttt{v2\_synonym\_aware}) reaches $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}. On \texttt{Qwen-Plus} \texttt{condition\_prompt}, the single seed with an omitted \texttt{cause} slot occurs on a non-pivotal scenario where both illness and accident completions yield the same waiting-period outcome, so completion-enumerated standalone decision determinacy reaches $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on both \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} ($100\%$ across all three models when source \texttt{policy\_id} is granted).

\paragraph{4. Strict Regex False Negatives and Blinded Review Pack.}
On \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{condition\_prompt}, \texttt{v1\_strict\_regex} flags $10/20$ questions even though $9$ of those $10$ retain all conditions under synonymous phrasing (e.g., ``生效是第20天'', ``同意免去等待期''). Because automated slot parsers are proxy evaluators rather than ground-truth semantic judges, we package all $80$ controlled cases and $40$ local generations into randomized, blinded two-rater HTML forms (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, scored by \texttt{score\_annotations.py}); human annotation results are not claimed in this manuscript prior to independent completion.

\section{Equal-Call-Budget Comparison on Multi-Threshold Rules}
To examine how double-witness verification and closed-loop slot repair behave on multi-threshold rules under an equal LLM call budget, we build a $16$-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark in \texttt{src/cross\_domain\_study.py} and \texttt{src/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_study.py} (\texttt{data\_v3/cross\_domain\_controlled\_cases.jsonl}, \texttt{cross\_domain\_summary.json}, and \texttt{equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_summary.json}). Unlike the nine SHA256-verified official insurance policy PDFs in Section~5, these four rules are researcher-authored synthetic composite clauses inspired by clinical pathway and PRC labor-law structures (used for controlled formal rule testing rather than clinical or legal compliance benchmarking):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare-Inspired Synthetic Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (a composite EGFR-TKI prior-authorization rule combining T790M mutation status, a 14-day second-line washout interval, and specialist waiver override); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (an orthopedic post-op VTE prophylaxis pathway contrasting a 10-day knee replacement window vs.\ a 35-day hip replacement window, with active bleeding contraindication and hematology consult waiver).
  \item \textbf{Labor-Law-Inspired Synthetic Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (inspired by PRC Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14(6) on non-principal commuting traffic fault and Art.~17 contrasting the 30-day employer filing window vs.\ the 1-year/365-day employee/kin window and administrative extension); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (inspired by PRC Labor Contract Law Art.~24 on confidential personnel and SPC Labor Dispute Judicial Interpretation~I Art.~38 on 3-month/90-day compensation default, augmented with a synthetic grace-period waiver clause).
\end{itemize}

Every seed scenario is compiled into four controlled variants (\texttt{complete}, \texttt{surface}, \texttt{missing\_critical} certified as \texttt{CLARIFY} via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses, and \texttt{counterfactual} flipping \texttt{RULE\_ELIGIBLE} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{RULE\_EXCLUDED}). In \texttt{enumerate\_cross\_decisions}, unobserved duration slots are expanded across a complete threshold-equivalence partition ($\{7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 30, 32, 35, 45, 90, 120, 365, 400\}$, covering $< t, = t, > t$ across all rule thresholds $\{10, 14, 30, 35, 90, 365\}$), and reachability is evaluated from the extracted question slots and public clause schema without reading ground-truth \texttt{seed\_frame} metadata (with negated waivers such as ``尚未获…特批'' or ``未批准延长申请时限'' parsed as \texttt{False}, explicit waiver uncertainty parsed as \texttt{unknown} $\rightarrow \{\texttt{False}, \texttt{True}\}$, and unmentioned extraordinary waivers defaulting to $\{\texttt{False}\}$). In \texttt{evaluate\_cross\_outcome}, mutations are checked across all six schema fields (\texttt{ALL\_CROSS\_FIELDS}, including optional waiver exceptions not in a seed's minimal \texttt{required\_fields}) and reachability is checked first: the scorer returns \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} iff no schema field is mutated and $\text{reachable} = [\text{cross\_decision}(f^*)]$, returns \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} whenever any schema field is mutated or $|\text{reachable}| = 1$ with $\text{reachable}[0] \neq \text{cross\_decision}(f^*)$, and returns \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} when $|\text{reachable}| \neq 1$.

To compare prompting and refinement strategies under matched call budgets, we evaluate \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32/eager}, greedy decoding) across all $16$ seeds under seven methods:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{seed\_passthrough} ($0$ calls): deterministic template question prior to LLM rewriting (information-preservation upper bound, zero template novelty).
  \item \texttt{ordinary\_style} ($16$ calls, $1$/seed): unconstrained conversational rewrite.
  \item \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($16$ calls, $1$/seed): open-loop explicit slot-constrained rewrite.
  \item \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($16$ calls, $1$/seed): instructs the LLM to first extract all 6 slot values in a JSON object on Line~1 and then emit \texttt{QUESTION: <single-sentence question>} on Line~2.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine\_budget\_matched} ($26$ calls: $16$ initial $+$ $10$ second-pass calls on the same $10$ seeds triggered by \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}): asks the LLM to self-check and revise its \texttt{condition\_prompt} draft against the source clause and seed question \emph{without symbolic parser feedback}.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine\_all} ($32$ calls: $2$ calls on all $16$ seeds): runs the unguided LLM self-check across every seed regardless of whether an error occurred.
  \item \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($26$ calls: $16$ initial $+$ $10$ parser-guided repair calls triggered by \texttt{should\_trigger\_slot\_guard\_repair(missing, mutated) = bool(missing or mutated)}): feeds the specific missing or mutated slot diagnostics from \texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2} into a 1-step repair prompt. Because \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} is triggered and scored by \texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2}, we report these metrics as parser-verified proxy rates.
\end{enumerate}

Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, however, decision preservation is $6.2\%$ ($1/16$), with \texttt{cross-001} (Day~7 of 14-day washout $\rightarrow$ \texttt{RULE\_EXCLUDED}) and \texttt{cross-002} (Day~20 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{RULE\_ELIGIBLE}) collapsing into the identical string (``我现在能申请奥希替尼的门诊特药报销吗？''), and \texttt{cross-011} flipping fault polarity from ``本人主要责任'' to ``不是主要责任'' (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}).
  \item \textbf{Single-Pass JSON Rewriting and Open-Loop Prompting on Multi-Threshold Rules}: Open-loop \texttt{condition\_prompt} raises decision determinacy to $43.8\%$ ($7/16$) but incurs $31.2\%$ ($5/16$) threshold-overwriting mutations, where the 1.5B model replaces the user's elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the clause's statutory threshold ($14$ or $90$ days). Meanwhile, while 1-call \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} worked on shorter insurance prompts (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance}), on longer multi-threshold rules the 1.5B model fails the two-part \texttt{JSON + QUESTION:} format on $16/16$ seeds (emitting raw multi-line JSON blocks or omitting the question line), achieving $6.2\%$ ($1/16$) all-slot match, $31.2\%$ ($5/16$) decision determinacy, and $31.2\%$ ($5/16$) mutations.
  \item \textbf{Equal-Budget Symbolic Repair vs.\ Unguided LLM Self-Refinement}: At the $26$-call budget ($16$ initial $+$ $10$ refinement calls), unguided LLM self-checking (\texttt{generic\_self\_refine\_budget\_matched}) improves decision determinacy by $+1$ seed over \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($8/16$ [$50.0\%$] vs.\ $7/16$ [$43.8\%$], all-slot match unchanged at $6/16$ [$37.5\%$]) and leaves $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) threshold mutations unresolved; spending $32$ calls (\texttt{generic\_self\_refine\_all}) yields the same result ($8/16$ decision determinacy, $4/16$ mutations). Without external symbolic localization, the 1.5B model does not detect that it substituted the clause threshold for the user's elapsed days.
  \item \textbf{Symbolic Parser Feedback Eliminates Threshold Mutations While Preserving Template Novelty}: At the same $26$-call budget, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} supplies the missing or mutated slot names from \texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2}, raising automatic all-slot match to $68.8\%$ ($11/16$) ($+31.3$ pp over equal-budget self-refine), lifting formal decision determinacy to $75.0\%$ ($12/16$: $8/8$ Healthcare, $4/8$ Legal) ($+25.0$ pp), and reducing parser-detected \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} from $25.0\%\text{--}31.2\%$ to $0.0\%$ ($0/16$). In addition, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} achieves higher template novelty ($1-\kappa_2(q', q_{\text{seed}}) = 0.3342$) and lower clause copying ($\kappa_2(q', d^*) = 0.4913$) than both \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} ($0.3151$ novelty, $0.5748$ copy) and \texttt{seed\_passthrough} ($0.0000$ novelty, $0.6065$ copy).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Practical Recommendations and Limitations}
\paragraph{Practical Recommendations.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Do not equate high synthetic retrieval scores with valid evaluation queries}: Even $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval (or title-boosted insurance clause retrieval) can coexist with $>68\%\text{--}93\%$ rates of eligibility-condition omission or threshold mutation under conversational rewriting.
  \item \textbf{Compile source rules into a reachability verifier and use symbolic slot repair}: Separate strict slot completeness from reference-independent decision determinacy (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}, \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}, and double-witness-certified \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}), and use symbolic verifier feedback (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) rather than unguided LLM self-refinement when generating constrained benchmarks with small models.
  \item \textbf{Account for source-sampling variance, $\kappa_2(q', d^*)$, and small-sample abstention in retriever ranking}: Pair synthetic benchmarks with source-matched real diagnostics, monitor $\kappa_2(q', d^*)$ via \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} ($q \in [0.10, 0.30]$), and apply paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention on $25\text{--}50$ group-disjoint real calibration queries.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, our FAQ corpus (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}, via \texttt{InsuranceModel}/\texttt{ChineseNlpCorpus}) originates from historical Baidu Zhidao community Q\&A with weak best-answer labels, unverified open redistribution licensing, and possible foundation-model pretraining exposure, and our $249$/$179$ synthetic sets exhibit source-subsampling variance relative to the full $2{,}994$-query test set; in addition, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} was formulated post-hoc and requires prospective validation on independent corpora. Second, our generators belong to the Qwen family (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, \texttt{Qwen-Max}) and are evaluated in inference mode without weight fine-tuning; testing additional model families is needed to assess broader generator diversity. Third, while our nine official insurance clauses ($5$ dev + $4$ held-out) are anchored to SHA256-verified insurer PDFs (with out-of-sample evaluation showing $15/16$ automatic and $16/16$ manually verified decision preservation under the frozen development-set parser), the four multi-threshold rules in Section~6 are researcher-constructed synthetic specifications, and automatic parser metrics in Section~6 remain proxy evaluations pending independent two-rater human annotation of the blinded HTML review packs.
