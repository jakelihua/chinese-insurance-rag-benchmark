\begin{abstract}
Synthetic queries are widely used to evaluate retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems before real user logs exist. In rule-governed insurance domains, however, a synthetic query can retrieve the right clause while dropping or altering a condition that determines coverage. We test whether synthetic rewrites preserve waiting-period decisions across 26 official Chinese insurance policies (five development, four post-refinement extension, 11 zero-alias confirmation, and six untouched post-hardening holdout policies) and a multi-threshold testbed across Qwen and DeepSeek models without fine-tuning. We separate full slot retention from decision determinacy and question ambiguity from parser grammar boundaries via a text-only uncertainty gate. Across a 120-case gold diagnostic suite and a 400-query dual-implementation consistency check, our verifier achieves $0.0\%$ false-accept rate on the diagnostic suite and $0.0\%$ semantic false-accept rate on generated queries (six format-truncation false accepts in structured JSON rewriting; $0/80$ on parser-guided repair). On the 40-seed confirmation set and 24-seed untouched holdout, parser-guided repair reaches $78/80$ ($97.5\%$) and $48/48$ ($100.0\%$) usable decisions, outperforming constrained prompting and equal-budget self-refinement ($70/80$, McNemar $p = 0.007812$, seed-clustered $p = 0.0078125$ and $0.015625$) and preventing downstream pipeline ranking reversal (Kendall's tau from $-0.8367$ to $1.0000$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating a vertical-domain retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system often begins before real user traffic is available. A standard cold-start practice in retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas,zhu2025rageval,filice2025datamorgana} is to prompt an instruction-tuned language model to turn domain passages into synthetic user questions, filter the outputs for surface fluency, and use the resulting benchmark to rank retrievers or score end-to-end answers.

Prior work has shown that synthetic queries can shift system rankings relative to real user queries \cite{rahmani2024syndl,vanelburg2025synthetic,kucia2026beyond}. In rule-governed settings such as insurance underwriting and claims review, a more direct problem arises at the level of individual test cases: retrieval metrics reward lexical and topical similarity, whereas coverage decisions depend on discrete eligibility conditions. Under Chinese commercial health insurance clauses, whether a claim is excluded by a waiting period depends on the exact product and benefit claimed, whether the policy is a first-time purchase or a continuous renewal, how many days have elapsed since the policy took effect, whether hospitalization resulted from illness or accidental injury, and whether the insurer approved a waiting-period waiver.

When a language model rewrites a structured eligibility scenario into a natural-sounding user question, two distinct failure modes occur even when the rewrite ranks the source clause at Top-1:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Ambiguity Degradation (Clarification Required)}: To make a question sound conversational and brief, models frequently drop product titles, benefit types, enrollment status, or elapsed days. Omitting a \emph{pivotal} slot turns a decidable test case into an underspecified inquiry under which both coverage and exclusion remain possible. Conversely, omitting a \emph{non-pivotal} slot---for example, dropping whether a claim arose from illness or accident after the waiting period has already expired---leaves the coverage outcome unchanged. Simple slot-matching rules conflate pivotal and non-pivotal omissions, while retrieval scores ignore both.
  \item \textbf{Condition Mutation and Decision Flip}: Smaller models and open-loop prompts often alter numeric durations (for instance, turning ``Day~20'' into ``Day~2'') or overwrite the policyholder's actual elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the statutory waiting-period threshold stated in the clause ($14$ or $90$ days). Such edits increase lexical overlap with the target document while reversing the ground-truth eligibility decision.
\end{enumerate}

This paper asks a focused diagnostic question: \emph{when synthetic queries are generated from rule-bearing clauses, do they preserve the underlying eligibility decision, and can lightweight symbolic verification detect and repair decision drift?} Rather than proposing a new model architecture or claiming automated rule extraction from arbitrary legal prose, we formalize hand-compiled waiting-period rules over traceable policy clauses and separate surface slot completeness from reference-independent decision determinacy. Specifically, our verifier enumerates all admissible completions of any omitted fields over threshold-partitioned equivalence regions without consulting reference seed metadata, classifying each synthetic rewrite as preserving the unique reference decision, mutating a condition (or flipping the unique decision), or degrading into an ambiguous query accompanied by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses.

Our empirical analysis covers three connected settings, with supplementary community-QA retrieval diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Development Policy Evaluation Across Model Scales (Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies})}: On five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs ($20$ seed scenarios, $80$ controlled cases, and $120$ paired rewrites across local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and cloud Qwen-Plus and Qwen-Max), we measure how unconstrained conversational rewriting versus explicit slot prompting affects slot retention and decision determinacy. Conversational rewrites preserve standalone decisions on only $30\%$, $0\%$, and $45\%$ of seeds across the three model scales, whereas explicit condition constraints reach $95\%\text{--}100\%$.
  \item \textbf{Extended Policy Evaluation and Benefit-Specific Waiting Periods (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies})}: We extend our evaluation to four additional official insurance policy PDFs ($16$ seeds, $64$ controlled cases) by incorporating benefit-specific waiting periods within a single contract (e.g., general inpatient allowance with a 30-day wait and accident exemption versus cancer inpatient allowance with a 90-day wait in \texttt{pa\_2011\_36}) and colloquial product aliases into the shared parser. Under this post-refinement evaluation, unconstrained conversational rewrites preserve $0/16$ standalone decisions ($5/16$ when the policy identity is given), while constrained prompting preserves $16/16$ on the first pass (requiring $0$ repair calls). Title-masked retrieval across all nine clauses further shows that standard retrieval scores rely heavily on product-title strings rather than eligibility conditions.
  \item \textbf{Equal-Budget Repair on Multi-Threshold Rules (Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold})}: On a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark where all non-JSON rewrites reach $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval, we compare symbolic parser-guided repair against an equal-budget unguided self-refinement baseline ($26$ LLM calls). Symbolic feedback raises standalone decision preservation from $8/16$ ($50.0\%$) to $12/16$ ($75.0\%$, exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.125$) and reduces parser-detected condition mutations from $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) to $0/16$ ($0.0\%$); in a post-hoc sensitivity decomposition where the source clause identity is granted at evaluation time, conversational rewrites rise from $1/16$ to $7/16$ and parser-guided repair reaches $15/16$ versus $8/16$ for self-refinement (exact McNemar $p=0.0156$).
  \item \textbf{Dual-Implementation Consistency Audit, 40-Seed Confirmation \& 24-Seed Untouched Holdout Across Model Families, and Isolated Downstream Pipeline Selection Impact (Section~\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation})}: Testing $11$ zero-alias confirmation insurance policy PDFs ($40$ seeds, $160$ cases) plus $6$ newly collected untouched post-hardening holdout policy PDFs ($24$ seeds, $48$ cross-model pairs) across \texttt{Qwen-Plus} (\texttt{response\_model=qwen-plus}) and \texttt{DeepSeek} (\texttt{deepseek-chat} endpoint, \texttt{response\_model=deepseek-flash}), we separate parser syntax boundaries (\texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}) from question ambiguity (\texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED}) via a text-only gate, evaluate verifier accuracy on a 120-case constructive gold diagnostic suite ($0.0\%$ false-accept rate) and a 400-query shared-rule-base dual-implementation consistency check ($0.0\%$ semantic false-accept rate [$296/0/86/18$], and $97.97\%$ joint format-and-semantic precision [$290/6/88/16$], with $5$ format-truncation false accepts on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and $1$ on \texttt{DeepSeek} in 160-token \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite}, and $0/80$ false accepts on \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}), evaluate fair structured baselines (\texttt{max\_new\_tokens=320} at $73/80$ and post-hoc deterministic JSON schema rendering from the 160-token Line-1 JSON at $70/80$) alongside \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($78/80 = 97.5\%$ on the 40-seed confirmation set and $48/48 = 100.0\%$ on the 24-seed untouched holdout), and show using strictly isolated inference (\texttt{predict(question, retrieved\_clause, inference\_config)}) and $100\%$ pure BGE dense vector caching (with an explicit lexical zero-overlap guard when $\text{BM25} \le 0$) that unverified rewrites invert downstream pipeline rankings ($\tau \in [-0.8367, -0.7746] \rightarrow 1.0000$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Dataset Provenance}

\paragraph{Synthetic Queries and Retriever Ranking Shifts.}
LLM-generated queries are widely used to train dense retrievers \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and to construct automated RAG benchmarks \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas}. Several studies have examined whether synthetic benchmarks reproduce real retriever rankings. Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and van Elburg et al.\ \cite{vanelburg2025synthetic} analyze system-ranking correlations and generator biases across synthetic test collections. Kucia and Gawlik \cite{kucia2026beyond} compare $1{,}851$ LLM-generated synthetic questions against $322$ student survey questions for a university faculty information system across dense, lexical (BM25), and hybrid (RRF) retrievers, showing that synthetic queries overestimate absolute performance and shift system rankings relative to human questions. Because ranking shifts between real and synthetic queries are already established in prior work, we treat our community-QA retrieval audit (Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}) as a complementary diagnostic that separates document-subsampling variance from within-document lexical copying.

\paragraph{Schema-Driven RAG Synthesis and Question Evaluation.}
To ground synthetic queries in domain structure, RAGEval \cite{zhu2025rageval} extracts domain schemas from financial, medical, and legal documents to generate evaluation sets, while DataMorgana \cite{filice2025datamorgana} parameterizes user personas and question categories for RAG benchmarking. For general question generation, QGEval \cite{fu2024qgeval} benchmarks multi-dimensional quality across seven criteria: fluency, clarity, conciseness, relevance, consistency, answerability, and answer consistency. However, open-loop schema prompting and surface answerability judges do not compile clause thresholds into a formal reachability check. As our multi-threshold experiments in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} show, even when a prompt explicitly lists every required slot, a 1.5B generator still overwrites user durations with clause thresholds in $25.0\%\text{--}31.2\%$ of cases unless checked by a symbolic verifier.

\paragraph{Rule-Grounded Clarification, Risk Control, and Insurance Benchmarks.}
In conversational QA, ShARC \cite{saeidi2018sharc} studies rule-grounded answering where an assistant must ask follow-up questions when decision variables are underspecified. Recent work such as CLAMBER \cite{zhang2024clamber} and AskBench \cite{zhao2026askbench} benchmarks how well LLMs detect ambiguous user requests and ask clarifying questions in open-domain and clinical dialogues. From a selective prediction perspective, RC-RAG \cite{chen2024rcrag} uses counterfactual prompting to estimate RAG confidence and control risk via selective abstention under misleading contexts. In the insurance domain, InsuranceQA \cite{feng2015insuranceqa} provides community QA pairs for answer selection, and InsQABench \cite{ding2025insqabench} evaluates LLM question answering over insurance product rules. Our focus is upstream of assistant answering: rather than testing whether a downstream chatbot asks clarifying questions on underspecified prompts, we verify whether \emph{synthetic evaluation queries} retain the conditions needed to keep their reference labels determinate during benchmark creation.

\paragraph{Statistical Calibration with Small Gold Samples.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction}, ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}, and PRECISE \cite{divekar2026precise} combine synthetic data and automated LLM judges with small human-labeled calibration sets to construct valid confidence intervals for RAG evaluation metrics. When synthetic queries are generated from a small subset of corpus documents without paired real queries across the full index, unpaired shrinkage and marginal query-propensity weighting fail to correct document-subsampling and query-to-source overlap biases; we report these negative calibration results alongside a heuristic bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.

\subsection{Data Provenance, Licensing Scope, and Inference-Only Setup}
Table~\ref{tab:data_audit} documents the origin, scope, and usage constraints of all study materials:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-Only Evaluation}: No generative LLM or embedding model weights are pre-trained, instruction-tuned, or fine-tuned in this work. All local runs (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} and \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5}) use frozen weights under deterministic \texttt{float32/eager} inference, and cloud API runs (\texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}) use \texttt{temperature}=0. Few-shot prompts provide three fixed calibration examples in context. The only fitted statistical parameters in our code are corpus TF-IDF/BM25 term frequencies and a 2-fold logistic regression classifier used for propensity-weighting diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.
  \item \textbf{Official Insurance Clauses versus Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rules}: The nine insurance policies ($5$ development policies in Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies} and $4$ extension policies in Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies}) are official PDF wordings downloaded from public disclosure portals of Ping An, CPIC, and Taikang. Because public web availability does not confer a license to redistribute full commercial contracts, our repository releases SHA256 file hashes, source URLs, PDF page and bounding-box coordinates, and short verification excerpts rather than raw PDF binaries. In contrast, the four multi-threshold rules in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} are researcher-authored synthetic clauses designed for controlled stress testing.
  \item \textbf{Community QA Corpus (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv})}: Used in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}, this file ($8{,}362$ raw rows; $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid questions after normalization) comes from \texttt{meowcao/InsuranceModel}, which mirrors \texttt{SophonPlus/ChineseNlpCorpus} (\texttt{datasets/baoxianzhidao/intro.ipynb}). Upstream documentation states that the pairs were collected from Baidu Zhidao insurance Q\&A with original post URLs removed and sensitive fields filtered. Consequently, answers reflect historical community responses rather than actuarial ground truth, no verified open redistribution license chain is documented upstream, and Chinese foundation models may have encountered parts of this text during pretraining.
  \item \textbf{Proxy Parser Scope, Boundary Risks, and Manual-Inspection Watchlist}: Formally, our reachability verifier is exact \emph{relative to the extracted observation frame} $\hat{z} = g(q')$, where $g(\cdot)$ is a synonym-aware regular-expression slot parser that also resolves explicit contrastive corrections (\texttt{不是/并非/并不是 A，而是 B}), rejects negated numeric durations (\texttt{不到/未满 N 天}), and flags multi-value day conflicts (\texttt{第20天或第45天}). Because $g(\cdot)$ is a rule-based extractor rather than a full semantic parser, it faces two boundary risks: \textbf{false-reject errors} (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}), when a rewrite uses uncataloged colloquial paraphrases or omits formal rule titles while retaining domain keywords, and \textbf{false-accept errors} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}), if complex multi-clause discourse or long-range negation falls outside the regex scope rules. We provide two supplementary artifacts without claiming completed human validation: (i)~blinded two-rater HTML annotation packages (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, which remain uncompleted by external raters and are not reported as human results), and (ii)~an automated verifier explanation and 29-item manual-inspection watchlist across all $264$ primary generated questions (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}), which logs deterministic parser traces and flags format or phrasing edge cases for future human review without claiming an external semantic accuracy rate.
\end{enumerate}

Each development scenario frame specifies up to six fields: product identity ($20/20$ seeds), enrollment type (first-time purchase vs.\ renewal, $20/20$), policy effective day ($20/20$), claim cause (illness vs.\ accidental injury, $20/20$), selected plan waiting period ($4/20$ seeds on \texttt{cpic\_86017}), and insurer-approved waiting-period waiver ($3/20$ renewal seeds). From $20$ seed scenarios, we construct $80$ controlled test cases across four variants: \emph{complete} (all required slots stated), \emph{surface} (synonymous phrasing), \emph{missing-critical} (one pivotal condition removed, certified as requiring clarification via two completion witnesses that yield opposite decisions), and \emph{counterfactual} (one condition altered to flip the rule outcome between waiting-period exclusion and coverage).

Because a seed scenario can contain slots that are non-pivotal for its specific state---for example, on \texttt{pa\_h865} (30-day wait), a claim occurring on Day~45 satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting whether the cause was illness or accident still leads to coverage under every completion---we evaluate two distinct criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{All-Slot Completeness}: Every required field of the reference seed is extracted without omission or value conflict.
  \item \textbf{Reference-Independent Decision Determinacy}: Using only the slots extracted from the generated question and the public policy rules (without reading the reference seed's required-field list), the verifier enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unmentioned fields. Elapsed-day domains are partitioned into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t$, $= t$, $> t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ using representative days $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$), and all schema fields---including optional waiver overrides---are checked for value conflicts. A rewrite is classified as:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{Preserved Decision} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}) if no observed schema field conflicts with the seed and the set of reachable decisions across all admissible completions is the singleton matching the reference outcome;
    \item \emph{Condition Mutated / Decision Flip} (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} / \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP}) if any observed schema field conflicts with the seed or if the reachable set is a singleton with the opposite coverage decision;
    \item \emph{Degraded to Clarification} (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}) if both coverage and exclusion are reachable across completions ($|\text{reachable}| > 1$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Paired Rewriting Across Three Qwen Tiers}
We pass the $20$ seed questions and their source clause excerpts to three off-the-shelf generators---local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} (\texttt{temperature}=0, parameter counts undisclosed by the provider)---under two prompting conditions ($120$ total rewrites):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conversational Style (\texttt{ordinary\_style})}: Instructs the model to rewrite the seed question into a short, natural everyday user inquiry while keeping the original intent.
  \item \textbf{Condition-Constrained Prompt (\texttt{condition\_prompt})}: Explicitly instructs the model to retain the full product title, first-time vs.\ renewal status, effective day, illness vs.\ accident cause, coverage scope, plan waiting period, and waiver approval status without adding or omitting conditions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Development Policy Findings}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} reveal three key patterns:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{High Retrieval Accuracy Coexists with Severe Decision Loss Under Conversational Rewriting}: Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, cloud models achieve $80\%\text{--}90\%$ Top-1 clause retrieval, yet standalone decision determinacy is only $30\%$ ($6/20$) on \texttt{1.5B}, $0\%$ ($0/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and $45\%$ ($9/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}.   \item \textbf{Numeric Mutations on \texttt{1.5B} versus Conversational Compression on Cloud Models}: On \texttt{1.5B}, $3/20$ conversational rewrites (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016}) corrupt ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2), and two seeds with opposite ground-truth outcomes (\texttt{condition-009} with a 7-day wait on Day~10 vs.\ \texttt{condition-010} with a 25-day wait on Day~10) collapse into the exact same question after dropping the plan waiting period. On \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, numeric value mutations are $0/20$ in this sample (a finite-sample observation on $n=20$ seeds rather than proof of a zero population rate), but both cloud models omit administrative qualifiers to sound natural: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the policy title in $20/20$ seeds, and \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops the policy title in $10/20$ seeds and replaces ``首次投保'' (first-time purchase) with ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which does not distinguish first purchase from renewal) in $6/20$ seeds. When \texttt{Qwen-Max} keeps the product title ($10/20$ seeds), $5$ of those seeds omit enrollment status on non-waivable clauses or post-waiting-period days where the coverage decision is still invariant, explaining the gap between all-slot match ($4/20$, $20\%$) and formal decision determinacy ($9/20$, $45\%$).
  \item \textbf{Explicit Slot Constraints Restore Determinacy}: Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, standalone decision determinacy rises to $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on both \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} ($100\%$ across all three models when policy identity is granted). On \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, one constrained rewrite omits the illness/accident cause on a post-waiting-period seed where both completions lead to coverage, yielding $19/20$ all-slot match but $20/20$ decision determinacy. On \texttt{1.5B}, the single \texttt{condition\_prompt} failure (\texttt{condition-008} on \texttt{pa\_h865}) omits the product title while retaining the renewal waiver and effective day, so it is classified as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} standalone and \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} when the source policy is granted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Extended Evaluation on Four Additional Official Insurance Policies}
\label{sec:heldout_policies}

\subsection{Benefit-Specific Clause Modeling and Iterative Parser Refinement}
To test our verification workflow beyond the five development contracts in Table~\ref{tab:clauses}, we collected four additional official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_clauses\_sources.json}). Extending the development verifier to these four contracts exposed two modeling requirements that were not present in the initial five-policy schema: (i)~a single contract can assign different waiting periods and exemption rules to distinct \emph{benefit types} rather than treating the waiting period as an arbitrary user-chosen number, and (ii)~when textbook book-title brackets (\texttt{《》}) and section headers are stripped, rewrites frequently refer to products by shortened business aliases. We therefore updated the shared parser (\texttt{extract\_fields\_surface\_normalized} in \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}) to extract benefit types (\texttt{benefit\_id}) and colloquial product aliases before scoring the $16$ seeds ($64$ controlled cases). Because these schema and parser updates were informed by inspecting the four extension contracts, we report the results in this section as a \textbf{retrospective post-refinement evaluation} rather than a blind zero-shot test of an untouched parser.

Each of the four extension contracts is anchored to Page~1, Article~4 (``第四条 保险责任'') of its official PDF (verified by full-file SHA256 hashes in \texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_clauses\_sources.json}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{pa\_2011\_36} (\emph{Ping An Worry-Free Inpatient Medical Insurance} / 平安住院安心医疗保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{10916a68758a}): defines two distinct benefits with benefit-specific waiting periods and exemption rules:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{General Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{general\_hospital}, ``（一）一般住院医疗津贴''): 30-day waiting period; accidental injury hospitalization (\texttt{accident\_exempt=True}) or continuous renewal (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}) has no waiting period.
    \item \emph{Cancer Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{cancer\_hospital}, ``（二）癌症住院医疗津贴''): 90-day waiting period for first-time cancer diagnosis and hospitalization; continuous renewal has no waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}), whereas no accidental-injury exemption applies (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \end{itemize}
  Under this benefit-resolved model, on Days~$31\text{--}90$ where the waiting period determines the outcome (e.g., first-time sickness claims without an approved renewal waiver that exempts both benefits), omitting which benefit is claimed---or conflating the two benefits by asking whether the claim should be settled under the 30-day general inpatient allowance or the 90-day cancer inpatient allowance---yields reachable set $[\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}, \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}]$ and is classified as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} (whereas if an explicit renewal waiver exempts both benefits, omitting the benefit leaves the waiting-period outcome invariant). Any question that pairs the general allowance with a 90-day wait or the cancer allowance with a 30-day wait is flagged as \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0254} (\emph{Ping An Inpatient Medical Insurance} / 平安住院医疗保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{d6ad23849fec}): 90-day waiting period for first-time or non-continuous sickness inpatient medical expense reimbursement; accidental injury treatment and continuous renewal have no waiting period.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0321} (\emph{Ping An Lung Cancer Disease Insurance} / 平安肺癌疾病保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{e04e0b452858}): a single-disease policy with a 30-day waiting period for first-time diagnosis of primary lung cancer, explicitly excluding carcinoma in situ (``初次发生原发性肺癌，上述原发性肺癌不包括原位癌''); continuous renewal or other agreement exempts the waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}), whereas accidental injury does not waive the disease waiting period (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \item \texttt{cpic\_93896} (\emph{CPIC Additional [Guangxi] Student \& Child Inpatient Medical Insurance [2025 Edition]} / 附加（广西地区）学生幼儿住院医疗保险（2025版）, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{309d8f029fb8}): Article~4 (Page~1) specifies a 30-day waiting period for illness hospitalization and states that accidental injury and renewal are not subject to the 30-day limit (``因遭受意外伤害或者自本附加险合同生效之日起30日（续保不受30日规定的限制）后因疾病住院治疗''), while Article~6 (Page~3) specifies that the contract is a one-year non-guaranteed renewal rider (``本产品为不保证续保产品''). To avoid conflating guaranteed and non-guaranteed renewal semantics, all four evaluation seeds on \texttt{cpic\_93896} test first-time enrollment (\texttt{enrollment=first}), where the 30-day illness waiting period and accidental-injury exemption apply directly.
\end{enumerate}

We construct $16$ seed scenarios ($4$ per policy) and $64$ controlled test cases (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_controlled\_cases.jsonl}). In each case record, we strip textbook book-title brackets (\texttt{《》}) and explicit clause-number prefixes as a surface formatting normalization (while noting that retaining full product names or aliases and exact conditions means these remain controlled synthetic items rather than organic customer queries). Each case record separates contract-clause evidence (\texttt{source\_type=pdf\_contract\_clause}, linked to the PDF filename, page number, and bounding-box coordinates \texttt{bbox\_pdf\_points}) from synthetic scenario parameters (\texttt{source\_type=synthetic\_seed\_frame}, such as elapsed days and enrollment status, which originate from the seed frame rather than the PDF), and includes two opposite-outcome completion witnesses on every \texttt{missing\_critical} case. Under the unified parser and reachability verifier, all $64/64$ controlled cases match their constructed labels ($16/16$ \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} on \texttt{complete} and \texttt{surface}, $16/16$ \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} on \texttt{missing\_critical}, and $16/16$ \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} on \texttt{counterfactual}), with zero parser discrepancies against \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}.

\subsection{Extension Policy Results and Title-Masked Retrieval}
Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance} reports the evaluation on the four extension policies (\texttt{data\_v3/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_summary.json}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]

\section{Equal-Call-Budget Comparison on Multi-Threshold Rules}
\label{sec:multithreshold}

\subsection{Synthetic Multi-Threshold Testbed and Evaluation Protocol}
While single-clause insurance rewrites already achieve $100\%$ decision preservation on the first pass under \texttt{condition\_prompt} (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance}), multi-threshold rules with competing numeric windows place heavier demands on small local generators. To test whether closed-loop symbolic verification provides value beyond open-loop prompting and unguided LLM self-refinement under a matched call budget, we evaluate a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark (\texttt{src/cross\_domain\_study.py} and \texttt{src/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_study.py}). Unlike the nine official insurance policy PDFs in Sections~\ref{sec:dev_policies}--\ref{sec:heldout_policies}, these four clauses are researcher-authored synthetic rules inspired by clinical pathways and PRC labor statutes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (combining T790M status, a 14-day washout threshold after prior therapy, and an emergency specialist waiver); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (contrasting a 10-day knee replacement window against a 35-day hip replacement window, with active bleeding contraindication and hematology consult override).
  \item \textbf{Labor-Law-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (inspired by PRC Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14(6) on non-principal commuting fault and Art.~17 contrasting the 30-day employer filing deadline against the 365-day employee/kin deadline and administrative extension); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (inspired by PRC Labor Contract Law Art.~24 and SPC Judicial Interpretation~I Art.~38 on 90-day default of post-termination non-compete compensation, augmented with a synthetic supplemental grace-period agreement).
\end{itemize}

For each seed, unobserved durations are expanded over a complete threshold partition ($\{7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 30, 32, 35, 45, 90, 120, 365, 400\}$ covering $< t, = t, > t$ for all thresholds $\{10, 14, 30, 35, 90, 365\}$), and all six schema fields are checked for mutations and reachability without consulting reference seed metadata.

We compare seven methods on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=160):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{seed\_passthrough} ($0$ calls): deterministic template question prior to LLM rewriting (information-preservation upper bound, zero template novelty).
  \item \texttt{ordinary\_style} ($16$ calls): 1-call unconstrained conversational rewrite.
  \item \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($16$ calls): 1-call open-loop slot-constrained rewrite.
  \item \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($16$ calls): 1-call prompt instructing the model to output a single-line JSON object of all 6 slot values on Line~1 and \texttt{QUESTION: <question>} on Line~2.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (budget-matched $26$ calls): takes the 16 \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts and runs a second unguided LLM self-check pass (asking the model to verify all conditions against the source clause and seed question \emph{without symbolic parser diagnostics}) on the exact same $10$ seeds triggered by \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}. Sharing the trigger schedule isolates the effect of symbolic slot feedback in the repair prompt while holding both the total call budget ($26$ calls) and the refined seed subset fixed.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (all seeds, $32$ calls): runs the unguided second-pass LLM self-check across all $16$ seeds without consulting the symbolic parser trigger.
  \item \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($26$ calls): runs the symbolic slot parser on the $16$ \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts, triggers a second pass on the $10$ seeds where any slot is missing or mutated, and supplies the exact missing/mutated slot names and target values in the repair prompt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Threshold Findings and Statistical Scope}
Table~\ref{tab:crossdomain} summarizes four findings:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{100\% Top-1 Retrieval Coexists with 93.8\% Standalone Decision Loss (and 56.2\% Granted-Clause Loss)}: All six non-JSON methods achieve $100\%$ ($16/16$) Top-1 retrieval on both \texttt{bm25\_char2} and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} under both standard and title-masked evaluation because the four clauses have distinct domain vocabulary. Consequently, under \textbf{Granted-Clause Sensitivity} (\texttt{grant\_source\_clause=True}, where \texttt{clause\_id} is supplied by the source context or matched via domain-unique non-compete phrases), \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} rises from $12/16$ ($75.0\%$) to \textbf{$15/16$ ($93.8\%$)}, whereas \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($7/16$) and \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} ($8/16$) remain unchanged.

Because the 4-policy extension in Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies} was evaluated after adding colloquial aliases and \texttt{benefit\_id} disambiguation, and the 16-seed testbed in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} had limited single-set sample size ($N=16$), we conducted an expanded confirmation study (\texttt{src/frozen\_confirmation\_and\_rag\_study.py}) over $11$ zero-alias confirmation official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{pa\_h716}, \texttt{pa\_2651}, \texttt{pa\_l0001}, \texttt{pa\_1714}, \texttt{pa\_1072}, \texttt{pa\_596}, \texttt{cpic\_107082}, \texttt{cpic\_106702}, \texttt{cpic\_60201}, \texttt{cpic\_79920}, \texttt{cpic\_85050}) plus $6$ newly collected untouched post-hardening holdout Ping An policy PDFs (\texttt{pa\_2650}, \texttt{pa\_1071}, \texttt{pa\_1073}, \texttt{pa\_595}, \texttt{pa\_597}, \texttt{pa\_l0002}, bringing total official policy PDFs to $26$), a 120-case constructive gold diagnostic suite, a 40-seed ($160$-case) confirmation benchmark, and a 24-seed ($48$-pair) untouched post-hardening holdout evaluated across two distinct model families: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} (DashScope endpoint \texttt{qwen-plus}, returning \texttt{response\_model="qwen-plus"}) and \texttt{DeepSeek} (official \texttt{https://api.deepseek.com/chat/completions} endpoint with \texttt{model="deepseek-chat"}, which routes to \texttt{response\_model="deepseek-flash"} with \texttt{system\_fingerprint="e72d3c7f663d3e1383b00b3884cb4501"}).

When a non-threshold-crossing numeric edit occurs within the same waiting-period partition (e.g., Day~20 $\rightarrow$ Day~21 on a 30-day waiting period), forward reachability preserves \texttt{specific\_decision = WAIT\_EXCLUDED} and labels the case \texttt{FACT\_MUTATED\_SAME\_DECISION} rather than falsely flipping the eligibility decision to \texttt{DECISION\_CHANGED}. \subsection{Strictly Isolated Retrieval + Symbolic Rule-Decision Pipeline Selection Impact}
To guarantee zero reference-label leakage during downstream benchmarking, all five candidate retrieval + symbolic rule-decision pipelines execute through the isolated top-level interface \texttt{predict(question, retrieved\_clause, inference\_config)}, which receives only the query text, the top-1 retrieved clause ID (with $100\%$ pure \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} dense vector cache coverage across all $80$ reference queries [$48$ insurance + $32$ multi-threshold] and all generated queries, raising a \texttt{KeyError} on any uncached string rather than falling back to lexical overlap, and applying an explicit lexical zero-overlap guard that returns \texttt{None} when $\text{BM25}(q, d) \le 0$ so completely out-of-vocabulary inputs abstain rather than matching an arbitrary dense neighbor), and public clause tables (\texttt{data\_v3/downstream\_rag\_selection\_study.json}). Perturbing \texttt{seed['reference\_decision']} while holding the query and retrieved clause fixed leaves every system's prediction strictly invariant (\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} $= \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}$). Across all $80$ reference cases, oracle system accuracy ranks \textbf{\texttt{S1\_dense\_rule\_clarify} ($97.50\%$, $78/80$) $>$ \texttt{S2\_bm25\_rule\_clarify} ($93.75\%$, $75/80$) $>$ \texttt{S5\_dense\_threshold\_echo\_heuristic} ($41.25\%$, $33/80$) $>$ \texttt{S3\_dense\_aggressive\_deny} ($35.00\%$, $28/80$) $>$ \texttt{S4\_dense\_aggressive\_approve} ($31.25\%$, $25/80$)}. Unverified \texttt{ordinary\_style} rewrites across all $40$ confirmation seeds (\texttt{combined\_40\_seeds}) suffer $97.5\%\text{--}100.0\%$ label corruption, penalizing clarifying pipelines \texttt{S1}/\texttt{S2} to $0.0\%\text{--}2.5\%$ and selecting \texttt{S3\_dense\_aggressive\_deny} ($50.0\%$), which inverts system rankings (\textbf{Kendall's $\tau = -0.7746$, Spearman's $\rho = -0.8660$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and $\tau = -0.8367$, $\rho = -0.8944$ on \texttt{DeepSeek}}, incurring a \textbf{$0.6250$ selection regret}). Both \texttt{verify\_only\_filter} and \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} eliminate label corruption ($0.0\%$) and restore the exact ground-truth ranking ($\tau = 1.0000$, $\rho = 1.0000$, zero regret), with \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} retaining $97.5\%$ ($39/40$) scenario coverage versus $82.5\%\text{--}92.5\%$ under \texttt{verify\_only\_filter}.

\section{Discussion, Practical Recommendations, and Limitations}
\paragraph{Practical Recommendations for Rule-Governed RAG Evaluation.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Do not treat retrieval Top-1 or surface fluency as evidence of label validity}: Across $26$ official insurance contracts and multi-threshold rules, $75\%\text{--}100\%$ Top-1 retrieval coexists with $55\%\text{--}100\%$ rates of decision ambiguity or mutation under conversational rewriting, directly inverting downstream pipeline rankings ($\tau \in [-0.8367, -0.7746]$) if seed labels are inherited without verification.
  \item \textbf{Separate slot completeness, question ambiguity (\texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED}), and parser syntax boundaries (\texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN})}: Threshold-partitioned reachability verification distinguishes harmless omissions of non-pivotal slots from pivotal omissions that require clarification, while routing out-of-grammar durations and unseen disease branches to \texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN} via a text-only gate ($0.0\%$ semantic false-accept rate across the 120-case diagnostic suite and 400-query dual-implementation consistency check).
  \item \textbf{Combine symbolic verification with targeted slot repair (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) rather than filtering or single-pass JSON rewriting alone}: While \texttt{verify\_only\_filter} drops $7.5\%\text{--}18.75\%$ of seeds, 160-token \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} suffers $17.5\%$ mid-sentence truncations on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and untruncated ($320$-token) or schema-rendered JSON baselines still suffer $8.75\%\text{--}12.5\%$ rejections from contract-level waiver-text echoing, schema omissions, or unmodeled organ lexicon, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} reaches $97.5\%$ ($78/80$) usable yield on the 40-seed confirmation set and $100.0\%$ ($48/48$) on the 24-seed untouched holdout.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, while we expanded our benchmark to $26$ official Chinese insurance policy PDFs ($5$ dev, $4$ extension, $11$ confirmation, and $6$ untouched holdout; author engineering compilation time estimate $11.72$~min/contract across $20$ contracts, documented as an author engineering record rather than a multi-participant stopwatch study) and $4$ multi-threshold clauses across two model families (\texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{DeepSeek}), our verifiers rely on semi-automated symbolic schemas for waiting-period and multi-threshold rules rather than fully automated extraction for complex multi-benefit claims (e.g., pre-existing condition grading or deductible tiers). Second, our Layer-3 audit of $400$ generated queries is a shared-rule-base dual-implementation consistency check (\texttt{shared\_rule\_base\_dual\_implementation\_consistency\_check}) rather than an external multi-rater blinded human annotation, and our downstream evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation} tests strictly isolated retrieval + symbolic rule-decision pipelines (\texttt{predict(question, retrieved\_clause, inference\_config)}) rather than open-ended LLM free-text generation. Third, as shown by our 120-case gold diagnostic suite, the regex parser achieves $0.0\%$ false-accept and $0.0\%$ false-reject rates on day-unit expressions (Categories~1--7) but conservatively routes month/week units, calendar-date arithmetic, and unseen disease-specific branches (\texttt{pa\_h716}) to \texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}. Finally, the community QA corpus in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval} (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}) carries noisy community best-answer labels, unverified redistribution licensing, and potential pretraining exposure.
