\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) benchmarks often synthesize test queries from source passages using instruction-tuned language models. In rule-governed insurance workflows, however, a rewrite can rank the source clause at Top-1 while omitting or altering conditions that determine whether a claim is covered, excluded, or requires clarification. Across $26$ official Chinese insurance policy wordings and $8$ multi-threshold rule families ($88$ seeds across $25$ clause clusters and $21$ source documents, $N = 176$ cross-model pairs) evaluated with Qwen and DeepSeek models without fine-tuning, we compile eligibility rules into a reachability verifier that separates slot completeness from decision determinacy and routes out-of-grammar phrasing via a text-only uncertainty gate. Across all $176$ pairs, parser-guided slot repair reaches $98.86\%$ ($174/176$) symbolic usability—outperforming all six LLM baselines under 21-document-clustered permutation tests after Holm-Bonferroni correction (all $p <= 0.0156$). Auditing against a blinded three-flagship panel (protocol-guided 3-way Fleiss' kappa $= 0.8578$, zero-hint pure-verbatim kappa $= 0.8366$; not human annotation) confirms $91.48\%$ ($161/176$) panel usability—beating conversational ($27.27\%$) and truncated/5-slot JSON baselines ($67.05\%\text{--}73.86\%$) while matching explicit-constraint prompting ($91.48\%\text{--}92.61\%$)—and $94.1\%\text{--}100.0\%$ zero-shot schema extraction across $17$ contracts. Across nine cloud LLM RAG pipelines, verified synthesis reduces evaluation MAE from $0.53\text{--}0.66$ to $0.04\text{--}0.07$ and prevents ranking reversal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating a vertical-domain retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system often begins before real user traffic is available. A standard cold-start practice in retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas,zhu2025rageval,filice2025datamorgana} is to prompt an instruction-tuned language model to turn domain passages into synthetic user questions, filter the outputs for surface fluency, and use the resulting benchmark to rank retrievers or score end-to-end answers.

Prior work has shown that synthetic queries can shift system rankings relative to real user queries \cite{rahmani2024syndl,vanelburg2025synthetic,kucia2026beyond}. In rule-governed settings such as insurance underwriting and claims review, a more direct problem arises at the level of individual test cases: retrieval metrics reward lexical and topical similarity, whereas coverage decisions depend on discrete eligibility conditions. Under Chinese commercial health insurance clauses, whether a claim is excluded by a waiting period depends on the exact product and benefit claimed, whether the policy is a first-time purchase or a continuous renewal, how many days have elapsed since the policy took effect, whether hospitalization resulted from illness or accidental injury, and whether the insurer approved a waiting-period waiver.

When a language model rewrites a structured eligibility scenario into a natural-sounding user question, two distinct failure modes occur even when the rewrite ranks the source clause at Top-1:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Ambiguity Degradation (Clarification Required)}: To make a question sound conversational and brief, models frequently drop product titles, benefit types, enrollment status, or elapsed days. Omitting a \emph{pivotal} slot turns a decidable test case into an underspecified inquiry under which both coverage and exclusion remain possible. Conversely, omitting a \emph{non-pivotal} slot---for example, dropping whether a claim arose from illness or accident after the waiting period has already expired---leaves the coverage outcome unchanged. Simple slot-matching rules conflate pivotal and non-pivotal omissions, while retrieval scores ignore both.
  \item \textbf{Condition Mutation and Decision Flip}: Smaller models and open-loop prompts often alter numeric durations (for instance, turning ``Day~20'' into ``Day~2'') or overwrite the policyholder's actual elapsed days ($7$, $45$, or $120$ days) with the statutory waiting-period threshold stated in the clause ($14$ or $90$ days). Such edits increase lexical overlap with the target document while reversing the ground-truth eligibility decision.
\end{enumerate}

This paper asks a focused diagnostic question: \emph{when synthetic queries are generated from rule-bearing clauses, do they preserve the underlying eligibility decision, and can lightweight symbolic verification detect and repair decision drift?} Rather than proposing a new model architecture or claiming automated rule extraction from arbitrary legal prose, we formalize hand-compiled waiting-period rules over traceable policy clauses and separate surface slot completeness from reference-independent decision determinacy. Specifically, our verifier enumerates all admissible completions of any omitted fields over threshold-partitioned equivalence regions without consulting reference seed metadata, classifying each synthetic rewrite as preserving the unique reference decision, mutating a condition (or flipping the unique decision), or degrading into an ambiguous query accompanied by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses.

Our empirical analysis covers five connected settings, with supplementary community-QA retrieval diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Development Policy Evaluation Across Model Scales (Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies})}: On five official Chinese insurance policy PDFs ($20$ seed scenarios, $80$ controlled cases, and $120$ paired rewrites across local Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and cloud Qwen-Plus and Qwen-Max), we measure how unconstrained conversational rewriting versus explicit slot prompting affects slot retention and decision determinacy. Conversational rewrites preserve standalone decisions on only $30\%$, $0\%$, and $45\%$ of seeds across the three model scales, whereas explicit condition constraints reach $95\%\text{--}100\%$.
  \item \textbf{Extended Policy Evaluation and Benefit-Specific Waiting Periods (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies})}: We extend our evaluation to four additional official insurance policy PDFs ($16$ seeds, $64$ controlled cases) by incorporating benefit-specific waiting periods within a single contract (e.g., general inpatient allowance with a 30-day wait and accident exemption versus cancer inpatient allowance with a 90-day wait in \texttt{pa\_2011\_36}) and colloquial product aliases into the shared parser. Under this post-refinement evaluation, unconstrained conversational rewrites preserve $0/16$ standalone decisions ($5/16$ when the policy identity is given), while constrained prompting preserves $16/16$ on the first pass (requiring $0$ repair calls). Title-masked retrieval across all nine clauses further shows that standard retrieval scores rely heavily on product-title strings rather than eligibility conditions.
  \item \textbf{Equal-Budget Repair on Multi-Threshold Rules (Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold})}: On a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark where all non-JSON rewrites reach $100\%$ Top-1 retrieval, we compare symbolic parser-guided repair against an equal-budget unguided self-refinement baseline ($26$ LLM calls). Symbolic feedback raises standalone decision preservation from $8/16$ ($50.0\%$) to $12/16$ ($75.0\%$, exact two-sided McNemar $p=0.125$) and reduces parser-detected condition mutations from $4/16$ ($25.0\%$) to $0/16$ ($0.0\%$); in a post-hoc sensitivity decomposition where the source clause identity is granted at evaluation time, conversational rewrites rise from $1/16$ to $7/16$ and parser-guided repair reaches $15/16$ versus $8/16$ for self-refinement (exact McNemar $p=0.0156$).
  \item \textbf{Dual-Implementation Consistency Check, Cross-Model Confirmation, and Rule-Based Pipeline Selection (Section~\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation})}: We evaluate $11$ zero-alias confirmation policy PDFs ($40$ seeds, $160$ cases) and $6$ post-hardening extension policy PDFs ($24$ seeds, $48$ cross-model pairs) across \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{DeepSeek}. A text-only uncertainty gate separates parser syntax boundaries (\texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}) from question ambiguity (\texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED}). Across a 120-case diagnostic suite and a 400-query shared-rule-base consistency check, the verifier records a $0.0\%$ semantic false-accept rate, while $6$ format-truncation false accepts occur in 160-token JSON rewriting ($0/80$ on \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}). On the confirmation set, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} reaches $97.5\%$ ($78/80$) joint usability, outperforming single-pass prompting and equal-budget self-refinement (both $87.5\%$, $70/80$) and showing a positive trend over 320-token JSON rewriting ($91.25\%$, $73/80$, $p = 0.0625$). In downstream evaluation using a reference-free inference interface, unverified conversational rewrites invert pipeline rankings ($\tau \in [-0.8367, -0.7746]$), whereas verified filtering and repair recover the reference ordering ($\tau = 1.0000$).
  \item \textbf{Blinded Three-Flagship Adjudication (Protocol-Guided \& Pure-Verbatim), Nine Real LLM RAG Pipelines, and Verbatim PDF Non-Waiting-Period Rule Generalization (Section~\ref{sec:ccf_a_upgrade})}: To decouple evaluation from the symbolic regex parser and test beyond waiting periods, we evaluate all $88$ seeds ($N=1{,}408$ core records plus $160$ template records) with a blinded three-flagship panel (\texttt{Kimi-K3}, \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Pro}, and \texttt{Qwen3.8-Max}) under both a protocol-guided cross-check ($264$ batches; binary Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7411$, 3-way semantic $\kappa = 0.8578$ on confirmation; $87.19\%$ verifier agreement) and a strictly zero-hint pure-verbatim protocol ($120$ confirmation batches + $72$ non-waiting batches reading only verbatim PDF excerpts and universal 3-class task definitions; binary $\kappa = 0.7008$, 3-way semantic $\kappa = 0.8366$, $96.56\%$ majority agreement with protocol-guided adjudication), audit uncontaminated zero-shot waiting-period schema extraction across all $17$ waiting-period PDFs ($17/17$ [$100\%$] 3-field exact match on \texttt{qwen3.8-max} and \texttt{kimi-k3}, $16/17$ [$94.12\%$] on \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}), evaluate nine end-to-end cloud LLM RAG pipelines ($4{,}067$ reader calls including controlled same-model abstention vs.\ forced-binary pairs and a 48-query external holdout), and extend to three verbatim PDF-grounded non-waiting-period insurance rule families ($24$ seeds, $N=48$ pairs). Pooled across all $88$ seeds ($N=176$ pairs across $25$ clause clusters and $21$ independent source documents), \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} achieves $174/176$ ($98.86\%$) symbolic usability (beating all six LLM baselines at $p_{\text{Holm,doc}} \le 0.015625$) and $161/176$ ($91.48\%$) panel usability (significantly beating conversational [$48/176$], 160-token JSON [$118/176$], and 5-slot schema rendering [$130/176$] while matching explicit-constraint baselines [$161\text{--}163/176$, $p \ge 0.50$]).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Dataset Provenance}

\paragraph{Synthetic Queries and Retriever Ranking Shifts.}
LLM-generated queries are widely used to train dense retrievers \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and to construct automated RAG benchmarks \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas}. Several studies have examined whether synthetic benchmarks reproduce real retriever rankings. Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and van Elburg et al.\ \cite{vanelburg2025synthetic} analyze system-ranking correlations and generator biases across synthetic test collections. Kucia and Gawlik \cite{kucia2026beyond} compare $1{,}851$ LLM-generated synthetic questions against $322$ student survey questions for a university faculty information system across dense, lexical (BM25), and hybrid (RRF) retrievers, showing that synthetic queries overestimate absolute performance and shift system rankings relative to human questions. Because ranking shifts between real and synthetic queries are already established in prior work, we treat our community-QA retrieval audit (Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}) as a complementary diagnostic that separates document-subsampling variance from within-document lexical copying.

\paragraph{Schema-Driven RAG Synthesis and Question Evaluation.}
To ground synthetic queries in domain structure, RAGEval \cite{zhu2025rageval} extracts domain schemas from financial, medical, and legal documents to generate evaluation sets, while DataMorgana \cite{filice2025datamorgana} parameterizes user personas and question categories for RAG benchmarking. For general question generation, QGEval \cite{fu2024qgeval} benchmarks multi-dimensional quality across seven criteria: fluency, clarity, conciseness, relevance, consistency, answerability, and answer consistency. However, open-loop schema prompting and surface answerability judges do not compile clause thresholds into a formal reachability check. As our multi-threshold experiments in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} show, even when a prompt explicitly lists every required slot, a 1.5B generator still overwrites user durations with clause thresholds in $25.0\%\text{--}31.2\%$ of cases unless checked by a symbolic verifier.

\paragraph{Rule-Grounded Clarification, Risk Control, and Insurance Benchmarks.}
In conversational QA, ShARC \cite{saeidi2018sharc} studies rule-grounded answering where an assistant must ask follow-up questions when decision variables are underspecified. Recent work such as CLAMBER \cite{zhang2024clamber} and AskBench \cite{zhao2026askbench} benchmarks how well LLMs detect ambiguous user requests and ask clarifying questions in open-domain and clinical dialogues. From a selective prediction perspective, RC-RAG \cite{chen2024rcrag} uses counterfactual prompting to estimate RAG confidence and control risk via selective abstention under misleading contexts. In the insurance domain, InsuranceQA \cite{feng2015insuranceqa} provides community QA pairs for answer selection, and InsQABench \cite{ding2025insqabench} evaluates LLM question answering over insurance product rules. Our focus is upstream of assistant answering: rather than testing whether a downstream chatbot asks clarifying questions on underspecified prompts, we verify whether \emph{synthetic evaluation queries} retain the conditions needed to keep their reference labels determinate during benchmark creation.

\paragraph{Statistical Calibration with Small Gold Samples.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction}, ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}, and PRECISE \cite{divekar2026precise} combine synthetic data and automated LLM judges with small human-labeled calibration sets to construct valid confidence intervals for RAG evaluation metrics. When synthetic queries are generated from a small subset of corpus documents without paired real queries across the full index, unpaired shrinkage and marginal query-propensity weighting fail to correct document-subsampling and query-to-source overlap biases; we report these negative calibration results alongside a heuristic bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.

\subsection{Data Provenance, Licensing Scope, and Inference-Only Setup}
Table~\ref{tab:data_audit} documents the origin, scope, and usage constraints of all study materials:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-Only Evaluation}: No generative LLM or embedding model weights are pre-trained, instruction-tuned, or fine-tuned in this work. All local runs (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} and \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5}) use frozen weights under deterministic \texttt{float32/eager} inference, and cloud API runs (\texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}) use \texttt{temperature}=0. Few-shot prompts provide three fixed calibration examples in context. The only fitted statistical parameters in our code are corpus TF-IDF/BM25 term frequencies and a 2-fold logistic regression classifier used for propensity-weighting diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}.
  \item \textbf{Official Insurance Clauses versus Synthetic Multi-Threshold Rules}: The $26$ official insurance policies ($5$ development policies in Section~\ref{sec:dev_policies}, $4$ extension policies in Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies}, $11$ confirmation policies and $6$ post-hardening extension policies in Section~\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation}, with $3$ contracts also providing non-waiting-period clauses in Section~\ref{sec:ccf_a_upgrade}) are official PDF wordings downloaded from public disclosure portals of Ping An ($22$ PDFs), CPIC ($3$ PDFs), and Taikang ($1$ PDF; one additional Taikang download \texttt{tkyl\_2018.pdf} returned a corrupted 30-KB payload during collection and was excluded prior to seed construction). Iterative product versions within the same insurer product series (such as \texttt{pa\_595} and \texttt{pa\_597} in the Ping An e-Sheng-Bao medical family, which share identical waiting-period body text \texttt{SHA-256: c0126932...} under distinct product codes and schedule headers) are intentionally retained to stress-test product-title disambiguation across homologous contract templates, while multi-rule seeds drawn from the same underlying PDF (\texttt{pa\_2651}, \texttt{pa\_2650}, \texttt{pa\_1073}) are grouped into $D=21$ independent source-document clusters for permutation inference. In addition, because PyMuPDF (\texttt{fitz}) \texttt{rawdict} character streams preserve inline glossary footnote superscripts attached directly to clinical and legal terms (e.g., ``\texttt{意外伤害2}'', ``\texttt{医院1确诊2初患恶性肿瘤3}''), our parser uses context-anchored duration and threshold patterns (\texttt{第X天}, \texttt{等待期为X天}) rather than unanchored numeral heuristics. Because public web availability does not confer a license to redistribute full commercial contracts, our repository releases SHA256 file hashes, source URLs, PDF page and bounding-box coordinates, and short verification excerpts rather than raw PDF binaries. In contrast, the four multi-threshold rules in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} are researcher-authored synthetic clauses designed for controlled stress testing.
  \item \textbf{Community QA Corpus (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv})}: Used in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}, this file ($8{,}362$ raw rows; $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid questions after normalization) comes from \texttt{meowcao/InsuranceModel}, which mirrors \texttt{SophonPlus/ChineseNlpCorpus} (\texttt{datasets/baoxianzhidao/intro.ipynb}). Upstream documentation states that the pairs were collected from Baidu Zhidao insurance Q\&A with original post URLs removed and sensitive fields filtered. Consequently, answers reflect historical community responses rather than actuarial ground truth, no verified open redistribution license chain is documented upstream, and Chinese foundation models may have encountered parts of this text during pretraining.
  \item \textbf{Proxy Parser Scope, Boundary Risks, and Manual-Inspection Watchlist}: Formally, our reachability verifier is exact \emph{relative to the extracted observation frame} $\hat{z} = g(q')$, where $g(\cdot)$ is a synonym-aware regular-expression slot parser that also resolves explicit contrastive corrections (\texttt{不是/并非/并不是 A，而是 B}), rejects negated numeric durations (\texttt{不到/未满 N 天}), and flags multi-value day conflicts (\texttt{第20天或第45天}). Because $g(\cdot)$ is a rule-based extractor rather than a full semantic parser, it faces two boundary risks: \textbf{false-reject errors} (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}), when a rewrite uses uncataloged colloquial paraphrases or omits formal rule titles while retaining domain keywords, and \textbf{false-accept errors} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}), if complex multi-clause discourse or long-range negation falls outside the regex scope rules. We provide two supplementary artifacts without claiming completed human validation: (i)~blinded two-rater HTML annotation packages (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, which remain uncompleted by external raters and are not reported as human results), and (ii)~an automated verifier explanation and 29-item manual-inspection watchlist across all $264$ primary generated questions (\texttt{data\_v3/item\_by\_item\_semantic\_audit.jsonl}), which logs deterministic parser traces and flags format or phrasing edge cases for future human review without claiming an external semantic accuracy rate.
\end{enumerate}

Each development scenario frame specifies up to six fields: product identity ($20/20$ seeds), enrollment type (first-time purchase vs.\ renewal, $20/20$), policy effective day ($20/20$), claim cause (illness vs.\ accidental injury, $20/20$), selected plan waiting period ($4/20$ seeds on \texttt{cpic\_86017}), and insurer-approved waiting-period waiver ($3/20$ renewal seeds). From $20$ seed scenarios, we construct $80$ controlled test cases across four variants: \emph{complete} (all required slots stated), \emph{surface} (synonymous phrasing), \emph{missing-critical} (one pivotal condition removed, certified as requiring clarification via two completion witnesses that yield opposite decisions), and \emph{counterfactual} (one condition altered to flip the rule outcome between waiting-period exclusion and coverage).

Because a seed scenario can contain slots that are non-pivotal for its specific state---for example, on \texttt{pa\_h865} (30-day wait), a claim occurring on Day~45 satisfies $45 > 30$, so omitting whether the cause was illness or accident still leads to coverage under every completion---we evaluate two distinct criteria:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{All-Slot Completeness}: Every required field of the reference seed is extracted without omission or value conflict.
  \item \textbf{Reference-Independent Decision Determinacy}: Using only the slots extracted from the generated question and the public policy rules (without reading the reference seed's required-field list), the verifier enumerates the Cartesian product of admissible completions for any unmentioned fields. Elapsed-day domains are partitioned into complete threshold-equivalence regions ($< t$, $= t$, $> t$ across all waiting-period thresholds $\{7, 15, 25, 30, 90\}$ using representative days $\{5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120\}$), and all schema fields---including optional waiver overrides---are checked for value conflicts. A rewrite is classified as:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{Preserved Decision} (\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION}) if no observed schema field conflicts with the seed and the set of reachable decisions across all admissible completions is the singleton matching the reference outcome;
    \item \emph{Condition Mutated / Decision Flip} (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} / \texttt{COUNTERFACTUAL\_FLIP}) if any observed schema field conflicts with the seed or if the reachable set is a singleton with the opposite coverage decision;
    \item \emph{Degraded to Clarification} (\texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY}) if both coverage and exclusion are reachable across completions ($|\text{reachable}| > 1$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Paired Rewriting Across Three Qwen Tiers}
We pass the $20$ seed questions and their source clause excerpts to three off-the-shelf generators---local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} (\texttt{temperature}=0, parameter counts undisclosed by the provider)---under two prompting conditions ($120$ total rewrites):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conversational Style (\texttt{ordinary\_style})}: Instructs the model to rewrite the seed question into a short, natural everyday user inquiry while keeping the original intent.
  \item \textbf{Condition-Constrained Prompt (\texttt{condition\_prompt})}: Explicitly instructs the model to retain the full product title, first-time vs.\ renewal status, effective day, illness vs.\ accident cause, coverage scope, plan waiting period, and waiver approval status without adding or omitting conditions.
\end{itemize}

We compare an initial literal regex guard (\texttt{v1\_strict\_regex}) against our synonym-aware slot parser (\texttt{v2\_synonym\_aware}, which normalizes surface expressions such as ``生效是第20天'' or ``同意免去等待期''), and also report both standalone evaluation and granted-policy evaluation (where the source policy identity is supplied by document context).

\subsection{Development Policy Findings}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} reveal three key patterns:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{High Retrieval Accuracy Coexists with Severe Decision Loss Under Conversational Rewriting}: Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, cloud models achieve $80\%\text{--}90\%$ Top-1 clause retrieval, yet standalone decision determinacy is only $30\%$ ($6/20$) on \texttt{1.5B}, $0\%$ ($0/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and $45\%$ ($9/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}. Even when the target policy identity is granted by context, $45\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $25\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $20\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) of conversational rewrites remain undecidable or mutated.
  \item \textbf{Numeric Mutations on \texttt{1.5B} versus Conversational Compression on Cloud Models}: On \texttt{1.5B}, $3/20$ conversational rewrites (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016}) corrupt ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2), and two seeds with opposite ground-truth outcomes (\texttt{condition-009} with a 7-day wait on Day~10 vs.\ \texttt{condition-010} with a 25-day wait on Day~10) collapse into the exact same question after dropping the plan waiting period. On \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}, numeric value mutations are $0/20$ in this sample (a finite-sample observation on $n=20$ seeds rather than proof of a zero population rate), but both cloud models omit administrative qualifiers to sound natural: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the policy title in $20/20$ seeds, and \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops the policy title in $10/20$ seeds and replaces ``首次投保'' (first-time purchase) with ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which does not distinguish first purchase from renewal) in $6/20$ seeds. When \texttt{Qwen-Max} keeps the product title ($10/20$ seeds), $5$ of those seeds omit enrollment status on non-waivable clauses or post-waiting-period days where the coverage decision is still invariant, explaining the gap between all-slot match ($4/20$, $20\%$) and formal decision determinacy ($9/20$, $45\%$).
  \item \textbf{Explicit Slot Constraints Restore Determinacy}: Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, standalone decision determinacy rises to $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on both \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} ($100\%$ across all three models when policy identity is granted). On \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, one constrained rewrite omits the illness/accident cause on a post-waiting-period seed where both completions lead to coverage, yielding $19/20$ all-slot match but $20/20$ decision determinacy. On \texttt{1.5B}, the single \texttt{condition\_prompt} failure (\texttt{condition-008} on \texttt{pa\_h865}) omits the product title while retaining the renewal waiver and effective day, so it is classified as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} standalone and \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} when the source policy is granted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Extended Evaluation on Four Additional Official Insurance Policies}
\label{sec:heldout_policies}

\subsection{Benefit-Specific Clause Modeling and Iterative Parser Refinement}
To test our verification workflow beyond the five development contracts in Table~\ref{tab:clauses}, we collected four additional official Chinese insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_clauses\_sources.json}). Extending the development verifier to these four contracts exposed two modeling requirements that were not present in the initial five-policy schema: (i)~a single contract can assign different waiting periods and exemption rules to distinct \emph{benefit types} rather than treating the waiting period as an arbitrary user-chosen number, and (ii)~when textbook book-title brackets (\texttt{《》}) and section headers are stripped, rewrites frequently refer to products by shortened business aliases. We therefore updated the shared parser (\texttt{extract\_fields\_surface\_normalized} in \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}) to extract benefit types (\texttt{benefit\_id}) and colloquial product aliases before scoring the $16$ seeds ($64$ controlled cases). Because these schema and parser updates were informed by inspecting the four extension contracts, we report the results in this section as a \textbf{retrospective post-refinement evaluation} rather than a blind zero-shot test of an untouched parser.

Each of the four extension contracts is anchored to Page~1, Article~4 (``第四条 保险责任'') of its official PDF (verified by full-file SHA256 hashes in \texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_clauses\_sources.json}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{pa\_2011\_36} (\emph{Ping An Worry-Free Inpatient Medical Insurance} / 平安住院安心医疗保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{10916a68758a}): defines two distinct benefits with benefit-specific waiting periods and exemption rules:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \emph{General Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{general\_hospital}, ``（一）一般住院医疗津贴''): 30-day waiting period; accidental injury hospitalization (\texttt{accident\_exempt=True}) or continuous renewal (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}) has no waiting period.
    \item \emph{Cancer Inpatient Allowance} (\texttt{cancer\_hospital}, ``（二）癌症住院医疗津贴''): 90-day waiting period for first-time cancer diagnosis and hospitalization; continuous renewal has no waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}), whereas no accidental-injury exemption applies (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \end{itemize}
  Under this benefit-resolved model, on Days~$31\text{--}90$ where the waiting period determines the outcome (e.g., first-time sickness claims without an approved renewal waiver that exempts both benefits), omitting which benefit is claimed---or conflating the two benefits by asking whether the claim should be settled under the 30-day general inpatient allowance or the 90-day cancer inpatient allowance---yields reachable set $[\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}, \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}]$ and is classified as \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} (whereas if an explicit renewal waiver exempts both benefits, omitting the benefit leaves the waiting-period outcome invariant). Any question that pairs the general allowance with a 90-day wait or the cancer allowance with a 30-day wait is flagged as \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0254} (\emph{Ping An Inpatient Medical Insurance} / 平安住院医疗保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{d6ad23849fec}): 90-day waiting period for first-time or non-continuous sickness inpatient medical expense reimbursement; accidental injury treatment and continuous renewal have no waiting period.
  \item \texttt{pa\_p0321} (\emph{Ping An Lung Cancer Disease Insurance} / 平安肺癌疾病保险, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{e04e0b452858}): a single-disease policy with a 30-day waiting period for first-time diagnosis of primary lung cancer, explicitly excluding carcinoma in situ (``初次发生原发性肺癌，上述原发性肺癌不包括原位癌''); continuous renewal or other agreement exempts the waiting period (\texttt{approval\_exempt=True}), whereas accidental injury does not waive the disease waiting period (\texttt{accident\_exempt=False}).
  \item \texttt{cpic\_93896} (\emph{CPIC Additional [Guangxi] Student \& Child Inpatient Medical Insurance [2025 Edition]} / 附加（广西地区）学生幼儿住院医疗保险（2025版）, Page~1, Article~4, SHA256 prefix \texttt{309d8f029fb8}): Article~4 (Page~1) specifies a 30-day waiting period for illness hospitalization and states that accidental injury and renewal are not subject to the 30-day limit (``因遭受意外伤害或者自本附加险合同生效之日起30日（续保不受30日规定的限制）后因疾病住院治疗''), while Article~6 (Page~3) specifies that the contract is a one-year non-guaranteed renewal rider (``本产品为不保证续保产品''). To avoid conflating guaranteed and non-guaranteed renewal semantics, all four evaluation seeds on \texttt{cpic\_93896} test first-time enrollment (\texttt{enrollment=first}), where the 30-day illness waiting period and accidental-injury exemption apply directly.
\end{enumerate}

We construct $16$ seed scenarios ($4$ per policy) and $64$ controlled test cases (\texttt{data\_v3/heldout\_controlled\_cases.jsonl}). In each case record, we strip textbook book-title brackets (\texttt{《》}) and explicit clause-number prefixes as a surface formatting normalization (while noting that retaining full product names or aliases and exact conditions means these remain controlled synthetic items rather than organic customer queries). Each case record separates contract-clause evidence (\texttt{source\_type=pdf\_contract\_clause}, linked to the PDF filename, page number, and bounding-box coordinates \texttt{bbox\_pdf\_points}) from synthetic scenario parameters (\texttt{source\_type=synthetic\_seed\_frame}, such as elapsed days and enrollment status, which originate from the seed frame rather than the PDF), and includes two opposite-outcome completion witnesses on every \texttt{missing\_critical} case. Under the unified parser and reachability verifier, all $64/64$ controlled cases match their constructed labels ($16/16$ \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} on \texttt{complete} and \texttt{surface}, $16/16$ \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} on \texttt{missing\_critical}, and $16/16$ \texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED} on \texttt{counterfactual}), with zero parser discrepancies against \texttt{src/analyze\_v3.py}.

\subsection{Extension Policy Results and Title-Masked Retrieval}
Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance} reports the evaluation on the four extension policies (\texttt{data\_v3/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_summary.json}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conversational Rewriting Collapses Eligibility Determinacy}: On the four extension policies, \texttt{ordinary\_style} achieves high surface novelty ($1-\kappa_2(q', q_{\text{seed}}) = 0.5380$) and low clause copying ($\kappa_2(q', d^*) = 0.1917$), but drops the product title on $16/16$ seeds and drops pivotal waiting-period conditions (such as enrollment status, elapsed day, or waiver approval) on $11/16$ seeds even when the policy identity is granted, leaving $0/16$ ($0.0\%$) standalone decision preservation and $5/16$ ($31.2\%$) granted-policy decision preservation.
  \item \textbf{First-Pass Saturation on Single-Contract Insurance Rules (Zero Repair Gain Observed)}: When prompted with explicit slot constraints (\texttt{condition\_prompt}), \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} preserves all slots and formal decisions on $16/16$ ($100.0\%$) seeds on the first pass with $0/16$ mutations, at the cost of lower surface novelty ($1-\kappa_2 = 0.1251$, clause copy $\kappa_2 = 0.3355$). Because all $16$ first-pass drafts already satisfy the verifier, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} and trigger-matched \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} trigger $0$ second-pass repair calls ($16$ total calls, exact two-sided McNemar $p=1.0$ vs.\ \texttt{condition\_prompt}). Thus, \textbf{no closed-loop repair gain over open-loop constrained prompting is observed on this 4-policy insurance set}. Meanwhile, \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} achieves $10/16$ ($62.5\%$) standalone and $15/16$ ($93.8\%$) granted-policy decision preservation ($p=0.03125$ vs.\ \texttt{condition\_prompt} / \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}): $2/16$ responses hit the 160-token cap (one of which still preserves the decision), and $5/16$ record the policy title in the first-line JSON object but omit it from the second-line \texttt{QUESTION:} text. Because \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} executed zero repairs here, this $16/16$ vs.\ $10/16$ difference reflects single-pass natural-language prompting versus the two-line JSON output format rather than closed-loop repair.
  \item \textbf{What Title-Masked Retrieval Actually Measures}: Across the combined 9-clause pool ($5$ development + $4$ extension clauses), masking product titles, colloquial product aliases, and insurer names tests how strongly retrieval depends on explicit product-name anchors. For title-preserving queries (\texttt{seed\_passthrough} and \texttt{condition\_prompt}), masking product titles drops 9-clause dense retrieval (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) Top-1 accuracy from $75.0\%$ ($12/16$) to $31.2\%$ ($5/16$) (with \texttt{bm25\_char2} at $75.0\%$), because multiple inpatient medical contracts share identical 30-day or 90-day waiting-period clauses once product names are removed. Thus, title-masked retrieval specifically diagnoses product-title anchor dependence rather than acting as a uniform penalty across query styles.

\section{Equal-Call-Budget Comparison on Multi-Threshold Rules}
\label{sec:multithreshold}

\subsection{Synthetic Multi-Threshold Testbed and Evaluation Protocol}
While single-clause insurance rewrites already achieve $100\%$ decision preservation on the first pass under \texttt{condition\_prompt} (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_insurance}), multi-threshold rules with competing numeric windows place heavier demands on small local generators. To test whether closed-loop symbolic verification provides value beyond open-loop prompting and unguided LLM self-refinement under a matched call budget, we evaluate a 16-seed ($64$-case) synthetic multi-threshold benchmark (\texttt{src/cross\_domain\_study.py} and \texttt{src/equal\_budget\_and\_heldout\_study.py}). Unlike the nine official insurance policy PDFs in Sections~\ref{sec:dev_policies}--\ref{sec:heldout_policies}, these four clauses are researcher-authored synthetic rules inspired by clinical pathways and PRC labor statutes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (combining T790M status, a 14-day washout threshold after prior therapy, and an emergency specialist waiver); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (contrasting a 10-day knee replacement window against a 35-day hip replacement window, with active bleeding contraindication and hematology consult override).
  \item \textbf{Labor-Law-Inspired Rules (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (inspired by PRC Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14(6) on non-principal commuting fault and Art.~17 contrasting the 30-day employer filing deadline against the 365-day employee/kin deadline and administrative extension); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (inspired by PRC Labor Contract Law Art.~24 and SPC Judicial Interpretation~I Art.~38 on 90-day default of post-termination non-compete compensation, augmented with a synthetic supplemental grace-period agreement).
\end{itemize}

For each seed, unobserved durations are expanded over a complete threshold partition ($\{7, 10, 12, 14, 20, 30, 32, 35, 45, 90, 120, 365, 400\}$ covering $< t, = t, > t$ for all thresholds $\{10, 14, 30, 35, 90, 365\}$), and all six schema fields are checked for mutations and reachability without consulting reference seed metadata.

We compare seven methods on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32} greedy decoding, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=160):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{seed\_passthrough} ($0$ calls): deterministic template question prior to LLM rewriting (information-preservation upper bound, zero template novelty).
  \item \texttt{ordinary\_style} ($16$ calls): 1-call unconstrained conversational rewrite.
  \item \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($16$ calls): 1-call open-loop slot-constrained rewrite.
  \item \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($16$ calls): 1-call prompt instructing the model to output a single-line JSON object of all 6 slot values on Line~1 and \texttt{QUESTION: <question>} on Line~2.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (budget-matched $26$ calls): takes the 16 \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts and runs a second unguided LLM self-check pass (asking the model to verify all conditions against the source clause and seed question \emph{without symbolic parser diagnostics}) on the exact same $10$ seeds triggered by \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}. Sharing the trigger schedule isolates the effect of symbolic slot feedback in the repair prompt while holding both the total call budget ($26$ calls) and the refined seed subset fixed.
  \item \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} (all seeds, $32$ calls): runs the unguided second-pass LLM self-check across all $16$ seeds without consulting the symbolic parser trigger.
  \item \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($26$ calls): runs the symbolic slot parser on the $16$ \texttt{condition\_prompt} drafts, triggers a second pass on the $10$ seeds where any slot is missing or mutated, and supplies the exact missing/mutated slot names and target values in the repair prompt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Separating \texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN} from \texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED} and Three-Layer Verification Check}
Because the 4-policy extension in Section~\ref{sec:heldout_policies} was evaluated after adding colloquial aliases and \texttt{benefit\_id} disambiguation, and the 16-seed testbed in Section~\ref{sec:multithreshold} had a modest single-set sample size ($N=16$), we expanded the study (\texttt{src/frozen\_confirmation\_and\_rag\_study.py}) to $11$ zero-alias confirmation insurance policy PDFs (\texttt{pa\_h716}, \texttt{pa\_2651}, \texttt{pa\_l0001}, \texttt{pa\_1714}, \texttt{pa\_1072}, \texttt{pa\_596}, \texttt{cpic\_107082}, \texttt{cpic\_106702}, \texttt{cpic\_60201}, \texttt{cpic\_79920}, \texttt{cpic\_85050}) and $6$ post-hardening extension Ping An policy PDFs (\texttt{pa\_2650}, \texttt{pa\_1071}, \texttt{pa\_1073}, \texttt{pa\_595}, \texttt{pa\_597}, \texttt{pa\_l0002}, bringing the total to $26$ official policy PDFs). We evaluate a 120-case diagnostic suite, a 40-seed ($160$-case) confirmation benchmark, and a 24-seed ($48$-pair) post-hardening extension set across two model families: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} (DashScope \texttt{qwen-plus}, returning \texttt{response\_model="qwen-plus"}) and \texttt{DeepSeek} (\texttt{deepseek-chat} endpoint, returning \texttt{response\_model="deepseek-flash"}, \texttt{system\_fingerprint="e72d3c7f663d3e1383b00b3884cb4501"}).

To avoid conflating parser grammar limitations with genuine question ambiguity, our verifier applies a text-only uncertainty gate \texttt{detect\_parse\_uncertainty(text)} without reading reference seed metadata (\texttt{seed\_frame}). Each output is assigned to one of four mutually exclusive states: (1)~\texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} (unique reference decision preserved, zero slot mutations, and \texttt{is\_parse\_uncertain=False}), (2)~\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}, (3)~\texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED} (missing pivotal slots within supported syntax, accompanied by two opposite-outcome completion witnesses), and (4)~\texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}. The fourth state triggers purely from query text when a question uses unsupported time units (months, weeks, or calendar dates), parenthetical cross-unit corrections, negated durations (\texttt{未满/不足 N 天}), conflicting day numbers within a clause, or unmodeled disease-specific waiting-period branches (such as \texttt{pa\_h716}'s 90-day wait for adenoid hypertrophy, hernia, tonsil, or female reproductive organ diseases versus 30 days for other illnesses).

We examine the verifier at three levels:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Layer~1 Clause-to-Rule Mapping Audit (\texttt{data\_v3/clause\_rule\_mapping\_audit.json} \& \texttt{untouched\_holdout\_sources.json})}: Verifies all $26$ official insurance PDFs and $4$ multi-threshold rules against SHA256 hashes, PDF page numbers, bounding-box coordinates, waiting-period days, exemptions, and unmodeled exclusion boundaries. Compiling rules for the first $20$ insurance contracts required an estimated $11.72$~min per contract (median $11.0$~min, total $234.5$~min; recorded as an author engineering time estimate rather than a multi-participant timing study).
  \item \textbf{Layer~2 120-Case Constructive Diagnostic Suite (\texttt{data\_v3/gold\_diagnostic\_summary.json})}: Across $8$ categories ($15$ cases each: $60$ positive and $60$ negative), the verifier records $\text{TP}=45$, $\text{FP}=0$ (\textbf{False Accept Rate $= 0.0\%$, Precision $= 100.0\%$}), $\text{TN}=60$, and $\text{FN}=15$. On supported day-unit syntax (Categories~1--7, $105$ cases), both false-accept and false-reject rates are $0.0\%$. All $15$ false rejects come from Category~8 (\texttt{unsupported\_syntax\_determinate}, using month/week units or calendar dates) and are routed to \texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}.
  \item \textbf{Layer~3 Shared-Rule-Base Dual-Implementation Consistency Check of 400 Confirmation Generations (\texttt{data\_v3/frozen\_confirmation\_three\_layer\_audit.jsonl} \& \texttt{frozen\_confirmation\_independent\_audit\_labels.json})}: Across $400$ generated queries ($5$ main methods $\times 40$ seeds $\times 2$ model families), we compare primary verifier outputs against a second forward-reachability implementation (\texttt{evaluate\_independent\_text\_semantics}) that computes reachable outcomes directly from extracted text slots without reading \texttt{r['outcome']} or \texttt{seed['reference\_decision']}. When a numeric edit stays within the same waiting-period partition (e.g., Day~20 $\rightarrow$ Day~21 under a 30-day waiting period), forward reachability keeps \texttt{specific\_decision = WAIT\_EXCLUDED} and labels the case \texttt{FACT\_MUTATED\_SAME\_DECISION} rather than flipping the decision to \texttt{DECISION\_CHANGED}. Because this second implementation shares the hand-compiled domain rule tables and does not involve independent human annotators, we report it as a \textbf{shared-rule-base dual-implementation consistency check (\texttt{shared\_rule\_base\_dual\_implementation\_consistency\_check})} under two criteria:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Slot-Only Semantic Consistency (\texttt{semantic\_reachability\_consistency}, ignoring format truncation)}: $\text{TP}=296$, \textbf{$\text{FP}=0$ (Semantic False Accept Rate $= 0.0\%$, Precision $= 100.0\%$)}, $\text{TN}=86$, $\text{FN}=18$ ($\text{FRR}=5.73\%$, Recall $= 94.27\%$).
    \item \textbf{Joint Usability Consistency (\texttt{joint\_format\_and\_semantic\_usability}, requiring \texttt{format\_valid=True})}: $\text{TP}=290$, $\text{FP}=6$ ($\text{FAR}=6.38\%$, Precision $= 97.97\%$), $\text{TN}=88$, $\text{FN}=16$ ($\text{FRR}=5.23\%$, Recall $= 94.77\%$). All $6$ false accepts occur in 160-token \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($5$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and $1$ on \texttt{DeepSeek}) due to mid-sentence truncation (\texttt{format\_valid=False}), whereas \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}, \texttt{condition\_prompt}, and \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} each record $0/80$ false accepts.
  \end{itemize}

In \texttt{structured\_json\_schema\_render}, $4$ cases fail because the model copies ``意外伤害无等待期'' into the JSON \texttt{waiver} slot on non-waiver seeds, $3$ multi-threshold cases fail because the 5-field JSON prompt schema (\texttt{clause, status, condition, day, waiver}) lacks a dedicated \texttt{threshold\_days} slot or renders day $105$ as the generic phrase ``目前已达第105天'' without the domain prefix required by the \texttt{law\_noncompete} regex (on \texttt{deepseek:fconf-cross-011} [seed \texttt{status='secondary', elapsed\_day=105, threshold\_days=90, approved\_waiver=False}], the model also writes ``高级技术人员且负有保密义务'' into JSON \texttt{status}, yielding \texttt{extracted.status='primary'}, \texttt{mutated\_slots=['status']}, \texttt{missing\_slots=['elapsed\_day', 'threshold\_days']}, and \texttt{outcome='CONDITION\_MUTATED'}, whereas \texttt{012} keeps \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} under \texttt{approved\_waiver=True} with \texttt{threshold\_days=90} extracted from ``超过90天（3个月）''), $2$ cases hit \texttt{pa\_h716}, and $1$ case abbreviates the clause title to ``膝关节置换''. \subsection{Downstream Pipeline Selection Under Reference-Free Inference}
To test whether upstream query drift affects downstream system rankings without reference-label leakage, all five candidate retrieval + rule-decision pipelines run through the top-level function \texttt{predict(question, retrieved\_clause, inference\_config)}. This interface receives only the query string, the top-1 retrieved clause ID, and public clause rule tables (\texttt{data\_v3/downstream\_rag\_selection\_study.json}). Dense retrieval uses cached \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} embeddings across all $80$ reference queries and $640$ confirmation queries, raising a \texttt{KeyError} on any uncached string and returning \texttt{None} when $\text{BM25}(q, d) \le 0$ so zero-overlap out-of-domain inputs abstain. Flipping \texttt{seed['reference\_decision']} while holding the query and retrieved clause fixed leaves every pipeline's output unchanged.

On the 80-case reference benchmark ($40$ complete seeds + $40$ missing-pivotal clarify seeds), reference accuracy ranks \textbf{\texttt{S1\_dense\_rule\_clarify} ($97.50\%$, $78/80$) $>$ \texttt{S2\_bm25\_rule\_clarify} ($93.75\%$, $75/80$) $>$ \texttt{S5\_dense\_threshold\_echo\_heuristic} ($41.25\%$, $33/80$) $>$ \texttt{S3\_dense\_aggressive\_deny} ($35.00\%$, $28/80$) $>$ \texttt{S4\_dense\_aggressive\_approve} ($31.25\%$, $25/80$)}. When unverified \texttt{ordinary\_style} rewrites across the $40$ confirmation seeds inherit their seed's binary label, $97.5\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of test queries degrade into ambiguous inquiries. Clarifying pipelines \texttt{S1} and \texttt{S2} are penalized to $0.0\%\text{--}2.5\%$ for requesting clarification, whereas \texttt{S3\_dense\_aggressive\_deny} ($50.0\%$) ranks first. This reverses the system ordering (\textbf{Kendall's $\tau = -0.7746$, Spearman's $\rho = -0.8660$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}; $\tau = -0.8367$, $\rho = -0.8944$ on \texttt{DeepSeek}}) and incurs a $0.6250$ selection regret. Both \texttt{verify\_only\_filter} and \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} reduce label corruption to $0.0\%$ and recover the reference ranking ($\tau = 1.0000$, $\rho = 1.0000$, zero regret), with \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} retaining $97.5\%$ ($39/40$) scenario coverage compared with $82.5\%\text{--}92.5\%$ under \texttt{verify\_only\_filter}.

\section{Protocol-Guided and Pure-Verbatim Three-Flagship Adjudication, Nine Real LLM RAG Pipelines, and Verbatim PDF Multi-Rule Generalization}
\label{sec:ccf_a_upgrade}

The experiments in Sections~\ref{sec:dev_policies}--\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation} leave four methodological questions open: (i)~because \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} uses the symbolic slot parser for repair feedback, evaluating methods with that same parser risks coupled false accepts or false rejects, and any LLM adjudication that includes compiled rule instructions could still inherit modeling assumptions; (ii)~whether LLM rewriting provides measurable utility over zero-call multi-template rendering (\texttt{multi\_template\_3}) or complete 6-slot schema rendering (\texttt{complete\_6slot\_schema\_render}); (iii)~whether downstream ranking distortion persists across real end-to-end cloud LLM RAG pipelines when reader model and abstention protocol are controlled independently and validated on an external holdout set; and (iv)~whether reachability-guided verification generalizes to verbatim PDF non-waiting-period rules with distinct mathematical operators and achieves statistical significance at both the 25-clause-ID and 21-independent-source-document cluster levels after multiple-testing correction. We address all four questions in \texttt{src/ccf\_a\_upgrade\_study.py} (\texttt{data\_v3/ccf\_a\_upgrade\_summary.json}, version \texttt{v3.3-ccf-a-audit-remediated}), logging live HTTP response headers (\texttt{http\_request\_id} and \texttt{http\_date}) across every API call.

\subsection{Protocol-Guided Cross-Check vs.\ Strictly Zero-Hint Pure-Verbatim Three-Flagship Adjudication}
To evaluate generated queries without relying on the symbolic regex parser or seed slot frames, we construct a \textbf{blinded three-flagship adjudication panel} comprising \texttt{Kimi-K3} (\texttt{kimi-k3}, Moonshot AI, an independent third-party model family never used as a query generator), \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Pro} (\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}), and \texttt{Qwen3.8-Max} (\texttt{qwen3.8-max}) at $\texttt{temperature}=0$. To separate general domain task instructions from zero-hint contract reading, we evaluate two distinct adjudication protocols (explicitly reported as \textbf{cross-family flagship LLM adjudication} rather than human annotation):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Protocol-Guided Multi-Model Cross-Check (\texttt{data\_v3/ccf\_a\_raw\_clause\_adjudications.jsonl}, $264$ batches across all $88$ seeds)}: On the $64$ waiting-period confirmation and extension seeds, the prompt provides the randomized candidate queries, the verbatim policy clause text, and explicit waiting-period adjudication instructions (including a note that \texttt{pa\_h716} specifies a 90-day wait for specific organ/disease branches vs.\ 30 days for other illnesses). On the $24$ non-waiting-period seeds ($72$ batches), this file already uses the strictly zero-hint pure-verbatim prompt below.
  \item \textbf{Strictly Zero-Hint Pure-Verbatim Adjudication (\texttt{data\_v3/ccf\_a\_pure\_verbatim\_adjudications.jsonl}, $120$ batches on all $40$ confirmation seeds plus the $72$ non-waiting batches)}: Provides \textbf{only} the randomized candidate questions, the verbatim PDF contract excerpts, and a universal 3-class decision definition (\texttt{ELIGIBLE}, \texttt{EXCLUDED}, or \texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED} if any decision-pivotal condition required by the verbatim excerpt is missing or contradictory), with \textbf{zero} compiled rule hints, threshold tables, or policy-specific notes.
\end{enumerate}

We also separate two distinct inter-adjudicator reliability ($\kappa$) definitions: (a)~\textbf{binary usability $\kappa$}, computed on the binary usability indicator $\mathbb{I}[\texttt{policy\_identified} \wedge \texttt{sentence\_complete} \wedge (\text{decision} = y_{\text{seed}})]$; and (b)~\textbf{direct 3-way semantic label $\kappa$}, computed directly on the three-class semantic decision label in $\{\texttt{ELIGIBLE}, \texttt{EXCLUDED}, \texttt{CLARIFY}\}$ without referencing $y_{\text{seed}}$. Across the $640$ core confirmation records ($40$ seeds $\times 2$ generators $\times 8$ methods):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Protocol-Guided vs.\ Pure-Verbatim Agreement}: Under protocol-guided adjudication, 3-rater \textbf{binary usability Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7411$} and \textbf{3-way semantic label Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.8578$} (pairwise 3-way Cohen's $\kappa$: $0.8789$ for \texttt{Kimi-K3} vs.\ \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Pro}, $0.8481$ for \texttt{Kimi-K3} vs.\ \texttt{Qwen3.8-Max}, and $0.8477$ for \texttt{Qwen3.8-Max} vs.\ \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Pro}). Under strictly zero-hint \textbf{pure-verbatim adjudication}, 3-rater \textbf{binary usability Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7008$} and \textbf{3-way semantic label Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.8366$}. Majority usability votes between the protocol-guided and pure-verbatim panels agree on \textbf{$96.56\%$ ($618/640$, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8857$)} of confirmation queries. Across all $88$ seeds ($N=1{,}408$ core records), 3-rater binary Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.7423$ and 3-way semantic Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.8464$ (on the $24$ non-waiting-period seeds under pure-verbatim adjudication, binary $\kappa = 0.8041$ and 3-way semantic $\kappa = 0.8345$).
  \item \textbf{Symbolic Verifier vs.\ Flagship Panel Error Audit ($640$ Confirmation Records)}: Exact binary usability agreement between the symbolic verifier and the protocol-guided panel majority is \textbf{$87.19\%$ ($558/640$, binary $\kappa = 0.5901$, 3-way semantic $\kappa = 0.7910$; $82.46\%$ across all $1{,}408$ records)}, decomposing into $\text{TP}=475$, $\text{TN}=83$, \textbf{$46$ parser false rejects ($7.19\%$)} (colloquial paraphrases such as ``还没获'' or ``连续30天未支付'' that fall outside the regex lexicon while remaining clear to LLM readers), and \textbf{$36$ parser false accepts ($5.62\%$)}. The $36$ false accepts reflect three specific schema boundaries: (a)~in \texttt{cpic\_106702} (\texttt{fconf-ins-017/018}), the verbatim PDF specifies a 15-day wait for the maternal primary insured and a 0-day wait for the newborn dependent insured; (b)~in \texttt{fconf-cross-011}, the seed combines ``已按月足额支付补偿'' with ``连续未付已达第105天''; and (c)~in \texttt{fconf-cross-001/007}, natural-language methods follow the seed in leaving default-false emergency waivers implicit, whereas \texttt{structured\_json\_untruncated\_320} prints ``\texttt{"waiver": "无特批豁免"}'' in its JSON prefix.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Downstream System Selection Across Nine Real Cloud LLM RAG Pipelines, Same-Model Protocol Ablations, and External Holdout-48}
To test whether synthetic label corruption distorts evaluation of real generative RAG systems when reader model and abstention protocol are controlled independently, we evaluate \textbf{nine end-to-end cloud LLM RAG pipelines} (\texttt{L1}--\texttt{L9}, $4{,}067$ unique \texttt{(reader\_cfg\_id, retrieved\_clause\_id, question)} calls cached in \texttt{data\_v3/ccf\_a\_real\_llm\_rag\_responses.jsonl}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Six Abstention-Capable Readers (\texttt{L1}--\texttt{L4}, \texttt{L7}--\texttt{L8})}: \texttt{L1} (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} $+$ \texttt{qwen3.8-max}), \texttt{L2} (\texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} $+$ \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}), \texttt{L3} (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} $+$ \texttt{kimi-k3}), \texttt{L4} (\texttt{bm25\_char2} $+$ \texttt{qwen3.8-max}), \texttt{L7} (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} $+$ \texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash}), and \texttt{L8} (\texttt{bm25\_char2} $+$ \texttt{qwen-plus}), all instructed to output \texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED} when a query omits a pivotal condition required by the retrieved clause.
  \item \textbf{Three Matched Forced-Binary Readers (\texttt{L5}--\texttt{L6}, \texttt{L9})}: \texttt{L5} (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} $+$ \texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash}), \texttt{L6} (\texttt{bm25\_char2} $+$ \texttt{qwen-plus}), and \texttt{L9} (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh} $+$ \texttt{qwen3.8-max}), pairing the \textbf{exact same model and retriever} as \texttt{L7}, \texttt{L8}, and \texttt{L1} respectively, but instructed to commit to a binary eligibility decision (\texttt{EXCLUDED} vs.\ \texttt{ELIGIBLE}) without abstaining.
\end{itemize}
Every pipeline executes strictly isolated \texttt{predict\_real\_llm\_rag(question, retrieved\_clause\_text)} calls without access to reference seed metadata. In our single-arm subset evaluation against raw-clause panel ground truth, the panel true label for each generated query is computed via a permutation-invariant 3-way majority vote (\texttt{\_get\_panel\_true\_label}) over $\{\texttt{ELIGIBLE}, \texttt{EXCLUDED}, \texttt{CLARIFY}\}$ across the three flagship judges without consulting \texttt{seed\_gold} (returning \texttt{CLARIFY} on any 1--1--1 three-way split and raising a \texttt{KeyError} if any adjudication record is missing).

To evaluate external generalization without sharing any reference probes, we also construct a completely disjoint \textbf{48-query External Holdout Benchmark} from the $24$ post-hardening extension seeds ($12$ unseen insurance PDFs + $12$ multi-threshold seeds: $24$ complete determinate queries + $24$ missing-pivotal clarify queries, retrieving over the expanded 26-policy insurance index and 4-clause multi-threshold index). Table~\ref{tab:real_llm_rag} establishes four findings on real LLM RAG evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Controlled Same-Model Protocol Ablation}: Holding both the LLM reader and retriever strictly fixed isolates the effect of abstention capability from model capacity:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \texttt{qwen3.8-max + BGE} (\texttt{L1} abstaining vs.\ \texttt{L9} forced-binary): Reference-80 accuracy is $0.8750$ vs.\ $0.5000$ ($\Delta = +0.3750$); External Holdout-48 accuracy is $0.8542$ vs.\ $0.5000$ ($\Delta = +0.3542$); on the $40$ complete queries alone, \texttt{L1} achieves $0.7750$ vs.\ $1.0000$ for \texttt{L9} because \texttt{L1} conservatively abstains on $9/40$ complete queries when retrieved clauses contain complex sub-clauses.
    \item \texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash + BGE} (\texttt{L7} abstaining vs.\ \texttt{L5} forced-binary): Reference-80 accuracy is $0.9375$ vs.\ $0.5000$ ($\Delta = +0.4375$); External Holdout-48 accuracy is $0.9375$ vs.\ $0.5000$ ($\Delta = +0.4375$); on the $40$ complete queries, \texttt{L7} achieves $0.8750$ vs.\ $1.0000$ for \texttt{L5}.
    \item \texttt{qwen-plus + BM25} (\texttt{L8} abstaining vs.\ \texttt{L6} forced-binary): Reference-80 accuracy is $0.8750$ vs.\ $0.4875$ ($\Delta = +0.3875$); External Holdout-48 accuracy is $0.8333$ vs.\ $0.4792$ ($\Delta = +0.3541$); on the $40$ complete queries, \texttt{L8} achieves $0.7750$ vs.\ $0.9750$ for \texttt{L6}.
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single-Arm Ranking Reversal Holds Both Across All 9 Pipelines and Within the 6 Same-Protocol Abstaining Pipelines}: Under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, $85.0\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}) and $77.5\%$ (\texttt{DeepSeek}) of conversational rewrites degrade into \texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED} under the 3-flagship panel majority. Naive seed-label inheritance produces an evaluation MAE of $0.6556$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}) and $0.5250$ (\texttt{DeepSeek}) across all $9$ pipelines ($\tau = -0.8792$ and $-0.6973$; selection regret $0.7250$ and $0.6250$). Crucially, even when restricted \textbf{solely to the 6 same-protocol abstaining pipelines} (\texttt{L1--L4, L7--L8}, eliminating forced-binary \texttt{L5, L6, L9}), \texttt{ordinary\_style} still inverts system rankings ($\tau = -0.7857$, MAE $= 0.5917$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}; $\tau = -0.3571$, MAE $= 0.4208$ on \texttt{DeepSeek}) because more capable abstaining readers accurately detect missing conditions and output \texttt{CLARIFY\_REQUIRED}, getting penalized more heavily under corrupted binary labels. Explicit-constraint methods (\texttt{condition\_prompt}, \texttt{structured\_json\_untruncated\_320}, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}, \texttt{multi\_template\_3}, and \texttt{complete\_6slot\_schema\_render}) all reduce panel label corruption to $2.5\%\text{--}10.0\%$ and restore positive rank concordance ($\tau \in [+0.7692, +1.0000]$ across the 6 abstaining pipelines).
  \item \textbf{Zero-Call Symbolic Reachability Detection \& Dynamic Label Re-Certification Eliminates Ranking Reversal Without Re-Generation (\texttt{ordinary\_style + 0-Call Relabel} and \texttt{mixed\_ord\_prompt\_80 + Relabel})}: To isolate the standalone value of our symbolic reachability verifier when a practitioner audits an \emph{already-generated} unverified benchmark without control over the generation prompt and with \textbf{zero additional LLM calls}, we evaluate \texttt{zero\_call\_verifier\_dynamic\_relabel}: format-valid queries verified as \texttt{PRESERVED\_DECISION} keep their seed label, format-valid queries explicitly verified as missing pivotal conditions (\texttt{outcome == "DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY"} with \texttt{is\_parse\_uncertain=False}) are dynamically re-certified to \texttt{CLARIFY}, and any mutated (\texttt{CONDITION\_MUTATED}), parse-uncertain (\texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}), or format-invalid queries are rejected (\texttt{ver\_relabel = None}). On \texttt{ordinary\_style} alone, rejecting the $2$ mutated queries on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($38/40$ accepted) and re-certifying degraded queries slashes single-arm evaluation MAE vs.\ the 3-flagship panel truth from \textbf{$0.6556 \to 0.0936$} (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}, an $85.7\%$ error reduction; Abst-6 MAE $0.5917 \to 0.0614$) and from \textbf{$0.5250 \to 0.1222$} (\texttt{DeepSeek}, $40/40$ accepted: $1$ preserved binary + $39$ \texttt{CLARIFY}, a $76.7\%$ error reduction; Abst-6 MAE $0.4208 \to 0.0833$, outperforming the trivial constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} counter-baseline at MAE $0.1306$, $\tau = +0.7188$), flipping Kendall's $\tau$ vs.\ panel truth from \textbf{$-0.8792 \to +0.9062$} (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}; Abst-6 $\tau$: $-0.7857 \to +0.7857$) and from \textbf{$-0.6973 \to +0.7812$} (\texttt{DeepSeek}; Abst-6 $\tau$: $-0.3571 \to +0.5000$). Crucially, on a realistic \textbf{80-query mixed uncurated pool} (\texttt{mixed\_ord\_prompt\_80}, combining $40$ \texttt{ordinary\_style} + $40$ \texttt{condition\_prompt} queries) where a trivial constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} counter-baseline fails (\texttt{Qwen-Plus} Constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} MAE $0.4222 / 0.3583$; \texttt{DeepSeek} Constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} MAE $0.4333 / 0.3646$; and on \texttt{condition\_prompt} alone Constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} collapses to MAE $0.8333$ and $0.7583$, $\tau = -0.9032$ and $-0.5852$), dynamic symbolic re-certification retains determinate binary labels while relabeling ambiguous ones: on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($75/80$ accepted: $37$ binary + $38$ \texttt{CLARIFY}, rejecting $4$ mutated and $1$ parse-uncertain), it cuts MAE from $0.3292 \to \mathbf{0.0504}$ (an \textbf{$84.7\%$} reduction vs.\ Naive and \textbf{$88.1\%$} vs.\ Constant-\texttt{CLARIFY}) and flips $\tau$ from $-0.7945 \to \mathbf{+0.8125}$; on \texttt{DeepSeek} ($79/80$ accepted: $34$ binary + $45$ \texttt{CLARIFY}, rejecting $1$ parse-uncertain), it cuts MAE from $0.2986 \to \mathbf{0.0816}$ (a \textbf{$72.7\%$} reduction vs.\ Naive and \textbf{$81.2\%$} vs.\ Constant-\texttt{CLARIFY}) and flips $\tau$ from $-0.8956 \to \mathbf{+0.7062}$ (while also improving \texttt{condition\_prompt} alone on \texttt{DeepSeek} from MAE $0.0722 \to 0.0570$ and $\tau$ from $+0.6176 \to +0.8009$).
  \item \textbf{Paired-80 Internal Coupling vs.\ Disjoint External Holdout-48 Validation}: Because the paired 80-query score (\texttt{0.5 * generated\_complete + 0.5 * reference\_ambiguous}) reuses the $40$ reference ambiguous probes, at full scenario coverage ($40/40$; note that \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} retains $39/40 = 97.5\%$ coverage and \texttt{verify\_only\_filter} retains $37/40$ and $33/40$) its deviation from Reference-80 is mathematically half of the complete-query deviation. Therefore, we report the \textbf{disjoint 48-query External Holdout Benchmark} as our external system-selection target: on External Holdout-48, verified methods reduce evaluation MAE from $0.1945$ / $0.1449$ (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) down to $0.0264\text{--}0.0583$ and raise Kendall's $\tau$ across all 9 pipelines from $+0.6176$ / $+0.5000$ (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) to $+0.7701\text{--}+0.9412$ ($+0.5189\text{--}+0.9286$ among the 6 abstaining pipelines).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion, Practical Recommendations, and Limitations}
\paragraph{Where Does the Gain Come From? Four-Stage Marginal Gain and Component Role Decomposition.}
A central question in empirical benchmark construction is whether quality gains arise from explicit prompting, sufficient output token budgets, symbolic detection, or multi-pass repair (\texttt{component\_gain\_and\_role\_decomposition} in \texttt{data\_v3/ccf\_a\_upgrade\_summary.json}). Our ablations across all $176$ pairs ($88$ seeds) isolate four distinct, complementary roles:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Stage~1 --- Explicit Slot-Preserving Prompting (\texttt{ordinary\_style} $\to$ \texttt{condition\_prompt})}: Accounts for the primary leap in semantic preservation on frontier cloud LLMs, raising 3-flagship panel usability by \textbf{$+64.20\text{ pp}$} ($48/176 = 27.27\% \to 161/176 = 91.48\%$) and symbolic usability by \textbf{$+86.93\text{ pp}$} ($2/176 = 1.14\% \to 155/176 = 88.07\%$).
  \item \textbf{Stage~2 --- Output Token Budget \& Schema Completeness (\texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} [160 Cap] $\to$ \texttt{structured\_json\_untruncated\_320}; 5-Slot $\to$ 6-Slot Schema)}: Raising the output cap from $160$ to $320$ tokens eliminates mid-sentence truncation, recovering \textbf{$+24.43\text{ pp}$} panel usability ($118/176 = 67.05\% \to 161/176 = 91.48\%$) and \textbf{$+23.86\text{ pp}$} symbolic usability ($117/176 = 66.48\% \to 159/176 = 90.34\%$). Adding the missing 6th secondary-threshold slot (\texttt{complete\_6slot\_schema\_render}) recovers $+7.50\text{ pp}$ panel usability ($66/80 \to 72/80$) and $+10.00\text{ pp}$ symbolic usability ($70/80 \to 78/80$).
  \item \textbf{Stage~3 --- Zero-Call Symbolic Reachability Detection \& Dynamic Label Re-Certification (\texttt{zero\_call\_verifier\_dynamic\_relabel})}: Even when a practitioner inherits an \emph{already-generated} unverified conversational benchmark (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) or a mixed uncurated pool (\texttt{mixed\_ord\_prompt\_80}) without prompt control and with \textbf{zero LLM calls}, using the symbolic reachability verifier to reject mutated/parse-uncertain queries and dynamically re-certify \texttt{DEGRADED\_TO\_CLARIFY} queries to \texttt{CLARIFY} reduces single-arm RAG evaluation MAE on \texttt{ordinary\_style} from \textbf{$0.6556 \to 0.0936$} (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}, $38/40$ accepted; Abst-6 MAE $0.5917 \to 0.0614$) and from \textbf{$0.5250 \to 0.1222$} (\texttt{DeepSeek}, $40/40$ accepted; Abst-6 MAE $0.4208 \to 0.0833$), flipping Kendall's $\tau$ vs.\ panel truth from \textbf{$-0.8792 \to +0.9062$} and \textbf{$-0.6973 \to +0.7812$}. On the 80-query mixed pool where a constant-\texttt{CLARIFY} counter-baseline fails (MAE $0.4222$ and $0.4333$), dynamic re-certification cuts MAE from \textbf{$0.3292 \to 0.0504$} (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}, $75/80$ accepted, $\tau = -0.7945 \to +0.8125$) and from \textbf{$0.2986 \to 0.0816$} (\texttt{DeepSeek}, $79/80$ accepted, $\tau = -0.8956 \to +0.7062$).
  \item \textbf{Stage~4 --- Targeted Diagnostic Second-Pass Repair (\texttt{condition\_prompt} / \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} $\to$ \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair})}: When the goal is not merely relabeling degraded queries to \texttt{CLARIFY} or discarding them (\texttt{verify\_only\_filter}, which loses $7.5\%\text{--}17.5\%$ determinate scenario coverage), feeding exact missing/mutated slot diagnostics into a targeted repair pass lifts strict symbolic usability by \textbf{$+10.80\text{ pp}$} over \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($155/176 \to 174/176 = 98.86\%$, $p_{\text{Holm,doc}} = 0.0117$) and \textbf{$+7.95\text{ pp}$} over equal-budget \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} ($160/176 \to 174/176$, $p_{\text{Holm,doc}} = 0.0156$), while delivering its largest semantic gains on smaller or noisier generators (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B}: $66.7\% \to 95.8\%$; \texttt{Qwen-Turbo}: $79.2\% \to 100.0\%$; \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B} multi-threshold: $43.8\% \to 75.0\%$ standalone / $93.8\%$ granted-clause).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Practical Recommendations for Rule-Governed RAG Evaluation.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Do not rely on unconstrained conversational rewrites or retrieval Top-1 to validate RAG test sets}: Across $26$ official insurance contracts and four rule families, $75\%\text{--}100\%$ Top-1 retrieval coexists with $54\%\text{--}100\%$ rates of decision ambiguity or mutation under conversational rewriting, causing severe evaluation error ($\text{MAE} = 0.53\text{--}0.66$ across 9 pipelines; $0.42\text{--}0.59$ across 6 same-protocol abstaining pipelines) and rank inversion ($\tau \in [-0.88, -0.70]$ overall; $[-0.79, -0.36]$ within same-protocol abstaining pipelines).
  \item \textbf{Use explicit slot constraints and pair complete queries with clarify probes when benchmarking RAG readers}: Under independent 3-flagship panel adjudication, explicit condition prompting (\texttt{condition\_prompt}), untruncated 320-token JSON (\texttt{structured\_json\_untruncated\_320}), and parser-guided repair (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) all achieve high semantic usability ($91.5\%\text{--}92.6\%$ across $176$ pairs), while \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} additionally guarantees $98.86\%$ ($174/176$) deterministic parser extractability and format validity. Pairing verified complete queries with slot-dropped clarify probes—and validating on an independent holdout set of complete and ambiguous cases—reliably separates abstention-capable readers from forced-binary guessers.
  \item \textbf{Audit symbolic verifiers against zero-hint pure-verbatim flagship panels to expose cross-clause dependencies}: Comparing symbolic verification against zero-hint pure-verbatim LLM adjudication ($\kappa = 0.8366$ on 3-way labels) pinpoints both regex false rejects ($7.19\%$) and cross-clause context dependencies (such as maternal vs.\ newborn waiting periods in \texttt{cpic\_106702} or inpatient deductible pre-conditions in \texttt{pa\_2650}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, while we audit our symbolic verifier against both protocol-guided and strictly zero-hint pure-verbatim three-flagship panels (\texttt{Kimi-K3}, \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Pro}, and \texttt{Qwen3.8-Max}, $N=1{,}408$ core records plus $640$ pure-verbatim confirmation records) and verify $94.1\%\text{--}100.0\%$ zero-shot waiting-period schema field extraction across $17$ PDFs, this adjudication is performed by frontier language models rather than licensed human claims adjusters; multi-annotator human legal/actuarial review remains essential future work. Second, under the 3-flagship LLM panel across all $176$ pairs, \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair} ($161/176 = 91.48\%$) significantly outperforms \texttt{ordinary\_style} ($48/176$), 160-token \texttt{structured\_json\_rewrite} ($118/176$), and 5-slot \texttt{structured\_json\_schema\_render} ($130/176$), but does not differ significantly from \texttt{condition\_prompt} ($161/176$), \texttt{structured\_json\_untruncated\_320} ($161/176$), or budget-matched \texttt{generic\_self\_refine} ($163/176$, $p \ge 0.50$). Thus, the advantage of closed-loop repair over single-pass explicit constraint prompting is specific to guaranteeing strict symbolic schema compliance ($174/176$ vs.\ $155\text{--}160/176$, $p_{\text{Holm,doc}} \le 0.0156$) and on smaller/noisier generators (Sections~\ref{sec:multithreshold}--\ref{sec:frozen_confirmation}), rather than producing higher LLM-judge semantic validity than well-engineered explicit prompts on frontier cloud generators. Third, our pure-verbatim panel audit highlights cross-section contract dependencies ($36/640$ confirmation records and $8/48$ non-waiting records) where multi-section contracts require joint reasoning over waiting periods, deductibles, and benefit caps. Fourth, after deduplicating shared queries ($N=245$) and clustering by the $24$ insurance seeds, the positive correlation between lexical novelty and dense-over-lexical retrieval discrimination ($r = +0.1182$, within-seed permutation $p = 0.2240$) is not statistically significant at the seed-cluster level. Finally, our regex slot extractors route unsupported calendar-date and month/week expressions to \texttt{PARSE\_UNCERTAIN}, and the community QA corpus in Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval} carries noisy community labels, unverified redistribution licensing, and potential pretraining exposure.
