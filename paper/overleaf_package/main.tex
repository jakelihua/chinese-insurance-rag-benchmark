\begin{abstract}
When cold-starting a domain-specific retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system before user query logs exist, practitioners often synthesize queries from knowledge-base passages to rank candidate retrievers. This paper examines two practical questions in Chinese insurance retrieval: (1)~when does ranking retrievers on synthetic queries reverse the winner observed on real user questions, and (2)~to what extent do format-valid synthetic rewrites drop or alter underwriting and claims eligibility conditions across model scales?

We study these questions in three rounds. In \textbf{Round~1}, we deduplicate $3{,}746$ Chinese insurance answers and partition QA pairs into $3{,}725$ connected components via Union-Find so that no shared answer spans calibration ($750$ queries) and test ($2{,}994$ queries) splits. Evaluating six lexical retrievers, we show that a source-prefix diagnostic proxy selects character $3\text{--}5$-gram TF-IDF ($\text{MRR@10}=0.9330$) over character-bigram BM25 ($0.9311$), whereas real test queries favor bigram BM25 ($0.3817$ vs.\ $0.2727$, selection regret $0.1090$). In \textbf{Round~2}, we add dense (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) and hybrid (\texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}) retrievers and generate zero-shot and 3-shot synthetic queries from held-out answers using both a local 1.5B model (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, $N=249$ common valid sources) and a cloud production model (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}, $N=179$). On real test queries, pure dense retrieval ranks first ($\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$ for hybrid RRF; paired difference $+0.0187$, 95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.0080, +0.0300]$; full-document sliding-window MaxP sensitivity check with zero truncated answers confirms $+0.0123$). On synthetic queries from both models and both prompting modes, passage-level lexical reuse inflates BM25 scores and reverses the top-1 choice in favor of hybrid RRF (regret $0.0187$). Marginal query-feature importance weighting fails ($0\%$ optimal selection), whereas our proposed \textbf{Relational Source-Overlap Gating} (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}, filtering synthetic queries by query-to-source bigram containment $\kappa_2(q', d^*)$) restores \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in $100\%$ ($5/5$) of trials across all budgets $B \in \{0, 25, 50, 100, 200\}$, both LLM scales, and both prompting modes ($0.0000$ regret), and paired-bootstrap \textbf{Candidate-Set Abstention} on small real calibration sets avoids confident wrong commitments at $B=25$ ($0\%$ confident wrong rate, $80\%$ abstention, $100\%$ optimal coverage, mean set size $2.60$ of $8$). In \textbf{Round~3}, we build $80$ controlled clause-grounded cases with double-witness counterexamples from five official insurance policy wordings and evaluate $120$ paired rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}. All $120/120$ rewrites pass syntactic format checks, yet unconstrained conversational rewriting (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) preserves standalone rule decisions in only $30\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $0\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $20\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) of seeds, whereas explicit slot constraints (\texttt{condition\_prompt}) raise preservation to $95\%\text{--}100\%$. Finally, a $64$-case cross-domain extension to \textbf{healthcare clinical eligibility} and \textbf{statutory labor-law compliance} ($48$ local generations) shows that \texttt{ordinary\_style} drops or mutates pivotal conditions in $93.8\%$ ($15/16$) of seeds, while pairing \texttt{condition\_prompt} with a 1-step closed-loop \textbf{slot-guard repair} (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) lifts decision preservation from $6.2\%$ to $68.8\%$ and eliminates $100\%$ of numeric threshold mutations ($31.2\% \rightarrow 0.0\%$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Selecting a retriever for a vertical-domain RAG system---for instance, choosing among character $n$-gram BM25, dense bi-encoders, or Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)---often happens before real user traffic is available. A common workflow in both retrieval research \cite{dai2023promptagator,jeronymo2023inparsv2} and automated RAG evaluation frameworks \cite{saadfalcon2024ares,es2024ragas} is to generate synthetic questions from corpus passages using an instruction-tuned LLM, score candidate retrievers on the synthetic set, and deploy the top-ranked system.

While prior work has studied macro-level rank correlations between synthetic and real test collections \cite{rahmani2024syndl,boyd2025reckoning}, two specific failure modes matter when making concrete deployment decisions in insurance retrieval:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Dense vs.\ Hybrid Selection Reversal}: Passage-conditioned query generation tends to reuse domain terms from the source passage. When synthetic queries have higher lexical overlap with target documents than colloquial user queries do, BM25 scores are artificially elevated, which in turn boosts hybrid RRF over pure dense retrieval even when dense retrieval performs better on real user questions.
  \item \textbf{Loss of Underwriting and Claims Conditions}: In insurance clauses, coverage outcomes (\texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED} vs.\ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) depend on specific variables: product identity, first-time vs.\ renewal enrollment, policy effective day, sickness vs.\ accidental injury cause, plan-specific waiting period (e.g., 7 vs.\ 25 days), and insurer waiver approval. A rewrite that satisfies standard format filters (single line, question mark, valid length) can still drop or alter one of these variables, making the question undecidable or flipping its coverage outcome.
\end{enumerate}

This paper reports a three-round empirical and methodological study on Chinese insurance retrieval, pairing diagnostic failure analysis with concrete, falsifiable mitigations. To avoid terminology ambiguity, we explicitly distinguish two levels of abstention studied in this work: \textbf{retriever-selection candidate-set abstention} (Round~2, where an evaluation pipeline returns a confidence set of candidate retrievers rather than committing to a single noisy winner under small calibration budgets) and \textbf{query-level clarification abstention (\texttt{CLARIFY})} (Round~3, where a question missing a pivotal underwriting slot cannot be decided without user clarification, proven via two counterexample completions). Our scope is intentionally bounded: we evaluate retrieval selection and clause condition conservation rather than end-to-end online claims adjudication, and we separate deterministic slot checks from human verification. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Round~1 (Group-Disjoint Lexical Baseline)}: On $3{,}746$ deduplicated answers from a public Chinese insurance QA corpus partitioned into $3{,}725$ connected groups ($750$ calibration, $2{,}994$ held-out test queries), we compare six lexical retrievers against extractive keyword and prefix-copy proxies, quantifying how long-$n$-gram copy bias selects \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} over the true lexical winner \texttt{bm25\_char2} (regret $0.1090$ MRR@10).
  \item \textbf{Round~2 (Cross-Scale Selection Reversal, Source-Overlap Gating, and Candidate-Set Abstention)}: Expanding to eight systems (six lexical, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}, and \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}), we evaluate zero-shot and 3-shot queries generated by local \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} ($N=249$ common valid sources) and cloud \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($N=179$). Both models select \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} under both prompting strategies, reversing the real-test winner \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} ($\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ vs.\ $0.5030$; confirmed by a zero-truncation sliding-window \texttt{Full-MaxP} ablation at $0.5125$ vs.\ $0.5002$, $\Delta = +0.0123$). We prove why marginal query-feature density weighting fails ($0\%$ optimal selection), introduce \textbf{relational source-overlap gating} (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}) to restore $100\%$ optimal selection ($0.0000$ regret) across all budgets $B \in \{0, 25, 50, 100, 200\}$ and both LLM scales, and show that paired-bootstrap \textbf{candidate-set abstention} reduces confident wrong retriever commitments on small real calibration sets at $B=25$ from $60\%\text{--}100\%$ to $0\%$.
  \item \textbf{Round~3 (Double-Witness Counterexamples, Cross-Domain Validation, and Closed-Loop Slot Repair)}: Using five official Chinese insurance policy clauses, we formalize a waiting-period rule compiler and construct $20$ seed scenarios ($80$ controlled variants where every \texttt{missing\_critical} \texttt{CLARIFY} case carries two opposite-outcome completion witnesses) alongside $120$ paired LLM rewrites across \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}. We separate scale-dependent numeric mutations ($15\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} vs.\ $0\%$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}/\texttt{Qwen-Max}) from scale-invariant pragmatic slot omission under ordinary conversational prompting, and show that explicit slot constraints (\texttt{condition\_prompt}) raise standalone decision preservation from $0\%\text{--}30\%$ to $95\%\text{--}100\%$. Extending the same compiler and double-witness protocol to $16$ seed scenarios ($64$ controlled cases, $48$ generations) in \textbf{healthcare clinical eligibility} and \textbf{statutory labor-law compliance}, we further demonstrate that pairing \texttt{condition\_prompt} with a 1-step deterministic \textbf{slot-guard repair} loop (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) raises standalone decision preservation from $6.2\%$ (\texttt{ordinary\_style}) and $37.5\%$ (\texttt{condition\_prompt}) to $68.8\%$ ($11/16$) and eliminates $100\%$ of threshold-overwriting mutations ($31.2\% \rightarrow 0.0\%$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Synthetic Queries in IR Evaluation.}
Promptagator \cite{dai2023promptagator} and InPars-v2 \cite{jeronymo2023inparsv2} use LLM-generated queries to train retrievers when in-domain labels are scarce. Evaluation frameworks such as ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} and RAGAs \cite{es2024ragas} also use synthetic queries to score RAG pipelines. Recent analyses by Rahmani et al.\ \cite{rahmani2024syndl} and Boyd et al.\ \cite{boyd2025reckoning} note that LLM-generated evaluation sets carry generator-specific biases. We focus on a concrete mechanism---lexical overlap inflation shifting the winner from pure dense retrieval to hybrid BM25+dense fusion---and test it across both 1.5B and commercial cloud generators.

\paragraph{Calibration and Abstention under Small Budgets.}
Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) \cite{angelopoulos2023prediction} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares} combine synthetic predictions with small gold samples to construct confidence intervals on paired data. In cold-start retriever selection, however, synthetic queries are generated from documents without paired real user questions on the same test split. We evaluate unpaired shrinkage and surface-density weighting as negative baselines, and formulate a bootstrap candidate-set abstention rule that withholds point commitment when calibration uncertainty spans multiple systems.

\paragraph{Insurance QA Benchmarks.}
InsuranceQA \cite{feng2020insuranceqa} and its Chinese adaptations provide community question-answering pairs for answer selection. Because community answers contain duplicates and lack formal underwriting parameters, we augment our Round~1--2 FAQ experiments with a Round~3 benchmark built directly from five official Chinese insurance policy wordings with deterministic waiting-period rules.

\section{Round 1: Group-Disjoint Split and Lexical Proxy Diagnostics}

\subsection{Leakage-Free Group Split}
Public Chinese insurance FAQ corpora (\texttt{baoxianzhidao\_filter.csv}, derived from insuranceQA-zh) contain repeated questions and shared boilerplate answers. Splitting QA pairs at random places identical answer texts in both calibration and test sets.

After normalizing whitespace and deduplicating exact answer strings, we obtain $3{,}746$ unique answers and $3{,}744$ valid QA pairs. We build an undirected graph linking any pairs that share normalized questions, normalized answers, or near-duplicate question prefixes, yielding $3{,}725$ connected components via Union-Find. Assigning whole components to splits (seed \texttt{20261025}) produces:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Real Calibration Pool}: $750$ queries across $745$ components (used for few-shot demonstrations and small-budget calibration sampling).
  \item \textbf{Real Test Set}: $2{,}994$ queries across $2{,}980$ components (zero component overlap with calibration).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Lexical Systems and Deterministic Proxies}
In Round~1, we index all $3{,}746$ answers under six character $n$-gram lexical retrievers: sublinear TF-IDF cosine similarity over character unigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1}), bigrams (\texttt{tfidf\_char2}), $1\text{--}3$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char1\_3}), and $3\text{--}5$-grams (\texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5}), alongside Okapi BM25 ($k_1=1.2, b=0.75$) over character unigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char1}) and bigrams (\texttt{bm25\_char2}). Each system is scored by $\text{MRR@10}$ and $\text{Hit@5}$ (recall at rank~5).

We compare held-out real test queries against two deterministic non-LLM proxies constructed from the target answers: (1)~\texttt{synthetic\_keywords}, which concatenates top corpus-level TF-IDF terms from the answer into a question template, and (2)~\texttt{source\_prefix\_diagnostic}, a stress-test proxy that turns the opening clause of the answer into a question.

\subsection{Round 1 Results}
Table~\ref{tab:round1} and Figure~\ref{fig:round1} show that on held-out real queries, \texttt{bm25\_char2} ranks first ($\text{MRR@10}=0.3817, \text{Hit@5}=0.4826$), whereas \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} ranks fourth ($\text{MRR@10}=0.2727, \text{Hit@5}=0.3557$) because real user questions rarely share contiguous $3\text{--}5$-character spans with answer texts. On \texttt{source\_prefix\_diagnostic}, contiguous substring copying pushes \texttt{tfidf\_char3\_5} to first place ($0.9330$ vs.\ $0.9311$ for \texttt{bm25\_char2}), causing a true-test selection regret of $0.3817 - 0.2727 = 0.1090$.

In budget sensitivity trials ($5$ random draws per budget $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$ from the calibration pool), selecting the winner directly on real calibration samples (\texttt{real\_only}) picks \texttt{bm25\_char2} in $80\%$ ($4/5$) of draws at $B=25$ (mean regret $0.0126$, Kendall's $\tau_b = 0.4133$) and $100\%$ ($5/5$) of draws at $B \ge 50$ (zero regret, with $\tau_b$ increasing from $0.6267$ at $B=50$ to $0.8933$ at $B=100$ and $0.9200$ at $B=200$).

\section{Round 2: LLM Synthetic Queries, Selection Reversal, and Abstention}

\subsection{Setup and Shared Valid Source Sets}
To test whether real LLM query generators induce selection errors when dense and hybrid retrievers are included, we expand the candidate pool to eight systems by adding:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}: Dense retrieval with \texttt{BAAI/bge-small-zh-v1.5} (512-dim normalized embeddings; 512-token limit, which truncates $509$ of $3{,}746$ long answers, while $2$ of $256$ source answers exceed $512$ tokens during LLM prompt construction).
  \item \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge}: Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k=60$, depth $100$) combining \texttt{bm25\_char2} (requiring positive lexical score) and \texttt{bge\_small\_zh}.
\end{itemize}

We sample $256$ target answer passages and generate synthetic questions under two prompting modes (\texttt{zero\_shot} and \texttt{few\_shot\_3}, where the 3 style demonstrations are fixed real questions drawn from the calibration pool; test questions are never exposed). We run two generators:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (local MPS, \texttt{float32/eager}, greedy decoding, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=64): out of $256$ sources, $255$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $250$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) pass format validation (single Chinese question ending in a question mark, length $6\text{--}160$ chars, no PII), leaving \textbf{$N=249$ common valid sources} for paired evaluation.\footnote{An initial \texttt{float16+sdpa} run on MPS produced numerical corruption and $0$ valid outputs; we logged that failed run in \texttt{failed\_float16\_run.json} and switched to \texttt{float32+eager}.}
  \item \texttt{Qwen-Plus} (cloud API, \texttt{temperature}=0, \texttt{max\_tokens}=64): $243/256$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $184/256$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) pass the same single-question filter, yielding \textbf{$N=179$ common valid sources}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Does Retriever Selection Reverse Across Scales?}
Table~\ref{tab:round2} and Figure~\ref{fig:round2} show a consistent top-1 reversal between real and synthetic evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Real Test Ordering and Full-Document MaxP Sensitivity Check}: On the $2{,}994$ held-out real questions, pure dense retrieval (\texttt{bge\_small\_zh}) achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5216$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6453$), beating \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.5030$, $\text{Hit@5}=0.6075$) by a paired difference of $+0.0187$ (1,000-resample paired percentile bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+0.0080, +0.0300]$). To verify that this dense-over-hybrid ranking is not an artifact of 512-token truncation on the $509$ long documents, we run a full-document sliding-window ablation (\texttt{Full-MaxP} in \texttt{mitigation\_and\_maxp\_diagnostics.json}), splitting all $509$ long answers into $512$-token windows with a $256$-token stride ($1{,}205$ additional overflow chunks, $0$ truncated answers) and scoring each document by maximum passage cosine similarity $\max_{c \in d} \cos(\mathbf{e}_q, \mathbf{e}_c)$. Under \texttt{Full-MaxP}, \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} achieves $\text{MRR@10}=0.5125$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6306$) vs.\ $0.5002$ ($\text{Hit@5}=0.6062$) for \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($\Delta = +0.0123$), confirming that pure dense retrieval remains the true test winner with zero document truncation.
  \item \textbf{Lexical Copy Inflation in \texttt{zero\_shot}}: On real test queries (mean length $25.25$ chars), the fraction of query character bigrams and trigrams appearing in the target answer is $0.3243$ and $0.1808$, respectively. Under \texttt{zero\_shot} generation, bigram and trigram containment jump to $0.4744$ and $0.3442$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B} ($13.94$ chars), and to $0.4640$ and $0.2850$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($22.83$ chars). Because the LLM reuses terminology directly from the passage it is conditioned on, \texttt{bm25\_char2} jumps from $0.3817$ (real) to $0.5013$ (\texttt{1.5B}) and $0.6528$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}). Fusing this inflated BM25 ranking with BGE via RRF pushes \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} above \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} by $+0.0445$ on \texttt{1.5B} ($0.5647$ vs.\ $0.5202$) and $+0.0366$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($0.7371$ vs.\ $0.7005$).
  \item \textbf{Few-Shot Tradeoff}: Providing 3 calibration examples (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) reduces n-gram copying: bigram containment drops to $0.3180$ on \texttt{1.5B} and $0.4108$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}. However, on \texttt{1.5B}, mimicking the surface style of 3 unrelated examples introduces semantic drift: in a 20-source sanity audit ($40$ questions in \texttt{semantic\_audit.json}), source-supported questions drop from $12/20$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) to $9/20$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}), with $6/20$ becoming unsupported by the passage (e.g., changing equal traffic liability to full counterparty liability in \texttt{doc\_id=545}, or turning medical fee advance conditions into post-hospitalization purchase rules in \texttt{doc\_id=2213}). On \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, \texttt{few\_shot\_3} avoids severe drift ($\text{MRR@10}=0.6794$) and narrows the RRF-vs-BGE gap from $-0.0366$ to $-0.0019$, but \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} ($0.6813$) still ranks first under point selection.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Marginal Reweighting Fails vs.\ Relational Source-Overlap Gating and Candidate-Set Abstention}
Can reweighting synthetic queries by surface similarity to real calibration queries fix this selection bias? In \texttt{validate\_v2.py}, a 2-fold cross-fit logistic domain classifier trained on character $1\text{--}3$-gram TF-IDF features separates real calibration queries from synthetic queries with $\text{ROC-AUC}=0.8120$ (\texttt{zero\_shot}) and $0.7733$ (\texttt{few\_shot\_3}) at $B=25$ (rising to $0.8765$ and $0.9148$ at $B=200$). Yet across $5$ repeated draws at each budget $B \in \{25, 50, 100, 200\}$, propensity density weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_density\_weighted}) selects the true winner \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in \textbf{$0\%$ of trials} (regret $0.0187$), and length weighting (\texttt{synthetic\_length\_weighted}) reaches $0\%$ on \texttt{zero\_shot} ($0\%\text{--}20\%$ on \texttt{few\_shot\_3}).

Why does marginal query-feature reweighting fail? Because selection reversal is governed by the \emph{relational} conditional distribution $P(q \mid d^*)$---specifically, the character bigram containment $\kappa_2(q, d^*) = |\text{Bi}(q) \cap \text{Bi}(d^*)| / |\text{Bi}(q)|$ between a query and its target document $d^*$---rather than marginal query text $P(q)$. Stratifying \texttt{real\_test} ($N=2{,}994$) by $\kappa_2(q, d^*)$ reveals a structural regime switch:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Low-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 < 0.25$, $40.0\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=1{,}199$)}: \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} substantially outperforms \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} on \texttt{real\_test} ($0.3022$ vs.\ $0.2220$, $\Delta = +0.0801$) \emph{and on all four LLM synthetic sets}: \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} ($+0.0839$, $n=29$), \texttt{1.5B few\_shot\_3} ($+0.0326$, $n=115$), \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot} ($+0.0821$, $n=13$), and \texttt{Qwen-Plus few\_shot\_3} ($+0.1600$, $n=14$).
  \item \textbf{High-Overlap Stratum ($\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$, $20.6\%$ of \texttt{real\_test}, $n=617$)}: \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} outperforms \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} on \texttt{real\_test} ($\Delta = -0.0457$) and on all four synthetic sets ($\Delta \in [-0.0811, -0.0447]$). Unweighted synthetic evaluation reverses the global winner because passage-grounded generation shifts query mass into $\kappa_2 \ge 0.50$ ($48.6\%$ on \texttt{1.5B zero\_shot} and $41.9\%$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus zero\_shot}, vs.\ $20.6\%$ on \texttt{real\_test}).
\end{itemize}

This diagnostic motivates two complementary mitigations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Relational Source-Overlap Gating (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated})}: Instead of weighting marginal query features, we filter synthetic queries to retain only those whose source bigram containment does not exceed the calibrated real-query median $\hat{\tau}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)} = \text{median}_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{cal}}^{(B)}} \kappa_2(q, d^*)$ ($\approx 0.292$), or in the zero-calibration cold-start regime ($B=0$), the synthetic bottom quintile $Q_{0.20}(\kappa_2)$. Across \textbf{all budgets $B \in \{0, 25, 50, 100, 200\}$, both LLM scales (\texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and both prompting modes (\texttt{zero\_shot} and \texttt{few\_shot\_3})}, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} selects the true winner \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} in \textbf{$100\%$ ($5/5$) of trials} ($0.0000$ regret).\footnote{By contrast, \texttt{source\_overlap\_gated} fails on deterministic \texttt{keywords\_matched} ($100\%$ wrong selection) because extractive keyword concatenation has zero support in the low-overlap stratum ($\min \kappa_2 > 0.25$).}
  \item \textbf{Retriever-Selection Candidate-Set Abstention}: When evaluating directly on a small real calibration sample (\texttt{real\_only}) of size $B$ with empirical leader $\hat{s}$, we run $200$ paired bootstrap resamples over the $B$ queries and retain all systems $s$ whose 95\% upper bound on $(\text{MRR}_s - \text{MRR}_{\hat{s}})$ is $\ge 0$. When $|\mathcal{C}_B| > 1$, the selector abstains from point commitment and returns $\mathcal{C}_B$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key Findings on Condition Drift}
Table~\ref{tab:round3} and Figure~\ref{fig:round3} highlight four empirical observations:

\paragraph{1. Format Validation Does Not Catch Condition Loss.}
All $120/120$ generated questions pass \texttt{format\_valid}, yet under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, $70\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $100\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $80\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) fail to preserve standalone rule decidability.

\paragraph{2. Scale-Dependent Value Mutation vs.\ Scale-Invariant Omission.}
Comparing the three model tiers separates two failure types:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Value Mutation and Label Collision on \texttt{1.5B}}: In $3/20$ seeds (\texttt{condition-013}, \texttt{015}, \texttt{016} on \texttt{cpic\_82859}), \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{ordinary\_style} mutates ``保单生效第20天'' (Day~20) into ``保单生效后的第二天'' (Day~2). Furthermore, on \texttt{condition-009} (7-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_NOT\_EXCLUDED}) and \texttt{condition-010} (25-day plan wait, Day~10 $\rightarrow$ \texttt{WAIT\_EXCLUDED}), \texttt{1.5B} drops both the product name and the plan waiting period, generating the exact same text for two opposite ground-truth labels:
  \begin{quote}
  \small
  ``我是在网上看病花了钱，是第一次买这个保险，保单生效第10天了，因为不是意外病，想问一下这属于等待期吗？''
  \end{quote}
  \item \textbf{Conversational Omission on \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max}}: Scaling up to \texttt{Qwen-Plus} and \texttt{Qwen-Max} reduces numeric value mutations to $0\%$ ($0/20$). However, under \texttt{ordinary\_style}, larger models aggressively shorten administrative details to sound natural: \texttt{Qwen-Plus} drops the product title in $20/20$ ($100\%$) of seeds, while \texttt{Qwen-Max} drops product titles in $10/20$ ($50\%$) and rewrites ``首次投保'' (first-time enrollment) into ``我刚买了…'' (``I just bought...'', which is ambiguous between first enrollment and renewal) in $6/20$ ($30\%$) of seeds. Even when the true \texttt{policy\_id} is granted externally, \texttt{ordinary\_style} preserves decisions in only $50\%$ (\texttt{1.5B}), $45\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Plus}), and $50\%$ (\texttt{Qwen-Max}) of seeds.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Slot-Constrained Prompting Across Scales.}
Under \texttt{condition\_prompt}, standalone decision preservation (\texttt{v2\_synonym\_aware}) reaches $95\%$ ($19/20$) on \texttt{1.5B} and \texttt{Qwen-Plus} ($100\%$ on \texttt{1.5B} when \texttt{policy\_id} is granted) and $100\%$ ($20/20$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}. Top-1 clause retrieval over the 5-policy corpus with \texttt{bm25\_char2} likewise improves from $50\% \rightarrow 100\%$ on \texttt{1.5B}, $80\% \rightarrow 100\%$ on \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and $90\% \rightarrow 95\%$ ($\text{MRR@10}=0.9750$) on \texttt{Qwen-Max}.

\paragraph{4. Strict Regex False Negatives and Blinded Review Pack.}
On \texttt{1.5B} \texttt{condition\_prompt}, \texttt{v1\_strict\_regex} flags $10/20$ questions even though $9$ of those $10$ retain all conditions under synonymous phrasing (e.g., ``生效是第20天'', ``同意免去等待期''). Because automated regex guards remain approximations of human reading comprehension, we package all $80$ controlled cases and $40$ local generations into randomized, blinded two-rater HTML forms (\texttt{reviewer\_A.html} and \texttt{reviewer\_B.html}, scored by \texttt{score\_annotations.py}); human annotation results are not claimed in this manuscript prior to independent completion.

\section{Cross-Domain Generalization: Healthcare and Statutory Legal Benchmarks}
To test whether condition drop, numeric threshold mutation, and our verification framework generalize beyond commercial insurance, we construct an out-of-domain benchmark in \texttt{cross\_domain\_study.py} (\texttt{data\_v3/cross\_domain\_controlled\_cases.jsonl} and \texttt{cross\_domain\_summary.json}) covering four clauses across two rule-governed verticals:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Healthcare Clinical Eligibility (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (1)~\texttt{med\_egfr\_tki} (\emph{NRDL Osimertinib Outpatient Special-Drug Access Protocol}: requires EGFR T790M mutation positivity and a 14-day washout after prior TKI failure for second-line conversion, unless first-line or specialist emergency waiver applies); and (2)~\texttt{med\_vte\_prophylaxis} (\emph{Orthopedic Post-Op LMWH VTE Prophylaxis Pathway}: 10-day standard window for knee replacement vs.\ 35-day window for hip replacement; contraindicated upon active bleeding without a hematology continuation waiver).
  \item \textbf{Statutory Labor-Law Compliance (2 clauses, 8 seeds, 32 controlled cases)}: (3)~\texttt{law\_work\_injury} (\emph{Work Injury Insurance Regulations Art.~14 Commuting Traffic Accident Rule}: requires non-principal traffic fault and filing within 30 days by the employer or 365 days by the employee/kin, unless an HRSS written extension is granted); and (4)~\texttt{law\_noncompete} (\emph{Labor Contract Law Art.~24 Non-Compete Termination Guideline}: binds senior/confidential personnel; unilateral termination requires $>90$ consecutive days of employer non-payment without a signed grace-period waiver).
\end{itemize}

Every seed scenario is compiled into four controlled variants (\texttt{complete}, \texttt{surface}, \texttt{missing\_critical} certified as \texttt{CLARIFY} via two opposite-outcome completion witnesses, and \texttt{counterfactual} flipping \texttt{RULE\_ELIGIBLE} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{RULE\_EXCLUDED}). We then run \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (\texttt{float32/eager}, greedy decoding) across all $16$ seeds under three generation protocols ($48$ total generations): (1)~\texttt{ordinary\_style}, (2)~open-loop \texttt{condition\_prompt}, and (3)~\textbf{1-step closed-loop \texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}}, which runs the synonym-aware slot parser (\texttt{extract\_cross\_slots\_v2}) on the \texttt{condition\_prompt} output and, if any required slot is missing or mutated, issues a single targeted repair prompt naming the deficient slots.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Practical Recommendations and Limitations}
\paragraph{Practical Recommendations.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Gate synthetic queries by relational source overlap ($\kappa_2(q', d^*)$) rather than marginal surface density}: Filtering synthetic queries to match real-query bigram containment (\texttt{source\_overlap\_gated}) eliminates dense-vs-hybrid selection reversal across budgets $B \in \{0, 25, 50, 100, 200\}$ and LLM scales ($0\%$ wrong selection).
  \item \textbf{Use small group-disjoint real calibration sets with candidate-set abstention}: When evaluating directly on $25\text{--}50$ real queries, paired-bootstrap candidate-set abstention avoids premature point commitment while retaining the true winner in $\mathcal{C}_B$.
  \item \textbf{Pair slot-enumerated prompts with closed-loop deterministic slot repair}: Combine \texttt{condition\_prompt} with synonym-aware slot verification (\texttt{slot\_guard\_repair}) and double-witness counterexamples to catch both pragmatic slot omissions and threshold-overwriting mutations across insurance, healthcare, and legal domains.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
First, our Round~1--2 FAQ corpus comes from a single community insurance dataset whose historical answers provide weak relevance labels rather than expert-verified legal advice; while our \texttt{Full-MaxP} sliding-window ablation ($1{,}205$ overflow chunks, $0$ truncated answers) confirms that \texttt{bge\_small\_zh} still outperforms \texttt{rrf\_bm25\_bge} on \texttt{real\_test} ($+0.0123$ MRR@10), evaluating native long-context dense encoders remains useful future work. Second, our experiments evaluate models within the Qwen family (\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, \texttt{Qwen-Plus}, and \texttt{Qwen-Max}); testing additional LLM families is needed to assess broader generator diversity. Third, our clause-grounded benchmarks cover five insurance clauses ($80$ cases, $120$ rewrites) and four healthcare/legal clauses ($64$ cases, $48$ rewrites), and independent two-rater human annotation of the blinded HTML review packs remains pending.
